Fill-Distance-Guided Active Learning for Causal Function Recovery under Network Interference

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (206 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.24143v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Causal inference seeks to learn how outcomes change under alternative interventions, but network interactions complicate this task. Under network interference, outcomes depend on both own treatment and neighbors' treatments, making causal effects functions of neighborhood exposure rather than single treatment contrasts. Existing methods primarily target prespecified causal contrasts or their estimation precision, leaving global response-function recovery largely unexplored. We study sequential experimentation under a limited experimental budget, with the goal of recovering the full exposure-dependent population-average response function. To this end, we propose FANCI (Fill-Distance-Guided Active Learning for Network-Interference Causal Inference). For function learning, treatment-specific Gaussian processes flexibly model nonlinear conditional response surfaces. For experimental design, we formulate global response-function recovery as a coverage problem and propose a fill-distance criterion that guides upcoming experiments toward poorly covered regions. To account for network constraints on attainable exposure patterns, we develop full-network and target-screened strategies for selecting feasible network--assignment pairs. Theoretically, we derive uniform error bounds linking fill distance to response-function estimation and characterize the approximation loss from target screening. Simulations and an online-game experiment show that FANCI improves recovery of nonlinear exposure-dependent effects and reveals treatment patterns obscured when network interference is ignored.
