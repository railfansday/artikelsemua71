AcousticBEV: Acoustic BEV Object Localization for Intelligent Vehicles

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (226 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.19381v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Modern autonomous driving and driver assistance systems rely predominantly on cameras, radars and LiDARs for environmental perception. However, these modalities face different environmental limitations, including poor illumination for cameras and adverse weather for LiDAR. Acoustic sensing, particularly using microphone arrays, could provide complementary perception capabilities for autonomous driving, but has remained largely underexplored. In this work, we design a horizontally arranged microphone array that functions as the "ears" of a vehicle, enabling localization of nearby moving vehicles in a bird's-eye view (BEV) representation that captures both their relative direction and distance. Using this array, we collect a real-world driving dataset, AcousticScenes, which contains 2,117 static and 2,863 dynamic samples with synchronized spatial acoustic signals, LiDAR point clouds and 3D bounding box annotations. Building on this dataset, we propose AcousticBEV, a learning-based method for acoustic BEV object localization, which estimates the center locations of moving traffic participants in BEV from the microphone-array signals, conditioned on the ego vehicle's own speed. Experimental results demonstrate that AcousticBEV outperforms both classical signal-processing baselines and other learning-based methods, achieving F1 scores of $47.7 \pm 1.2$% on "Easy" scenarios and $45.1 \pm 1.4$% on "Difficult" cases. Our findings demonstrate that acoustic signals from a low-cost, vehicle-mounted microphone array can localize nearby moving vehicles in both azimuth and distance.
