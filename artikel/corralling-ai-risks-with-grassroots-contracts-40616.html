Corralling AI Risks with Grassroots Contracts

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (293 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.06374v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: AI's inflection point is a fountain of risks for digital platforms, as those in control of global platforms cede control of the algorithms, membership and content to AI: (i) AI control of global platforms used by the majority of humanity; (ii) unaccountable AI agents operating anonymously, possibly masquerading as humans, serving unknown masters with unknown goals; and (iii) unattributed deepfakes generated by anonymous AI, which people cannot distinguish from genuine content. Here, we propose to corral these AI risks with grassroots contracts, a digital incarnation of a two-centuries old notion of Pierre-Joseph Proudhon: a social contract that can be freely adopted by any group of people, where groups bound by the same contract can union by mutual consent, possibly into a single global union. Grassroots contracts are realised by grassroots platforms, person-to-person platforms owned, operated and governed by their participants. We developed, with AI, a methodology to compile grassroots contracts to working smartphone apps. We present grassroots contracts for a social graph with accountable AI agents and for a social network with digital provenance, and working smartphone apps that implement the two, and show how they corral these risks: (i) grassroots platforms are not controlled by a third party who may cede control to AI; (ii) AI agents are introduced with accountability: similarly to cars, their manufacturers are accountable for manufacturing defects and their operators for reckless operation; and (iii) every item carries the signatures of its author and of every agent that forwarded it, so creators and forwarders of harmful content are accountable and culpable. Much work remains for grassroots apps to be competitive with incumbent global platforms; but perhaps now this could be achieved with some AI-months rather than with incalculably-many person-years.
