Infrared fluctuations and collective entanglement modes in harmonic lattices

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (235 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21830v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate how infrared fluctuations and spatial separation shape vacuum entanglement between two finite regions in the relativistic harmonic chain and its Lifshitz generalizations. At fixed lattice spacing and finite geometry, we establish that logarithmic negativity remains uniformly bounded as the mass approaches zero, while mutual information diverges. Infrared-enhanced total correlations therefore do not provide an unbounded distillable-entanglement resource. Using local symplectic consolidation, we identify the collective canonical modes carrying the negativity and follow their evolution with separation. Their participation crosses over from localization near the facing boundaries to a collective distribution within each region. Expansion in the adjacent-state canonical basis connects this evolution to progressively weaker, more extended reference modes, including adjacent-halo directions. Building on the established concentration of long-distance logarithmic negativity into a single canonical pair, we show that its spatial participation is smooth and infrared-dominated despite its ultraviolet canonical structure, a distinction also present in pure-state entanglement modes. These results refine the ultraviolet mode picture by resolving the long-wavelength organization of the oscillators participating in the surviving entanglement. Across the massless dispersions studied, the normalized participation approaches a universal long-distance profile when separation is scaled by the negativity radius. Its detailed form and the entanglement range nevertheless remain sensitive to ultraviolet deformations. Together, these findings distinguish infrared-enhanced correlations from distillable entanglement and reveal the collective spatial organization underlying its persistence at large separation.
