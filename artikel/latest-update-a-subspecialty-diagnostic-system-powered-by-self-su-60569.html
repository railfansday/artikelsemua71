Latest Update: A Subspecialty Diagnostic System Powered by Self-Supervised Learning for Cervical Histopathology

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (212 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2510.10196v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Cervical cancer remains a major malignancy in women, with large-scale human papillomavirus-based screening generating a large volume of biopsies and, for advanced cases, surgical specimens, imposing an overwhelming diagnostic burden that necessitates precise histopathological evaluation. Although deep learning shows promise, general foundation models offer a broader reach but remain limited in capturing subspecialty-specific features and task adaptability. We introduce the Cervical Subspecialty Pathology (CerS-Path) diagnostic system, built upon the subspecialized dataset and developed through two synergistic pretraining stages: self-supervised learning on approximately 190 million tissue patches to build a cervical-specific feature extractor, and multimodal enhancement with 2.5 million image-text pairs, followed by diagnostic functions integration and downstream task enhancement. CerS-Path integrates eight functions -- across morphological diagnosis, predictive analytics, and multimodal tasks -- to cover the diagnostic workflow from biopsies to resection specimens. Comprehensive evaluations demonstrate improved performance in cervical pathology in female patients, where CerS-Path outperforms state-of-the-art foundation models across 26 of 30 diagnostic tasks. Furthermore, in multicenter retrospective and prospective validation across six independent cohorts of women, CerS-Path achieves up to 99% sensitivity for clinically relevant lesion detection with acceptable specificity across sites, highlighting its potential for screening-derived histopathology and suitability for integration into high-volume diagnostic pathways.
