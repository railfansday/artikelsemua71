Suno has introduced image and video generation in its latest effort to expand beyond being an AI music platform. The upcoming feature, called “Visuals,” allows people to turn Suno songs into AI-generated video clips that automatically lipsync to lyrics, or make images via prompt descriptions that can be used as thumbnails and album artwork.

In a recent development, visuals is available on web, iOS, and Android. The exact layout differs across Suno’s desktop and mobile interfaces, but owners will find a new Visuals tab under Create mode on each version. Web users can also access Visuals by selecting the three dot menu on a song in their library. From there, users can select which section of the track to use, up to a maximum of 15 seconds, and select a reference image to build the generated video clip.

Sources indicate that there are three options available for the starting image reference: selecting one of the original character presets provided by Suno, uploading your own photo or artwork, or generating the image directly on Suno using descriptive prompts. The image generation capabilities also allow users to select specific aspect ratios, optimizing results for landscape or portrait modes and online platforms.

For video generation, users can select whether the reference image is used as the start or end frame, making it easier to edit the 15-second clips together. There’s a toggle to automatically lipsync vocals to the generated video, and tools for adjusting aspect ratio and clip length. Visuals also includes a dedicated video editing timeline for assembling clips into longer videos, and adding customizable song lyric text.

Industry observers note that the Visuals launch follows Suno introducing a Speech feature in October for generating spoken words and voiceovers. Adding video capabilities is another attempt to diversify the platform after Suno’s music generator attracted a number of high-profile lawsuits, though it seems some consumers would rather the tech giant focus on improving those song-generating capabilities. In the YouTube video for the Visuals launch, one user notes “FIX AUDIO BEFORE ADDING CRAP NOBODY ASKED FOR” — a sentiment that other commenters are echoing.
