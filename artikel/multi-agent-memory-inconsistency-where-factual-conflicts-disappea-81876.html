Multi-Agent Memory Inconsistency: Where Factual Conflicts Disappear in LLM Agents

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (151 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22245v2 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Multi-agent delegation lets LLM systems distribute evidence collection across workers with separate contexts. This creates a consistency challenge: facts retrieved by workers may be missing from the evidence available to the lead for a joint conclusion. We introduce a benchmark and evidence-flow profiler for studying multi-agent memory inconsistency through controlled factual conflicts in Harvey LAB document-review tasks. Pooled across three models with comparable generation spending, requiring delegation increases retrieval of both conflicting claims from 50.2% to 68.7% relative to single-agent execution, yet conflict detection falls from 16.2% to 13.2%. Within this setting, workers report both claims for only 35.2% of retrieved pairs, and pooled matched comparisons show higher detection than single-agent execution when one worker reads both claims but lower detection when the claims are split across workers. Worker-to-lead handoffs are a central target for improving conflict detection in multi-agent LLMs.
