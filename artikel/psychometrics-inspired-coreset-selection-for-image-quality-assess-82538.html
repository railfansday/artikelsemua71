Psychometrics-Inspired Coreset Selection for Image Quality Assessment

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (214 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.24233v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Collecting large-scale subjective annotations remains a critical bottleneck in training accurate image quality assessment (IQA) models. Coreset selection reduces this burden by selecting representative images before annotation. However, diversity in a visual feature space does not necessarily reflect diversity in perceptual quality, raising a fundamental question about which quality-related factors the representation should encode. We propose GRAM, a quality-aware representation learning framework inspired by the graded response model (GRM) from psychometrics. GRM describes how an individual's latent ability determines the probabilities of reaching successive assessment grades, with response sensitivity and grade thresholds governing this relationship. In this paper, we find that this principle naturally aligns with perceptual quality assessment, where the impact of image degradation depends on image content and distortion characteristics. Building on this connection, GRAM models quality judgments through three complementary factors: latent quality, content-dependent response sensitivity, and distortion-dependent grade thresholds. These factors jointly determine the probabilities of attaining different quality grades and the resulting expected quality score. The learned parameters provide a structured quality-aware representation that complements general visual features for coreset selection. Experiments on nine datasets demonstrate improvements across different selection strategies, downstream IQA models, and annotation budgets. The code will be made publicly available.
