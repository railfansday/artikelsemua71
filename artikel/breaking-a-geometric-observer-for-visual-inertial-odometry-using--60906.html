Breaking: A Geometric Observer for Visual-Inertial Odometry using Optical-Flow Constraints

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (159 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20098v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: This paper presents a geometric observer for monocular visual-inertial odometry driven by individual optical-flow constraints. For each tracked point, the bearing and its rotationally compensated derivative give a depth-free homogeneous constraint on the body-frame velocity. These constraints enter the innovation of an observer on the gravity-augmented group $\mathbb{SE}_3(3)$ through a Riccati gain, without estimating depths or reconstructing the direction of motion. The auxiliary gravity state makes the six-dimensional body-frame velocity-gravity error dynamics independent of the attitude correction, and their uniform observability is shown to be equivalent to persistent excitation of a matrix formed from the inertial bearings and their derivatives. Under this condition, the body-frame velocity and gravity errors converge globally and uniformly exponentially, and a gravity-based attitude correction renders the combined velocity, gravity, and reduced-attitude error almost globally asymptotically stable and locally uniformly exponentially stable. Simulations illustrate the excitation condition, recovery from initial errors, and sensitivity to perturbed measurements.
