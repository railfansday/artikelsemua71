Coordinating integration of multidisciplinary design analysis and optimisation codes with design structure matrices

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (264 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.22522v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The integrated analysis and optimisation codes used to design nuclear fusion reactors need to become increasingly large scale, multidisciplinary and complex to computationally verify design requirements more thoroughly and earlier in the design process. Though the manner in which the integrated models are coupled can have significant implications for computational efficiency, the structure of couplings typically remains hidden in the implementation of a design framework. As this problem is not unique to fusion, we frame integrated design problems as applications of the broader field of Multidisciplinary Design Analysis and Optimisation (MDAO). Originally developed for complex system design problems in e.g. aerospace, the MDAO field uses (extended) Design Structure Matrices (DSMs) to display the architecture of integrated analysis and optimisation codes. The aim of this work is to introduce these DSM methods and demonstrate how they can be used to improve the transparency and efficiency of design codes in fusion. As a proof of concept, DSM methods are applied to two case studies: stellarator optimisation with SIMSOPT and the PROCESS systems code. With these cases, we illustrate how a DSM can help identify structural assumptions in the code, and how architectural insight can be used to improve computational efficiency. In particular, we demonstrate the computational impact by modifying the PROCESS solver architecture based on the feedback loops revealed in an automatically generated DSM, yielding a significant reduction in model evaluations. Such architectural changes are considered especially impactful for future design frameworks that combine higher fidelity models with large numbers of inexpensive models.
