Model Discovery for Virtual Chaos Engineering with Incomplete Tracing: An eBPF Study

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (249 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.23313v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Model-based fault experiments can assess the consequences of component failures before intervening in a running application. Recent work on model discovery and graph simulation acquired operation models from application telemetry and used them for availability prediction. Applying this approach to operational legacy systems raises an acquisition problem: the required traces may be absent or incomplete, while introducing them can require changes across heterogeneous components. These systems continue to serve business operations during extended maintenance and modernization. External observation offers a possible route to model-based fault analysis before a tracing retrofit, but collecting telemetry does not by itself establish that an executable operation model can be obtained. We investigate this route using extended Berkeley Packet Filter (eBPF) observations and the existing graph-based modelling approach. A 108-case study varied application software development kit (SDK) tracing across absent, partial and full conditions in three public applications. Discovered dependencies were combined with declared operation requirements, deployment facts and scenario assumptions; model forecasts preceded independent execution checks. With SDK tracing disabled, the tested OpenTelemetry eBPF Instrumentation path supported all seven operations in two applications across three repetitions. Forecasts agreed exactly in 17 of 21 operation-repetition groups; the maximum state-weighted discrepancy was 4.22 percentage points. Combining observed context links supported nine additional groups under partial tracing. The results establish an acquisition path for virtual fault experiments under the tested observation constraints and identify limits imposed by protocols, evidence association and operation semantics.
