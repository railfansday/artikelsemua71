Efficient logical operations in quantum LDPC codes using simple resource states

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (221 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2607.16166v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum LDPC codes provide a substantial reduction in qubit overhead required for fault-tolerant quantum computation compared to surface code, thanks to their high encoding rate. However, operating simultaneously on multiple logical qubits encoded in the same block is more challenging and may slow down logical operations. Prior work addresses this problem by designing complex resource states to perform logical measurements in LDPC codes. Here, we propose an approach that only consumes cat states. Whereas previous work on cat-based measurements focuses on a single logical measurement, we design a protocol for the joint measurement of $\ell$ commuting logical operators. The key ingredient is the design of a scheduler code determining the measurement sequence and allowing for the decoding of all logical measurement outcomes. Numerical simulations with the LDPC codes Q70 and Q102 of the walking cat architecture show a reduction of nearly $3\times$ in the number of cat-based measurements required versus the Viterbi method for measuring $\ell=20$ commuting logical operators. Combining our logical measurement protocols with a new variant of the CliNR partial error correction scheme, we achieve a reduction of up to $73\times$ versus the Viterbi method for random Clifford circuits. Our approach also applies to non-Clifford gates, producing a reduction of up to $5\times$ versus the Viterbi method for Toffoli gates.
