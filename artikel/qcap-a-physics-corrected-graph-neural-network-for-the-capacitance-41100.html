QCap: a physics-corrected graph neural network for the capacitance matrix of any planar superconducting device layout

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (263 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.22057v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Electromagnetic simulations of device layouts to extract capacitance are computationally intensive, a major obstacle to accurate design of circuit devices. Machine learning models can bypass these simulations, but previous approaches have been restricted to layouts using only particular components, and require thousands of simulations of those same components as training data. We present QCap, a model that accurately predicts the Maxwell capacitance matrix of an arbitrary superconducting device layout in seconds, compared to minutes to hours of Ansys Q3D simulations. We develop a novel design-embedding algorithm that converts the layout into a universal graph format, allowing the model to work with any layout created with any tool. To achieve high accuracy, we obtain a coarse physics prior from a fast method of moments simulation of capacitance, then train the model to correct this prior. On 4,009 test designs withheld from training data the model outputs agree with Q3D simulations to a median error of 0.177%, and on never-seen component families it stays below 1% error. We demonstrate the model's scaling by obtaining the capacitance matrix of a 9-superconducting-qubit device in 12 seconds on a laptop (compared to 19.9 h and 228 GB of memory in Ansys) with sub-percent error, and capacitance of a (too large to simulate) 100-qubit, 781-conductor device in 3.4 min on a laptop. We provide the open-source models and data on SQuADDS and Hugging Face. Our results provide a powerful tool for large-scale accurate circuit design and guide the development of models to bypass other physical simulations.
