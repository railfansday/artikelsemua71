Wireless Edge-Assisted Adaptive Latent Reasoning for Vision-Language-Action Models in Physical AI

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (212 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.19131v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable embodied artificial intelligence (AI) by translating visual observations and language instructions into robotic actions. Emerging use cases in physical AI are expected to drive the need for efficient inference and communication, as the computational demands of the VLA models pose challenges for real-time control with limited onboard resources. Test-time scaling through latent reasoning allows these models to refine hidden representations without generating explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) tokens, but introduces a trade-off between task success, inference latency, and energy consumption. In this paper, we address the efficient collaborative inference under a realistic communication scenario. We propose a wireless edge-assisted framework for adaptive latent reasoning (WEALR) that augments onboard computation with edge computing. A decision-making agent jointly selects the execution location and latent-reasoning convergence threshold. We formulate this joint adaptation as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP). A Lagrangian-constrained deep reinforcement learning (CDRL) approach, namely trust region policy optimization Lagrangian (TRPO-Lag), learns to balance task performance and resource consumption under varying wireless conditions and task requirements. We evaluate the framework using LIBERO manipulation tasks, profiled VLA inference costs, and ray-traced wireless channels. Experimental results demonstrate that TRPO-Lag achieves up to 23% higher feasibility-weighted task success rate compared to benchmark CDRL methods.
