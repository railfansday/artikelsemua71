Industry News: Centroid Spectral Coupling: Implicit Alignment and Representation Diversity in Self-Supervised Learning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (210 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.22158v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) aims to align augmented views while preserving representation diversity and preventing collapse. Existing non-contrastive methods often address these goals using multiple weighted loss terms. We introduce Centroid Spectral Coupling (CSC), a single-term SSL objective that implicitly couples view alignment, representation diversity, and collapse resistance. CSC constructs centroids from independently normalized augmented-view pairs and applies a ridge-based spectral objective to their uncentered second-moment matrix. Preserving centroid magnitudes encodes view agreement while encouraging balanced representation energy across spectral directions. We establish an exact decomposition into nonnegative alignment and spectral-imbalance components, characterize the geometry of global optima, and derive finite-loss guarantees for alignment and diversity. We also show that complete constant-output collapse is a strict saddle in normalized representation space. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Imagenette show that CSC achieves the highest linear Top-1 accuracy among the evaluated non-contrastive methods on all three benchmarks, and the highest kNN Top-1 accuracy on both CIFAR datasets. A ridge-parameter sensitivity study reveals an empirical alignment--diversity trade-off: increasing $\kappa$ strengthens view alignment but reduces representation effective rank. These results show that a unified spectral objective yields competitive visual representations without explicit contrastive negatives or separately weighted alignment and diversity penalties.
