Quantum Circuits for Quantum Spatial Search on $d$-Dimensional Hypercubic Lattices

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (243 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2607.19151v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We propose an explicit quantum-circuit construction for quantum spatial search based on discrete-time quantum walks on $d$-dimensional hypercubic lattices and systematically evaluate its circuit-resource scaling. Although the algorithmic performance of quantum spatial search has been extensively studied, a systematic evaluation of its circuit-resource scaling remains limited. We implement the flip-flop shift operator using coin-controlled modular increment and decrement operations for position updates and a single $X$ gate for direction reversal. We compare four implementations of the position update based on the quantum Fourier transform (QFT), ripple-carry addition, multi-controlled $X$ (MCX) gates, and automatically synthesized arithmetic. The results show that the QFT-based implementation reduces circuit width and depth, while the ripple-carry implementation reduces the CX-gate count at the cost of additional ancilla qubits. We also extend the construction to non-periodic boundary conditions by introducing self-loops at lattice boundaries. We confirm that numerical simulations of the circuits reproduce the theoretical search dynamics for $d=2,3,4$, and $5$ under periodic boundary conditions and for $d=2$ under non-periodic boundary conditions. For $N=L^d$ lattice sites, the circuit width scales as $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$. We derive asymptotic upper bounds on the total circuit depth of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N}\log^{5/2} N)$ for $d=2$ and $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N}\log^2 N)$ for $d\geq3$, consistent with the numerical results. Furthermore, CX-gate depolarizing noise simulations show that reducing the CX-gate count improves noise robustness. These results provide guidance for efficient quantum-circuit design for spatial search on hypercubic lattices.
