Cybersecurity is a fast-growing field, with a constant need for skilled professionals. But unlike other professions — like medicine or aviation — there’s no clear-cut pathway to qualifying for cybersecurity positions. For employers and job seekers alike, this can make the journey to building a team (or entering a successful cybersecurity career) feel uncertain.

Industry observers note that enter the registered apprenticeship program — a proven method for developing skilled talent in cybersecurity that benefits both the employer and the latest professional. Let’s commit to supporting this crucial talent development approach as we celebrate National Apprenticeship Week from November 17-23, 2024.

What Is a Cybersecurity Apprenticeship?

Recent findings suggest that an apprenticeship is a paid, on-the-job training program where individuals learn essential skills while working alongside experienced professionals. Unlike traditional education paths, apprenticeships combine the best of both worlds: earn-while-you-learn, with real-world experience in the field. The apprentice is guided by a mentor and can expect to gain expertise over one to two years, often culminating in a full-time permanent job offer.

Much like a medical resident or an aviation trainee, a cybersecurity apprentice benefits from structured, hands-on learning, with mentorship ensuring they develop the technical and workplace skills and gain the confidence needed to succeed. Successful apprenticeship program completers also earn one or more portable, nationally-recognized and industry-valued credentials or certifications. “The apprenticeship program has allowed me entry into a field/position that most can't break into without a 4-year degree,” remarked Brittany Patterson-Morris, an apprentice with Interapt, an IT services apprenticeship program.

Why Cybersecurity Apprenticeships Matter.

Recent findings suggest that in 2023, nearly 61,000 individuals participated in a registered cybersecurity apprenticeship program in the U.S., a 254% increase in just five years, according to the Department of Labor (DOL)’s Office of Apprenticeship. This growth reflects a fundamental shift in how we prepare the next generation of cybersecurity professionals. As Carla Miller from Albireo Energy puts it, "It’s what we need to expand the workforce.”[1].

For employers, apprenticeships can be an affordable way to build a workforce from the ground up, ensuring that new hires are trained with the specific skills they need. Chris Dunn, founder of Covered 6, shares, “Our cybersecurity apprenticeship programs have helped us attract passionate candidates while lowering the time and cost barriers of traditional training.”[2] Employers can also use apprenticeships to upskill current employees, providing a career pathway to retain proven workers. For job seekers, apprenticeships offer a direct pathway to a career, complete with certifications and mentorship. “The key is blending intensive on-the-job practice with high-quality, up-to-date asynchronous learning," claims Ximena Gates, CEO of BuildWithin, an apprenticeship management software company built by former apprentices. “This is ideal for career changers and those without technical backgrounds as skills can transfer from other disciplines.”.

According to recent reports, A way that employers can fast track a newly introduced program is to partner with an intermediary. As the DOL National Industry Intermediary for Cybersecurity Apprenticeship Expansion, Safal Partners helps employers more quickly, easily, and effectively navigate Registered Apprenticeships. “Employers of any size can join our already-approved National Registered Apprenticeship program, access no-cost apprenticeship expertise to build or expand their own program, utilize industry-built online coursework for their apprentices, and get ongoing program support to fund, modify, and sustain their program,” said Katie Adams, chief delivery officer at Safal Partners.
