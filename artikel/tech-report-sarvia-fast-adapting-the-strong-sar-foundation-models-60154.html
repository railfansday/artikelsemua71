Tech Report: SARVIA: Fast-Adapting the Strong SAR Foundation Models That Are Already in Your MLLMs

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (228 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22273v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) foundation models provide transferable representations for diverse perception tasks in all-weather Earth observation. However, their development often relies on costly large-scale SAR pretraining, raising the question of whether such extensive domain-specific pretraining is necessary. We find that modern multimodal large language models (MLLMs) already provide visual encoders that can outperform dedicated SAR foundation models as downstream initializations. This motivates a new paradigm: reuse general visual capabilities and devote domain training to brief specialization. We therefore introduce SARVIA, an adaptive post-pretraining method that specializes modern MLLMs using self-supervised visual questions with computable answers. These questions are constructed from unlabeled observations through general image tasks that capture spatial relations and SAR-specific tasks that exploit native measurements and SAR--optical correspondence. A shared answer-prediction objective jointly trains low-rank adapters across visual and language components, with loss-driven task sampling. We then export the adapted visual encoder as a standalone backbone. With 600 adaptation steps and 2 GPU-hours, SARVIA outperforms the compared foundation models on all six SAR detection and segmentation benchmarks. Compared with dedicated SAR pretraining, SARVIA reduces domain-training costs by orders of magnitude while improving visual transfer across architectures and scales. This new paradigm shifts the emphasis from repeated, costly pretraining to a reusable specialization process that can benefit from advances in open MLLMs. Dataset and code: https://github.com/zcablii/SARVIA.
