Cooperative Transport of Unknown Objects by Distributed Effort Regulation in Multi-agent Systems

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (224 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.19731v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: A distributed multi-robot object transportation strategy is proposed for objects of unknown shape, size, geometry, mass and mass distribution without any prior knowledge about the object or number of robots involved in the transportation process. Robots follow the boundary of the object and dynamically determine viable pushing points. A novel object-model-free heading-error-driven pushing effort regulation method is proposed to promote goal-directed object motion; robots regulate their pushing effort using individual and collective heading errors. Robots dynamically relocate around the object boundary if a pushing point is lost or the required pushing effort exceeds its capability to correct its heading. The proposed method is validated using simple and complex shapes with convex and non-convex boundary regions and dynamically changing weight distribution of objects. Detailed analysis on the effects of robot distribution and initial object pose on the transportation performance in terms of required time and path efficiency is also presented. Scalability analysis revealed that object transportation time is dependent on the number of pushing surfaces available which in turn depends on the object size, shape, and set inter-robot safety distance. The proposed system yielded highly efficient object paths with consistent elapsed transport times over several trials robust to external disturbances such as non-uniform friction between contacting surfaces and robot wheel slip.
