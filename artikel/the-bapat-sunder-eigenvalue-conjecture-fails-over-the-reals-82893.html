The Bapat-Sunder eigenvalue conjecture fails over the reals

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (152 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.23269v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In 1986 Bapat and Sunder asked whether $\operatorname{per}(A)$ is the largest eigenvalue of $F_A=[a_{i,j}\operatorname{per}(A(i,j))]$ for every positive semidefinite Hermitian $A$, where $A(i,j)$ deletes row $i$ and column $j$. Drury disproved this over the complex field in 2018 and conjectured that the property holds for real positive semidefinite matrices. We disprove Drury's conjecture with a rank-$4$, $32\times32$ integer Gram matrix and an integer vector whose Rayleigh quotient exceeds $\operatorname{per}(A)$; verification uses integer arithmetic. Together, the two counterexamples resolve the Bapat-Sunder eigenvalue conjecture negatively over both fields. Counterexamples exist of every order $n\ge32$. As an application to quantum optics, the violation yields a $36$-mode orthogonal interferometer and a direction of relative arrival-time delays among $32$ photons with identical-variance Gaussian wave packets along which every sufficiently small nonzero delay increases the probability that all photons are detected within four designated output modes.
