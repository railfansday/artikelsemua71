Intuition Vectors from Pretrained Representations Steer Diffusion-Based Reasoning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (237 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2609.32619v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Abstract visual reasoning requires identifying relationships between few examples and applying them to novel inputs. Recent advances have been driven by large language and vision-language models or specialized domain-specific architectures. It remains unclear whether abstract reasoning can be supported directly by the relational geometry of pretrained visual representations, without relying on language-based inference or task-specific representation learning. We investigate this possibility using frozen self-supervised vision encoders. With latent vector arithmetic, we confirmed that the representational geometry of two self-supervised encoders support abstract concept discrimination on Bongard problems and encode systematic relationships between input-output pairs in ARC tasks. Simple vector differences between image embeddings, which we call intuition vectors, capture transformations that generalize across instances of the same task. These vectors distinguish among 794 abstract reasoning tasks with up to 87.2\% accuracy, preserve relational structure across task-invariant visual transformations, and guide output retrieval for unseen inputs. Given this, we tested the hypothesis that this relational structure can steer generative reasoning by conditioning a diffusion transformer operating in pretrained visual latent space. On held-out ARC tasks, intuition-vector conditioning outperforms conditioning on complete demonstrations while requiring nearly six times less test-time compute, and supports zero-shot inference. Taken together, our findings suggest that pretrained visual representations provide a transferable substrate for abstract reasoning, enabling a modular approach in which the relational structure inferred from demonstrations steer generative reasoning.
