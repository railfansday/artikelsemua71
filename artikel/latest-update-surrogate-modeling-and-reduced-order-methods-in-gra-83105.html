Latest Update: Surrogate Modeling and Reduced-Order Methods in Gravitational-Wave Physics and Numerical Relativity: From Spectral Representations to Adaptive Compression

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (183 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.24727v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The rapid expansion of gravitational-wave astronomy and high-precision numerical relativity has created an acute computational bottleneck: direct numerical integration of the Einstein field equations and high-dimensional Bayesian parameter estimation require millions of waveform evaluations that cannot be sustained by traditional solvers alone. Reduced-order modeling (ROM) and surrogate techniques have emerged as foundational tools to bridge this gap, compressing high-fidelity solution manifolds into compact, rapidly evaluable representations without sacrificing physical accuracy. This perspective reviews the mathematical pillars of projection-based reduced-order modeling---including Proper Orthogonal Decomposition, greedy basis selection, and the Empirical Interpolation Method---and traces their operational impact in gravitational-wave data analysis through surrogate waveform families and Reduced-Order Quadratures. We then examine the fundamental approximation barriers posed by slow Kolmogorov n-width decay in non-stationary, transient, and strongly nonlinear regimes, showing how adaptive hp-greedy domain partitioning and multi-scale spectral compression overcome these limits. Finally, we discuss how combining certified reduced-order structures with modern scientific machine learning and sensitivity-aware quantization provides a rigorous blueprint for next-generation detectors such as LISA, Einstein Telescope, and Cosmic Explorer.
