CAPTOR: a vision-language foundation model for Chest-Abdomen-Pelvis Oncologic CT

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (248 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.14226v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Objectives: Radiologists interpreting oncologic chest-abdomen-pelvis (CAP) CT must also detect non-oncologic conditions, yet existing CT foundation models are trained on single anatomical regions or on non-oncologic data. We developed and evaluated CAPTOR, a vision-language foundation model for oncologic CAP CT that uses an anatomically structured preprocessing strategy to learn from whole examinations. Materials & Methods: CAPTOR is a contrastive vision-language model pretrained on 25,512 predominantly oncologic CAP CT-report pairs (single or multiple series) from a retrospective multicenter dataset (North America, Europe, Asia). It was compared with five CT foundation models, a larger-scale variant (CAPTOR-100K) and an ablation without anatomical preprocessing (CAPTOR-NoPre) on classification and retrieval using held-out internal and public test sets, and on survival prediction using TumorImagingBench. Confidence intervals and pairwise comparisons used patient-level bootstrap and permutation tests. Results: On 10,794 test studies, CAPTOR achieved higher AUC than all five models on CAP (0.767 vs. 0.695 for the next-best model; p<0.001) and oncologic subsets (0.784 vs. 0.734; p<0.001), with superior retrieval on both, and ranked first on three of four oncologic survival benchmarks. Despite substantially fewer training examinations, it performed comparably to CAPTOR-100K. Removing the anatomically structured preprocessing reduced classification performance, including on the institutionally held-out test subset (macro-AUC 0.737 vs. 0.691 for CAPTOR-NoPre, p<0.01). Conclusion: Anatomically structured preprocessing enabled whole-examination CAP CT interpretation, including multiple-series acquisitions, and improved downstream performance. CAPTOR outperformed state-of-the-art CT foundation models on CAP and oncologic subsets while matching a substantially larger-scale variant.
