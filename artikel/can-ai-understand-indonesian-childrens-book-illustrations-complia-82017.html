Can AI Understand Indonesian Children's Book Illustrations? Compliance, Safety, and the Cultural Alignment Gap

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (201 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23887v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Children's book illustrations carry developmental, pedagogical, and cultural responsibilities that are often formally codified in guidelines. As AI systems begin entering children's publishing pipelines, both as evaluators and as generators, a natural question arises: can they reliably meet the structural, safety, and cultural standards required? We investigate this across three criteria: guideline compliance, safety, and cultural alignment, using Indonesia's Book Leveling Guideline as a government-issued evaluation framework with explicit visual criteria for four reading levels. To understand how well VLMs can identify and apply these guideline criteria, we first evaluate them on real published children's book pages and compare their assessments against a human baseline. To then examine whether AI-generated illustrations meet the same standards, we apply the VLM judge to images produced by two generation models and cross-check them against a subset of human illustrator raters. We find that VLMs are reliable on explicit criteria such as compliance and safety, but fall short on cultural alignment, systematically missing content that human raters flag as culturally inappropriate. AI-generated illustrations show the same blind spot: they satisfy structural and safety criteria but fail to capture implicit cultural details that human raters consistently identify.
