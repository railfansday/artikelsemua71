Breaking: Graph-augmented Segmentation for Enhanced In-Situ Geometry Reconstruction of Complex Shapes in Laser Powder Bed Fusion

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2604.24234v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In situ geometric inspection in Laser Powder Bed Fusion of Metals (PBF-LB/M) opens latest possibilities for anticipated defect detection and more efficient qualification. It relies on image segmentation to reconstruct layer-wise geometries and identify manufacturing deviations as the part is being built. However, existing approaches face a critical yet largely overlooked challenge: photometric out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, namely the ability to maintain reconstruction accuracy when image appearance differs from the conditions represented during model training. This study represents the first exploration of a graph-augmented U-Net architecture for in situ geometric inspection in PBF-LB/M. The hybrid network integrates a Graph Neural Network (GNN) within the bottleneck to enhance reconstruction robustness under photometric distribution shifts. It is evaluated against conventional U-Net and active contour segmentation methods using two experimental case studies involving different lattice geometries, materials, and manufacturing setups, with X-ray computed tomography providing reference geometries. Generalization is assessed across different training and inference conditions and under controlled image perturbations. Results show that the UNet-GNN achieves higher reconstruction accuracy and consistency than the benchmark methods, with more pronounced improvements when training and inference conditions differ. Its advantages are confirmed across both case studies and under controlled photometric perturbations, while preliminary anomaly detection experiments indicate that improved robustness does not necessarily compromise sensitivity to geometric deviations. These findings highlight graph augmentation as a promising strategy for more reliable in situ geometric inspection under heterogeneous manufacturing conditions.
