JEV vs. LLMs: A Comparative Analysis for Phishing Email Detection

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (199 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23683v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Phishing remains a major source of credential theft and fraudulent payments, and email security systems must classify large message volumes reliably, promptly, and at manageable cost. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer flexible text understanding, but their token-based generation increases response time and API cost, and the reliability of their confidence values requires measurement. Jev 1.13, a typed decision model from TypeSafe AI, instead returns a structured outcome with a confidence value. This paper compares Jev 1.13 with Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra, and Meta Llama 4 Maverick on a labelled dataset of 1500 emails, covering classification performance, decision latency, token consumption, API cost, and probability calibration. Jev achieves the highest accuracy (79.67%), precision (71.43%), recall (97.97%), and F1 score (82.62%). It also records the shortest batch time (173.88 seconds), the lowest token count (597,895), the lowest cost (USD 0.015103 per 1000 decisions), and the lowest Brier score (0.1698) and ECE (0.1594). These results suggest that Jev is a promising option for fast, structured, high-volume triage. LLMs retain the advantage of generating explanations, which we do not evaluate, and all four systems require further validation before standalone deployment.
