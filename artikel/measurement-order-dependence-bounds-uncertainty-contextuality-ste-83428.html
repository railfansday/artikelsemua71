Measurement-order dependence bounds uncertainty, contextuality, steering, and Bell nonlocality

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (279 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24604v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Two quantum measurements applied to the same input in opposite orders give two joint distributions of the same outcomes. For L\"uders instruments we measure how far apart these order records can be, over all inputs, by their total-variation distance $\mathcal{T}$, their Jensen-Shannon divergence $\mathcal{J}$ (in bits), and their squared Hellinger distance. These order scores vanish exactly when the measurements commute, and $\mathcal{T}=\|[A,B]\|/4$ for two binary sharp observables. Some pairs of bases exceed mutually unbiased bases in squared Hellinger score, and in odd dimensions in $\mathcal{J}$, although their minimal entropy sum is smaller. For projective measurements, a Maassen-Uffink bound $q>0$ implies $\mathcal{T}\ge1-2^{-q}$ and a positive lower bound on $\mathcal{J}$. Measuring all observables of a scenario in sequence defines a joint distribution, so violations of noncontextuality, steering, and Bell inequalities are bounded by distances between order records. For correlation Bell inequalities with binary sharp observables, the squared Bell operator is a symmetrized (Jordan) part minus tensor products of local commutators. If one party has two settings, the violation is bounded bilinearly in the two parties' order scores; we conjecture this bound in general. On Clifford realizations it would follow from a Hilbert-Schmidt variant of Grothendieck's inequality, which we prove when one party has at most three settings. For three binary sharp measurements, the violation of Bell's original inequality by a maximally entangled state equals an order effect in three-measurement records of the maximally mixed state minus four times an alternation probability. For the rank-one Klyachko-Can-Binicio\u{g}lu-Shumovsky pentagon on a qutrit, the violation is at most the largest product of the order scores of two crossing diagonals, with equality at the maximal violation.
