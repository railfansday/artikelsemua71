The Spectral Distribution of Matrix Least Squares Solutions, and the Ellipsoid Fitting Problem

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (194 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23431v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We study the spectral distribution of the least squares solution to a random linear system whose unknown is a symmetric matrix. In the normalized and isotropic setting, we derive a closed-form description of the support of the spectral distribution. As an application, we consider the ellipsoid fitting problem: given $n$ standard Gaussian vectors in $\mathbb{R}^d$, does there exist a centered ellipsoid passing through all of them? It was recently shown that this problem has a sharp phase transition at $n \approx d^2/4$. On the satisfiability side, unlike earlier results, these sharp results do not rely on least squares candidates. We show that the least squares candidates considered in earlier works have a threshold of positive semi-definiteness at $n \approx d^2/10$, which was previously observed numerically and predicted non-rigorously using statistical physics methods. Moreover, we show that the corresponding least squares dual certificates have a threshold of positive definiteness at $n \approx 2d^2/5$. Our approach combines several matrix concentration tools. A key technical ingredient, which may be of independent interest, is showing that a certain kernel matrix is well-conditioned with high probability until $n \approx d^2/2$.
