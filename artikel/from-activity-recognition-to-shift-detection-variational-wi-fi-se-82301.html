From Activity Recognition to Shift Detection: Variational Wi-Fi Sensing with Evidential Uncertainty

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (218 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24596v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Human motion perturbs wireless channels, making commodity Wi-Fi devices a practical platform for device-free activity recognition. Yet Channel State Information (CSI) also depends strongly on the person, receiver position, recording session, and surrounding environment, so a classifier can continue producing activity labels after its deployment conditions have moved away from those seen during training. We propose Wi-HAR, a multi-antenna architecture in which four Variational Auto-Encoders learn compact distributions from raw Wi-Fi 6 CSI magnitudes and a delayed-fusion classifier combines their means and log-variances. On twelve activities from a public benchmark, Wi-HAR reaches 96.6% median recognition accuracy, compared with 70.8-81.5% for independently optimized single-antenna models, showing that the antennas provide complementary views of the same activity. We then use the same latent backbone to detect deployment shifts without using shifted samples for model selection or calibration. A class-conditional uncertainty score derived from Evidential Deep Learning achieves a macro-average ROC-AUC of 0.897 across eleven datasets that change receiver position, participant, recording session, or room, and detects 82.5% of shifted samples at a realized 5.2% in-distribution rejection rate. Detection performance nevertheless varies substantially across receiver positions, showing that the same physical change does not induce a uniform uncertainty response. The compact representation also supports shallow decision-tree analysis using variables from several antennas.
