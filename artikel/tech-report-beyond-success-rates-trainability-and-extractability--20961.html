Tech Report: Beyond Success Rates: Trainability and Extractability for Offline GCRL

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (270 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2602.05459v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) methods are typically benchmarked by the success rates of optimized hyperparameter configurations. However, success rate by itself does not provide information about the sensitivity of algorithms to hyperparameters for value learning or policy extraction, since an algorithm could obtain the same success rate across a wide range of hyperparameters, or might succeed only on specific settings. We study this phenomenon in the offline GCRL algorithms GCIQL, GCIVL, QRL, and CRL under a shared policy extractor, by constructing landscapes of interaction between the learning rate and policy extraction hyperparameters such as the temperature of advantage-weighted regression (AWR). These landscapes reveal large, task-dependent differences in hyperparameter sensitivity. On AntMaze-M, 59% of QRL configurations and 66% of CRL configurations achieve at least 90% of the method's best sampled success rate, compared with 3% for both GCIQL and GCIVL. On Cube and Scene, GCIQL and GCIVL instead attain higher success over broader hyperparameter regions than QRL and CRL. To interpret these differences, we analyze how reliably learned advantages rank actual goals from the same trajectory above random goals, and how strongly AWR concentrates policy updates on a small subset of transitions. Our findings indicate strongly differing behavior of these algorithms at policy-extraction time, even under the same success rates. Given these results, we argue that offline GCRL methods should be evaluated by both attainable success and the needed hyperparameter range to sustain it, complemented by post-hoc diagnostics to understand the signal used to train the policy. The overview and code are available at https://luh-ai.github.io/GCRL-Landscapes/ .
