Exact and Approximate Maximum Likelihood Estimation for Multivariate Diffusions under High-Frequency Sampling

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (235 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22675v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We establish the asymptotic theory of the exact maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) for discretely observed multivariate ergodic diffusions under high-frequency, long-span sampling, solving an open problem posed by \citet{YCW2019} and \citet{Li2013}. The drift may grow linearly, and the volatility is bounded away from zero and either bounded or proportional to $1+|x|$. When the sampling interval decays polynomially, the exact MLE is consistent, asymptotically normal and efficient, with rates $\sqrt{n\Delta_n}$ for drift and $\sqrt n$ for diffusion parameters. Under an additional condition on ergodic averages, and when the time span $n\Delta_n$ also grows polynomially, its moments converge as well. The proof never analyzes the exact likelihood directly: it indicates that a closed-form density expansion of sufficiently high order is uniformly accurate in relative terms at every observed transition, so that the two log-likelihoods differ by $o_P(1)$ uniformly in the parameter. The same argument yields accuracy conditions under which an approximate likelihood is asymptotically equivalent to the exact one; in particular, the approximate MLE of Yang, Chen and Wan converges to the true parameter. For the delta-expansion estimator of fixed order $K\ge1$, we derive the leading bias explicitly. It shows that $n\Delta_n^{K+1}\to0$ cannot be weakened when the bias constant is nonzero, and that the bias falls on the drift parameters for even $K$ and on the diffusion parameters for odd $K$. Monte Carlo experiments confirm the predicted biases.
