Contract-Explicit Evaluation: From Auditing Learned Readers to Scoped Certificates

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (207 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22665v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Evaluation readings of document-level relation extraction (DocRE) systems depend jointly on the model and the evaluation contract: gold version, scoring function, threshold. Rankings reverse across contracts, yet no existing method determines which comparisons survive the reversal. We address this gap with contract-explicit evaluation: a preregistered five-step protocol (Declare, Measure, Scope, Certify, Hedge), operationalizing the classical concordance decomposition of Kendall's rank correlation, that scopes certifiable regions and delivers distribution-free census certificates across two ecosystems. In the DocRE audit (44 training runs of one in-house architecture family), the scoring dimension scopes to a census certificate (three flagged instances, Clopper--Pearson upper bound 0.146); the confirmatory record includes registered negatives, and the audit prices its own simplification finding---the calibration dial's entire margin is one withdrawn commit. In an LLM-judge census (17 judges, 49,938 battles), 12.25% of leaderboard comparisons require hedging at the pre-declared split criterion (bound 0.124; panel-robust 0.143), with an exploratory two-tier reading certifying 69% at a bound \le 0.072 and a validity decomposition against released human labels (65% of contested battles are judge error, not ambiguity). The cross-model certificate is withdrawn on real confidence scores; the failure spectrum maps the boundary. The protocol, map, ledger, and per-instance artifacts are released.
