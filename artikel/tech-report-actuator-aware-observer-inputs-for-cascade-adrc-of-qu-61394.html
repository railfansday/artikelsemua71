Tech Report: Actuator-Aware Observer Inputs for Cascade ADRC of Quadrotors Under Rotor Lag, Measurement Delay and Sensor Noise

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (245 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.39126v4 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In sampled cascade linear active disturbance rejection control (LADRC) of quadrotors, the extended-state observer is usually driven by the instantaneous command, although the delayed measurement reflects an earlier, rotor-filtered acceleration. This simulation and software-in-the-loop (SITL) study examines actuator-aware (AA) observer inputs: the altitude and attitude observers use the limited virtual input filtered by a rotor-lag model and aligned with the measurement delay, and the horizontal observers use the acceleration reconstructed from the measured attitude. Fixed gains are selected on the sampled model under sensitivity and step-response requirements and covariance-based ceilings on the noise-induced control increment, including the path from position noise to the inner loop. In 30 paired set-point trials, the two AA designs reach position root-mean-square errors (RMSEs) of 19.2 and 16.9~mm, versus 65.5~mm for a proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controller tuned under the same requirements, with lower rotor-command increments. Ablations attribute the improvement to the rotor-lag model and the measured-attitude input; delay alignment is negligible at the nominal 5-ms delay. PX4 SITL runs reproduce these results within 2~mm. Behind the PX4 state estimator, the white-noise ceilings no longer describe the loop. After redesign with an identified estimation-error model, one AA design reduces the set-point RMSE from 59.1 to 44.0~mm with a 15\% lower quantized rotor-command increment, whereas the half-gain variant is worse than the PID. With fixed gains, the AA designs tolerate less unmodelled rotor lag and delay than the PID. Hardware validation remains future work.
