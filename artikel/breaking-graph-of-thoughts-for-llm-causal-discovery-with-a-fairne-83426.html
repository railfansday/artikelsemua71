Breaking: Graph-of-Thoughts for LLM Causal Discovery with a Fairness-Pathway Case Study

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2608.02877v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Methods that use large language models (LLMs) for causal discovery reason locally, deciding one variable pair or one node at a time, so they never compare alternative whole graphs. We introduce GoT-CD, a Graph-of-Thoughts method that asks an LLM for several complete candidate graphs from variable metadata alone, ranks them with a deterministic validity score, merges them under a hard union constraint, refines the merge, and projects the result to a DAG. We evaluate on Asia, Child, and an Alzheimer's disease graph with 100 samples and GPT-4o-mini. Over five seeds on Alzheimer's and Child, GoT-CD gives the highest mean F1 score and the lowest mean structural Hamming distance of the LLM methods, with 95% intervals of the difference excluding zero in seven of eight comparisons, and it returns a DAG in every run while using three LLM requests per graph. Replaying the logged runs without individual components attributes most of the gain to generating and merging several complete candidates. A case study on the effect of Sex on MOCA Score in the Alzheimer's graph shows the limits of structural scores: the fairness verdict drawn from the discovered graph disagrees with the reference verdict in twelve of fifteen LLM runs, including all five GoT-CD runs, which mostly drop the edge out of Sex. Graphs meant for a specific downstream analysis should therefore also be checked on the pathways that analysis depends on.
