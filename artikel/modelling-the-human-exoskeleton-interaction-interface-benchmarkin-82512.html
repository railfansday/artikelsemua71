Modelling the human-exoskeleton interaction interface - Benchmarking MuJoCo's deformable model

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (214 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.24008v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The human-exoskeleton interaction interface, consisting of the exoskeleton attachment and the underlying human soft tissue, can affect the transfer of assistance. MuJoCo's \textit{flexcomp} has been previously used to model the soft tissue, but its accuracy has not been benchmarked against a finite element model. We benchmarked the \textit{flexcomp} Saint Venant-Kirchhoff (StVK) and Stable Neo-Hookean (SNH) models against COMSOL's Yeoh model in three cases: \textit{case 1}) spherical indentation, \textit{case 2}) compression, and \textit{case 3}) compression of a thigh. We compared force and strain energy as a function of indentation depth, hysteresis and computation time. MuJoCo's models capture force trends with a correlation of 0.97-0.99, but showed a stiffer response, which could be attributed to the volumetric locking of the linear finite elements. The MuJoCo models systematically underestimated hysteresis as MuJoCo's Rayleigh damping could not substitute a viscoelastic model. Moreover, MuJoCo's computational advantage was speed dependent: it was 2-2.5 times faster for spherical indentation, but COMSOL was faster for the other cases, which have more degrees of freedom and contacts. Although MuJoCo's \textit{flexcomp} offers a combination of soft-tissue modelling and multibody dynamics, the limitations regarding the stiffening and hysteresis should be addressed to be used for the analysis of the human-exoskeleton interaction interface.
