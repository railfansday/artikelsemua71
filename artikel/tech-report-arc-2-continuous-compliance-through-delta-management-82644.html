Tech Report: ARC-2: Continuous Compliance Through Delta Management

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (224 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21811v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: ARC 1 introduced a three-layer governance architecture for autonomous systems comprising model safety validation, cognitive certification (CRAB), and operational authorization (LEA). However, it did not address how compliance should be maintained after deployment. This paper introduces Delta Management, a continuous governance framework that measures deviations between a system's current operational state and its certified baseline, defined as a region rather than a fixed point. Compliance is assessed through continuous monitoring instead of static certification. Four extensions are proposed. First, CRAB evaluation incorporates the worst verified behavioral deviations, with isolated outliers quarantined pending confirmation to prevent unjustified restrictions. Second, a layered trigger taxonomy distinguishes hard safety violations, critical single-dimension failures, correlated multi-system degradation, and risk-weighted composite indicators, each triggering proportionate responses. Third, a graduated safe transition protocol separates withdrawal of operational authorization from physical shutdown, consistent with established safety standards and EU AI Act requirements. Fourth, environmental delta is introduced as a distinct baseline validity condition: environmental drift beyond the certified Operational Design Domain (ODD) triggers recertification or ODD revision rather than automatic authority contraction. Together, these extensions transform ARC from a deployment-time certification architecture into a continuous framework for monitoring, evaluating, and enforcing compliance throughout the operational lifecycle of autonomous systems. The framework addresses the continuous compliance obligations established by EU AI Act Article 72(2).
