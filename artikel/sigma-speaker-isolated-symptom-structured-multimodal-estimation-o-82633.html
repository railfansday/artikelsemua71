SIGMA: speaker-isolated, symptom-structured multimodal estimation of depression severity from clinical interviews

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (148 words, ⚡ Quick Read)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.23348v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Clinical interviews are central to depression assessment but time-consuming and rater-dependent. Automated severity estimation offers scalable screening, yet existing methods often retain interviewer speech (confounding participant cues), fuse modalities late (losing fine-grained cross-modal dynamics), or score symptoms independently (ignoring inter-symptom correlations). We present SIGMA, a speaker-aware, symptom-structured framework for depression-severity estimation from semi-clinical interviews. SIGMA uses speaker diarization to restrict input to participant speech, cross-attention so that text, audio and video condition one another during encoding, and a graded-response head that predicts the eight questionnaire items jointly through a shared latent severity trait with item-specific ordered thresholds. On the Extended Distress Analysis Interview Corpus, SIGMA achieves state-of-the-art performance (CCC 0.762, RMSE 4.13, MAE 3.30 over five seeds). Ablations confirm that isolating participant speech and joint, structured symptom prediction each substantially improve concordance and estimation accuracy.
