Quantum Reference Frames, Locality, and Microcausality in Quantum Field Theory

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (207 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21768v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: As a consequence of the relativistic spectrum condition, quantum reference frames for translations on Minkowski space are never described by projection-valued measures, and in many cases fail a weaker spacetime localisation property known as the norm-$1$ condition. This has several implications for the local and observable structure of quantum field theories described relative to even the best available frame: for a large class of frame states, strict locality is replaced by almost locality, and microcausality by commutators that decay rapidly with increasing separation, corresponding to an effective smearing of the light cone by the localisation density of the frame state. Operations built from finitely many almost local Kraus operators, for example nonselective measurements of relativised local observables, satisfy an approximate no-signalling bound, with a residue attributable to the localisation uncertainty of the frame. Whether causality in its strongest operational form -- non-disturbance under all physically realisable operations -- survives relativisation is left open. An upshot of our finding is that quantum field theories formulated relative to a quantum reference frame cannot rest on the traditional axioms, and some general relational axiomatisation must replace at least some of them by taking into account physically realistic properties of the frame.
