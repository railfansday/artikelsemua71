AfriNORM: An Evaluation Benchmark for Normative Reasoning of LLMs Across African Cultures

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (207 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22580v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed across various sectors. However, current evaluation benchmarks are insufficient to assess the applicability of these models in local African contexts, as most focus on linguistic fluency (grammar/translation) rather than cultural competence. The few existing benchmarks that measure general knowledge or social conventions do not capture the African deontic modality, i.e., the strict norms and taboos that govern diverse social interactions. In this paper, we introduce AfriNORM, a scenario-based, multiple-choice question dataset designed to measure and diagnose the normative reasoning limitations of LLMs when applied to African sociocultural contexts. We introduce two metrics: a Universal Commonsense Bias Score (UCBS) to quantify commonsense bias in LLM and a Distractor Bias Score (DBS) to quantify an LLM's limited understanding of African cultural norms. We also conduct a case study on the Yor\`ub\'a language, identifying confusion gaps in two closed-source LLMs, "Gemini 2.0 Flash" and "GPT-5", and one open-source LLM, "Llama 3.1 8B Instruct" when prompted in English versus Yor\`ub\'a. While their accuracy in English is high, their performance in Yor\`ub\'a is much lower, attributable to cultural confusion rather than simple bias, indicating that the models exhibit semantic confusion when reasoning about norms in Yor\`ub\'a.
