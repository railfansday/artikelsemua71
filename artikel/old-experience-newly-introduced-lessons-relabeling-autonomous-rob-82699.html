Old Experience, Newly introduced Lessons: Relabeling Autonomous Robot Rollouts in Interactive Imitation Learning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (219 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.24273v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Interactive imitation learning enables robots to learn from human interventions during deployment. These interactions naturally produce sparse human corrections and abundant autonomous robot experience without human action labels. Yet existing methods either leave this experience unused for action supervision or imitate historical robot actions, which can reinforce suboptimal behavior. To address this limitation, we propose Set-valued Relabeling of Autonomous experience (SeRA), a framework that learns from autonomous robot experience through set-valued supervision of policy relabels. Later policy checkpoints relabel this experience, and agreement across checkpoints filters the relabels. Since agreement does not guarantee correctness, SeRA constructs desired action sets around accepted relabels. Following Set-Supervised Diffusion Policy (SDP), it trains an action-chunking diffusion policy to increase the probability of actions within these sets. A step-masked denoising loss combines accepted relabels with available human corrections in partially supervised chunks. Experiments across five simulated manipulation tasks in online and offline settings show that SeRA improves policy performance over SDP and outperforms baselines that imitate historical robot actions or exact relabels. On a real-robot tomato-picking task, SeRA achieves 80% success, compared with 40% for SDP, using the same human feedback and training budget. These results demonstrate that relabeled autonomous robot experience can complement human corrections to improve policy performance.
