Latest Update: NONSENSE: Non-Semantic Strings Expose Semanticity Gaps in Dense Retrieval

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (147 words, ⚡ Quick Read)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.22353v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Dense retrieval is central to modern search, question answering, and retrieval-augmented generation, where high similarity is often treated as evidence of semantic relevance. We examine how far this assumption can be stressed. NONSENSE is a black-box sparse-edit probe that constructs visibly non-semantic strings while forbidding query-term copying, optimizing only the dense similarity between a query and the string. Across five BEIR-style datasets and three popular single-vector retrievers, these strings frequently outscore the retriever's own best judged query-relevant passage (QRP), and often rank first in the full corpus. The result indicates a semanticity gap in the evaluated dense retrievers: nearest-neighbor status can be separated from passage-like meaning. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating dense retrievers not only by whether they retrieve relevant documents, but also by how reliably their similarity scores reflect semantic evidence.
