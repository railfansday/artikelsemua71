When accuracy hides risk: risk-controlled confirmation of the critical view of safety under expert disagreement

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (257 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23781v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Automated assessment of the critical view of safety (CVS) in laparoscopic cholecystectomy has advanced rapidly, but a deployed check must decide, before the first clip, whether to confirm CVS. The standard rule calibrates a threshold so that false confirmations are rare on average. We show that this rule concentrates its errors on disputed videos, which one of three expert raters judged sufficient, and that the failure is structural. When reference labels are votes of a panel sampled from a larger expert pool, the rule that maximizes sensitivity at a fixed average false-confirmation rate also maximizes false confirmation of disputed videos, and for recognizers that recover expert judgement with less noise this rate rises with accuracy. We propose certified confirmation, a risk-controlled monitoring rule whose threshold is selected by multiple testing so that, with probability at least 1 - {\delta}, false confirmation is at most {\alpha} overall, among disputed videos, within vendor and country groups, and before CVS is visible, using disputed labels only to calibrate. In a pre-specified, hash-locked evaluation on the 300 test videos (18 countries) of the SAGES CVS Challenge, standard calibration falsely confirmed 8 of 39 disputed videos (0.21; one-sided p = 0.037 against {\alpha} = 0.10), whereas certified confirmation confirmed 2 (one-sided 95% upper bound 0.15). On development data, protection held for vendor and country groups left out of calibration. The cost was confirming 10% rather than 35% of sufficient videos on time. Average accuracy hides this risk; disputed cases must be protected explicitly.
