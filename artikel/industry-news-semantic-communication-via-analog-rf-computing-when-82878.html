Industry News: Semantic Communication via Analog RF Computing: When the Radio Computes the Meaning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22100v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Semantic communication (SC) systems transmit continuous latent representations of meaning, but computing the semantic encoder at resource-constrained edge devices incurs substantial energy and memory costs. To address this challenge, we propose an end-to-end SC framework that executes the semantic encoder in the client's RF receiver front end using analog RF computing. A base station (BS) broadcasts encoder weights as RF waveforms, while each edge device reuses its receiver's passive mixer to compute semantic latents at ultra-low energy. These latents are transmitted over the uplink for network-side task inference. We show that analog computing noise across encoder layers and channel noise affecting transmitted latents admit a unified mathematical model. We define a task-posterior semantic distortion metric and derive its second-order decomposition into per-client, per-stage semantic sensitivities. Using this decomposition, we obtain a closed-form semantic-aware water-filling allocation of the total client energy budget across clients, encoder layers, and uplinks, assigning tighter accuracy targets to semantically sensitive stages. This allocation induces a broadcast beamforming problem that accounts for client-specific, per-layer accuracy requirements. We further refine the allocation through stochastic optimization and jointly design it with multi-task noise-aware training of the shared semantic model. MNIST simulations show that, at matched total client energy, the proposed allocation reduces network semantic distortion by 9.2\%-19.2\% compared with uniform precision and 5.1\%-13.1\% compared with client-blind allocation. Noise-aware co-design yields a further 46\% reduction.
