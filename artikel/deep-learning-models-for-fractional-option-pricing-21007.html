Deep Learning Models for Fractional Option Pricing

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (200 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21988v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This paper investigates whether neural networks can infer fractional option prices and the Hurst parameter directly from sequences of fractional Gaussian returns, without separately estimating the latent parameters for each new sequence. Feedforward neural networks (FNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks are trained to jointly predict European call prices, put prices, and the Hurst parameter from 252-return sequences. A controlled simulation varies the total sample size from 5,000 to 500,000 sequences and evaluates point prediction, empirical residual-based prediction intervals, and computational performance. The neural-network models are also compared on a common simulated test set with Bayesian joint estimation and exact Gaussian maximum likelihood estimation. In that four-method comparison, the LSTM achieves the lowest Hurst-parameter root mean squared error (0.033) and option-price root mean squared error (1.305). After training, neural-network predictions are substantially faster than repeated parameter estimation, although the methods quantify uncertainty in different ways. Applications to five financial return series illustrate differences between data-driven and model-based fractional price estimates. The results support sequence-based learning as a computationally efficient complement to statistical inference under the assumed fractional model; they do not establish out-of-distribution accuracy or accuracy against observed traded-option prices.
