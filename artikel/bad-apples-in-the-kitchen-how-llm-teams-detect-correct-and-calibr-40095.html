Bad Apples in the Kitchen: How LLM Teams Detect, Correct, and Calibrate Against Their Misaligned Members

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (183 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22342v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: What protects an LLM team when one teammate quietly goes wrong? Most multi-agent LLM work assumes cooperative partners and inspects chat between turns before acting. Real-time embodied cooperation does not allow this pause: chat runs alongside action, so a teammate whose words stay cooperative while behavior diverges can cause damage faster than the team can name what is happening. We study this case by injecting three theory-grounded misaligned agents into a custom four-player Overcooked environment, each stressing a different joint-action layer (speech-action mismatch, role invasion, and aggregate-progress disruption), and decompose robustness into three sequential gates of detection, correction, and calibration through a 3x4x2 design (n=720 episodes). Nearly all trolls are eventually named, but recovery follows only when attribution and situational diagnosis appear in the same utterance; direct source-attribution prompting improves the hardest case only by degrading the troll-free baseline by 37.5%. Robustness, then, depends less on detection alone than on whether team chat combines attribution with diagnosis in a single utterance, suggesting that sensitivity to misalignment should be targeted rather than uniformly raised.
