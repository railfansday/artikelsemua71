Tech Report: Global sensitivity analysis of transient regimes in non-Newtonian dam-break flows applied in rheometry

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (212 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24335v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Non-Newtonian dam-break flows are a classical problem with numerous engineering applications. These flows are particularly key in alternative rheometry, where they are used to rapidly and affordably estimate rheological properties, such as in the slump test and Bostwick consistometer test. However, the lifting dynamics of the containing obstacle introduce inertial effects that can compromise the accurate estimation of these properties. These effects are influenced by the lifting velocity, the fluid's viscosity, and the initial geometry of the sample. This study aims to quantify the influence of lifting velocity and to provide physical insights into how inertial effects influence the flow. A global sensitivity analysis (GSA) framework adapted for functional outputs (e.g., time series) is employed, using data generated from high-fidelity simulations with ANSYS Fluent. The GSA produces sensitivity curves that track the evolving importance of each input parameter over time. Three distinct flow regimes are identified, namely mobilization, transition and viscous regimes, each governed by different dominant factors. The first two regimes are significantly affected by the lifting velocity, revealing the influence of inertial effects, whereas the viscous regime is dominated by fluid rheology and reveals a increasing importance of the yield stress. These results are contextualized in terms of traditional rheometry tests.
