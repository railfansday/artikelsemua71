Tech Report: Confidence-Based Materials Data Quality Assessment via Domain Knowledge and Graph-Structured Learning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (233 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23342v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: The rapid development of materials machine learning relies on large-scale, heterogeneous datasets, yet model reliability is often compromised by imperfect data from diverse experiments, simulations, and database integration. Real-world materials databases may contain measurement errors, failed calculations, missing or duplicate records, and hidden violations of physicochemical constraints. Such problems are difficult to identify because corrupted samples may remain statistically plausible rather than appearing as explicit outliers. In this paper, we propose a confidence-based materials data quality assessment framework that integrates domain knowledge with graph-structured learning. Data quality is formulated as a continuous confidence estimation problem rather than a binary anomaly identification task, enabling sample-level assessment and historical-reference-based batch comparison. A nine-dimensional feature-quality prior and duplicate-aware sample initialization are calibrated using materials domain rules and statistical evidence. Confidence is further refined through sample-feature bipartite graph propagation and weakly supervised graph attention, while a Memory-GAT module provides historical reference scoring. Experiments on 23 ODDS benchmark datasets show that the generic module achieves an average AUC-ROC of 0.866. In five repeated mixed-contamination experiments on mp\_20, rule calibration improves HistGradientBoostingRegressor test $R^2$ from $0.538\pm0.008$ to $0.616\pm0.014$. Graph refinement increases average precision from $0.943\pm0.004$ to 0.953\pm0.004$, while the rule-calibrated configuration has the highest mean test $R^2$ among the evaluated screening configurations. Five repeated MACE subset experiments further show an increase in $R^2$ from $0.590\pm0.053$ to $0.880\pm0.027$ after rule-based screening.
