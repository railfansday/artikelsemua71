Wall-to-wall maps of canopy height and aboveground carbon across the Atlantic Forest

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (179 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.23048v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Global canopy height maps are increasingly used to estimate forest carbon, yet their regional accuracy is rarely tested. We present wall-to-wall maps of canopy height (10 m) and aboveground carbon density (30 m) for the Brazilian Atlantic Forest in 2024. Height was predicted by a deep learning network fusing Sentinel-2 reflectance, Sentinel-1 and ALOS PALSAR backscatter and ALOS elevation, trained on 7,179 and tested on 1,793 patches (58,800 km$^2$) from a statewide airborne LiDAR campaign over S\~ao Paulo state, Brazil. On held-out test patches, the mean absolute error is 2.26 m at 10 m and 1.90 m at 30 m, and the model was validated at two LiDAR sites outside S\~ao Paulo. Over the same pixels, the Meta and World Resources Institute global product has more than twice this error. A power law fitted to 124 inventory plots converted canopy height to above ground carbon density. The remaining forest holds 1,944 TgC over 28.3 Mha. Global products therefore need local validation before use in carbon accounting.
