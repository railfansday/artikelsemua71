Experimental constraints on gauge hidden-variable models in Bell tests

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (188 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23418v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum mechanics assigns identical statistics to measurement procedures that realize the same final effect, irrespective of the transformations used to reach it. We test this operational equivalence with polarization-entangled photons by implementing one of Bob's analyzer settings either directly or through closed wave-plate trajectories $C_\gamma$ calibrated to produce the same measurement operator. Motivated by ontological descriptions in which a hidden frame is transported between contexts, we formulate a local path-memory model and derive the mixed-context bound $|S_\gamma^{\rm mix}|\leq 2+2p_\gamma$. The quantity $p_\gamma$ is a coupling-dependent counterfactual mismatch probability: it bounds a contextual Bell expression, but neither generates nor predicts the quantum Bell correlation. We therefore test path memory through the observable difference between direct and looped implementations, using a no-signaling phenomenological response in which a hidden holonomy $\delta_\gamma$ modifies one correlation term in $S_\gamma^{\rm mix}$. The direct reference violates the Bell inequality, while the looped mixed-context curves remain indistinguishable from it within the experimental precision. Our results support the quantum equivalence of calibrated measurement implementations and constrain path-sensitive hidden-variable extensions that predict an observable memory of the optical history.
