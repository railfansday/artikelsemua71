Expressiveness and Convertibility of Rule-Based Access Control Policies

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (176 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.23738v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Rule-based access control models such as attribute-based access control support flexible policies based on rules with conditional expressions. The design choices in those models such as what is allowed in the conditional expressions or supporting deny rules may impact the range of the policies that are expressible. In this paper, we propose a systematic approach to investigate the semantics and compare the expressiveness of various rule-based access control models that can be built based on different design choices of rule structure and rule composition. Furthermore, we investigate the problem of convertibility of a policy from one rule-based model to another. This question is critical for policy migration to ensure that a given policy instance in a source model is expressible in a target model. Some of our major results in this paper include well-formedness properties for policy semantics, expressiveness hierarchy of models, and computational complexity of policy convertibility, all having important implications on the design and application of rule-based access control policy models.
