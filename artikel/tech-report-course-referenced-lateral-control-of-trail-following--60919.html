Tech Report: Course-Referenced Lateral Control of Trail-Following Convoys: String Stability and Adaptive Feedforward under Load Uncertainty

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (192 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.20320v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Vehicles in a convoy can steer along the GNSS trails of the vehicles ahead when lane markings are unavailable, as on snow covered roads. Since each vehicle's tracking error enters the reference of the next, lateral errors can grow along the convoy. This paper presents a course referenced formulation of trail following in arc length, in which each follower takes the direction of its fitted trail as its heading reference. Error propagation then reduces to a single transfer function. We derive a condition under which combining the trails of the leader and the predecessor keeps the errors bounded for any convoy length, and show that following the predecessor alone lets the errors accumulate without bound, even without amplification, on roads that end turned or shifted sideways. Passenger and cargo loads and tire stiffness vary in practice, and we show that they enter the curvature feedforward through a single coefficient, whose mismatch leaves steady offsets on curves. We propose an adaptive law that estimates this coefficient on curves and removes the offsets. Simulations of an eleven vehicle convoy confirm the analysis.
