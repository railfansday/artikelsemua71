Scalable spin-nematic squeezing in multi-level dipole-interacting Rydberg atom arrays

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (213 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2605.00096v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We study the dynamical generation of metrologically useful entanglement in arrays of three-level (spin-1) systems coupled by spin-exchange interactions, as realized by dipole-interacting Rydberg atoms in optical tweezers. We quantify the metrological gain by a generalized squeezing parameter optimized over all linear $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ phase generators and readout observables. For equal exchange couplings on the two transitions, the dynamics reduces to one-axis twisting (OAT) of an effective two-level system. Away from this point, the full three-level structure provides a genuine advantage. For all-to-all couplings, the optimal squeezing at $N=4000$ exceeds the OAT value by a factor of 3.6, with a fitted scaling exponent of $0.93$ compared with $0.69$ for OAT over $100\le N\le4000$. For two-dimensional dipolar arrays, exact and tensor-network simulations up to $N=49$ show that the optimal three-level protocol outperforms the effective spin-1/2 dynamics, with a gain that increases with system size, and semiclassical simulations show that squeezing continues to grow up to $N=10^4$. Crucially, our results demonstrate a real advantage of utilizing the full three-level $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ structure of the spin-1 system over $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ type one-axis twisting dynamics. Our results extend interaction-driven scalable squeezing beyond the well-studied qubit setting to spin-exchange-coupled qudits with dipolar interactions, and can be realized in current Rydberg tweezer experiments.
