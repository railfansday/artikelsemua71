Industry News: Is 4-Bit Quantization More Than Noise to a Chat Model's Safety Behaviour? Attributing Behavioural Shifts with Matched Perturbations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (207 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22186v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Chat models on phones and laptops are usually 4-bit copies of the model that was evaluated, and audits of such copies disagree about what quantization does to refusal. A 4-bit build is one of many weight perturbations of the same size, so a change in its behaviour could come from its specific rounding error or from any perturbation that large. In a pre-registered study of three 3-4B chat models, with paired tests, blind human labels and an 8-bit null, we find two 4-bit shifts of opposite sign: with a compliant reply prefilled, Qwen2.5-3B continues into harmful content more often, and on held-out jailbreaks Llama-3.2-3B complies 10.3 points less often. Matched perturbations separate the two. Shuffled and Gaussian versions of the error average close to zero, while scaling the real error, from its mirror image to twice its size, changes behaviour near-linearly. At half the real error, Llama's likelihood is almost unchanged, yet it already complies 5.8 points less often, and llama.cpp's Q4_K_M, at the same bits per weight, moves neither model. Refusal under 4-bit quantization depends on the specific rounding error, not on bits per weight or likelihood, so each build has to be compared with its original.
