ABACUS: Program-Guided Multistep Reasoning over Frozen Judgment Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (197 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.25963v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Judgment models answer semantic questions by scoring a finite set of candidate outcomes. Can reusable programs turn these local decisions into powerful multistep reasoning? We introduce \method, a framework that jointly constructs judgment schemas and composition programs for frozen judgment models. A generative teacher designs the procedure offline; the judgment model resolves local semantic questions online, and the saved program composes its decisions into an answer. We study three allocations of design effort between humans and the teacher, focusing on construction through a shared interface. We instantiate the framework with Kev. On three selected MASSIVE-Bench Hard tasks, nine independently constructed programs solve all 450 executions of 150 development inputs. A separate deployment study freezes three programs after development-set reference-agreement refinement and evaluates them on 600 held-out questions. These programs achieve near-perfect 99.67\% macro accuracy, versus 64.00\% for direct Kev and 68.80\% for final-answer-only Qwen3.5-4B. They attain comparable observed accuracy to Qwen's unrestricted response policy (99.07\%) with an \textbf{8.43-fold online speedup}: 0.255 versus 2.150 seconds per question. These results show how reusable programs can substantially expand the reasoning capability of a compact judgment model while keeping its parameters fixed.
