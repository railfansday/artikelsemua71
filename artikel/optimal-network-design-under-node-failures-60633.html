Optimal Network Design under Node Failures

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (250 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.21856v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Networks are frequently constructed before it is determined which nodes will persist. We address the design problem in scenarios where nodes fail independently, links are costly and must be established in advance, and the benefits to surviving nodes diminish with distance. Examples include supplier, interbank, and infrastructure networks. Among all possible networks, the complete, star, and empty configurations are each welfare-optimal within specific ranges of link costs. However, with four or more nodes, failures introduce a range of costs in which every optimal network contains more links than the star but fewer than the complete network. At the lower end of this range, we show that the optimal networks are exactly those obtained by removing a matching from the complete network. We develop an exact algorithm for complete multipartite networks. It finds an optimal network by dynamic programming in a number of operations that grows with the fourth power of the number of nodes, although the class itself grows superpolynomially. For networks with up to six nodes, restricting the design to this class results in no loss under explicit conditions. We also characterize pairwise-stable networks within this class and establish their existence for every level of link cost. Finally, we quantify the cost of misjudging failure risk. Overestimating node survival leads to overbuilding and can yield lower welfare than building nothing, whereas underestimating it never does. Under uncertain failure risk, design should therefore favor conservative reliability estimates.
