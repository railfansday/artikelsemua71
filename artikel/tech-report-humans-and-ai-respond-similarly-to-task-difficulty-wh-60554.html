Tech Report: Humans and AI respond similarly to task difficulty when reasoning about content moderation dilemmas

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (226 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2508.20262v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Large reasoning models (LRMs) generate intermediate reasoning steps before producing answers, improving performance on challenging problems by iteratively developing solutions. There is evidence that LRMs expend more effort on the problems that take people longer to solve, but it is unclear whether these relationships hold on more subjective tasks without verifiable answers. This study addresses this glitch through an experiment using a content moderation task, where task difficulty is manipulated by varying the relative offensiveness of two different options. Comparing six large reasoning models with 1,854 human subjects, we find that models expend more reasoning effort, measured using the length of the chain-of-thought (CoT), on the kinds of cases that take people longer to adjudicate. People took 31\% longer and models produced 51 to 306\% longer CoTs when offensiveness was held constant across the two posts. The correlation is relatively low for individual decisions ($r = 0.15$--$0.19$), but average response times and average reasoning effort are closely correlated when tasks are grouped by the types of offensive language ($r = 0.85$--$0.93$). Qualitative analyses of the CoT summaries from Gemini 2.5 Pro corroborate these findings, showing that secondary factors were mentioned more often when offensiveness was fixed. The results point to the potential for using reasoning traces to prioritize difficult cases and enhance interpretability.
