Tech Report: SCOUT: Risk-Guided Foundation-Model Escalation for Robust Tissue Detection in Whole-Slide Images

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (251 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24366v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Reliable tissue detection is essential for whole-slide image analysis, yet high average segmentation accuracy can conceal failures involving pale tissue, small fragments, and heterogeneous staining. Pathology foundation models offer transferable representations, but exhaustive inference is computationally demanding and may introduce false-positive tissue predictions. We propose SCOUT (Selective Correction of Uncertain Tissue), a risk-guided cascade that selectively refines an initial thumbnail-based tissue mask. A lightweight audit network estimates the spatial distribution of segmentation errors and prioritizes candidate regions for processing by a frozen pathology foundation model. An asymmetric consensus rule integrates the resulting predictions with the initial mask to recover missed tissue while limiting false positives. A calibrated slide-level review score additionally controls the marginal rate of unflagged case-group failures under the calibration assumptions. Evaluation on a public multi-source, multi-stain benchmark demonstrates that SCOUT reduces the proportion of case groups requiring mask correction from 38.1% for the best released detector to 18.6%, with particularly pronounced improvements in immunohistochemistry. Source-held-out experiments demonstrate better generalization than a segmentation network trained on the same annotations, while selective inference achieves an observed failure rate no higher than processing all candidate regions. Downstream, lymph-node classification with SCOUT masks is non-inferior to that obtained with two of the three released detectors evaluated. A variant built on an openly licensed foundation model is publicly released as an installable Python package with trained weights. These results support risk-guided foundation-model inference as an effective approach to tissue detection across heterogeneous histopathology data.
