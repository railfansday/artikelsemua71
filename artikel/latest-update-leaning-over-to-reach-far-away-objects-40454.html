Latest Update: Leaning Over to Reach Far-Away Objects

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (214 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.19100v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: We study supported reaching to extend a humanoid robot's workspace by bracing against the environment for stabilization using a straightforward yet capable whole-body model predictive control (MPC) approach, exclusively with local sensing and fiducial markers. While typical solutions for whole body humanoid control aim at avoiding or accommodating contact, contact is explicitly required to reach objects placed on surfaces far out of normal reach. We employ sampling-based MPC for whole-body leaning, bracing, reaching, and recovery on a Unitree H1-2 humanoid. The controller runs in real-time at 34 Hz on a laptop, transfers sim-to-real, extends reach by about 50 cm beyond the unbraced standing workspace, and sustains the loaded posture for over four minutes, before pose estimation drifts. The controller reaches the commanded target in 89% of trials and completes the full reach-and-recovery cycle in 81%, across 54 trials spanning six targets and three table heights. We further characterize why such a small real-time sampling budget is sufficient, finding that temporal persistence of sampled actions and exploration scale strongly govern success, while CEM provides the widest operating region over planning rate and model mismatch. These results establish a real-world baseline for online, contact-rich humanoid manipulation. Code, videos, and full hardware details are available at https://h12leaner.github.io/.
