Tech Report: Towards Pose-Robust Few-Shot Vision-Language-Action Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (287 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24360v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Fine-tuning a vision-language-action (VLA) model on a few dozen demonstrations is the standard way to put a manipulation policy on a robot, yet the resulting policy silently assumes that the camera and the robot base stay exactly where they were during data collection: a bumped tripod or a base re-docked a few centimetres away makes it reach for the wrong place. We show that both failures are one phenomenon, a change of the camera-to-base extrinsic that the policy has memorised, and that the robot carries everything needed to undo it. We introduce WACO, a world-anchored canonicalization framework for VLA models: the arm, placed by forward kinematics at the measured joint angles, serves as a metric calibration target, and the static scene serves as the world anchor that tells a moved camera from a moved robot. From a single RGB-D frame, WACO estimates both shifts without ground-truth extrinsics or extra data, canonicalises the image to the calibration viewpoint and the state, action and start pose to the calibration base frame, and fine-tunes the unmodified policy under the artefacts of its own estimator. We also introduce LIBERO-PoseShift, the first benchmark for RGB VLAs with graded camera, base and compound shifts. Across LIBERO-PoseShift, the LIBERO-Plus camera track and four real-world tasks on a UR5e robot, WACO consistently outperforms the standard few-shot recipe and prior viewpoint-robust methods, raising shifted success by 13.2 points in simulation and by 36.7 points on the real robot, recovering 90% of the simulation gap to a ground-truth oracle and 87% of the real-robot gap, and establishing a new state of the art among methods that use no perturbed training data, with no test-time calibration. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/WACO/.
