ESI: An Embodied Self-Improvement Flywheel for LLM-VLA Agents

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (240 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.18176v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Independently pretrained language models and vision-language-action (VLA) policies provide complementary building blocks for embodied agents, but connecting them does not automatically produce coordinated behavior. The VLA may generate actions that diverge from the LLM's intended subtask and, upon regaining control after a correction, undo the promising state restored by the LLM. We introduce ESI (Embodied Self-Improvement), an automated flywheel that uses environmental interaction to align reasoning and action. ESI treats the modular agent as the unit of post-training, repeatedly refining the prompts that guide the LLM, the shared skill interface that expresses its intent, and the VLA parameters that realize that intent, then redeploying these components together. Our key insight is that collaboration itself can supply supervision for improving this interface. The LLM's subtask decisions provide semantic supervision for what the policy should execute, while its effective corrections and subsequent student continuations provide action-level supervision for recovery and handoff. The student also learns from its own effective execution, allowing locally useful experience to contribute even when the overall episode fails. On ten selected RoboDojo tasks, ESI achieves a mean Score of 62.03 and a mean success rate of 49.41% using an LLM-VLA agent without generating task-specific control code. On both metrics, it outperforms the published reference results of all hybrid embodied agents, including the Agent+WAM system GO-3 Nano Preview (47.27 Score and 36.60% success), as well as all compared code-based methods.
