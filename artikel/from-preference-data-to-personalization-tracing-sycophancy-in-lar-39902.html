From Preference Data to Personalization: Tracing Sycophancy in Large Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (180 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21650v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Sycophancy in large language models is often measured as agreement with the user, yet agreement can be appropriate when the user's claim is valid, their preference is genuine, or their experience warrants acknowledgment. We study sycophancy as inappropriate agreement: validating a claim, preference, or self-assessment when the context calls for correction, qualification, or resistance. We introduce a validity-aware sycophancy grader and SycoDrift, a persona-targeted evaluation set of 3,298 validity-annotated probes with controlled annotations for user openness and stakes. We use this framework to analyze three sources of agreement pressure: human preference data, automated judge specification, and in-context personalization. First, preference for validating responses in human datasets (Chatbot Arena, HH-RLHF, Community Alignment) is weak and concentrated where agreement is often appropriate. Second, in a selected-value, single-model experiment, emphasized judge rubrics produce consistent but modest increases relative to paired suppressed rubrics, demonstrating a plausible mechanism for training-time amplification. Third, in off-policy evaluations across 14 models, inference-time personalization increases inappropriate agreement, with the largest effect from matched user profiles placed directly before the query.
