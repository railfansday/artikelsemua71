A-Tag: Cryptographic File Tagging with Enforced Live Human Presence

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.23602v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The increasing autonomy of software agents and the proliferation of AI-generated content have weakened the reliability of digital actions as evidence of human involvement. Existing mechanisms, such as digital signatures and authentication protocols, verify identity and integrity, but do not establish whether a live human was present at the time an action was performed. In this paper, we introduce A-Tag, a system for verifiable human-in-the-loop tagging of digital artifacts. A-Tag cryptographically binds a file to a verified instance of live human presence at the moment of tagging. The resulting tag serves as a persistent and transferable proof that the action was triggered by a live human, without making claims about the origin, identity of the actor, or semantic content of the file. A-Tag is a modular framework combining liveness verification based on micro-motion signals shaped by human motor control, a secure cryptographic binding between the verified event and the artifact, and a publicly verifiable logging mechanism enabling independent validation. We formalize security properties of human presence signals, define an adversarial model capturing automated and relay attacks, and analyze the system's guarantees and limitations. We implement A-Tag on commodity devices and evaluate it under realistic conditions. Our evaluation shows that A-Tag provides cryptographically sound artifact binding with deterministic verification, while enabling robust recovery under real-world transformations, achieving over 99 percent success for image-based artifacts, and maintaining sub-second verification and practical tagging latency.
