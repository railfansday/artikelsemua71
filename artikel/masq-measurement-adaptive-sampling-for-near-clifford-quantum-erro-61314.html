MASQ: Measurement-Adaptive Sampling for Near-Clifford Quantum Error Correction Circuits

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (169 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.22232v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Classical sampling of quantum error correction (QEC) circuits supports fault-tolerant protocol evaluation, but estimating rare logical failures requires many independent shots. Frame-active-state simulators isolate non-stabilizer information in a dense active-state vector, yet dense-state processing remains exponential in the active width. This paper presents MASQ, an exact measurement-adaptive sampler for near-Clifford QEC circuits based on selective materialization of non-Clifford rotations. At each measurement boundary, MASQ materializes only rotations in a measurement-relevant closure capturing direct and indirect non-commuting dependencies, deferring the rest to limit active-state expansion. Stabilizer-assisted elimination further removes rotations certified to be invisible to all future measurements. A circuit-dependent schedule is computed once and shared across shots, while adaptive caching of conditional Born probabilities and batched execution reduce repeated sampling work while preserving the measurement-record distribution. Across 19 QEC benchmarks, MASQ reduces peak active width by up to 40 active virtual qubits and achieves up to 8,098-fold CPU throughput speedup over Clifft on multi-round circuits.
