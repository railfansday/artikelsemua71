Breaking: From Data to Valves: A Self-Learning, Production-Grade Edge Stack for Sustainable HVAC Control

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (282 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24052v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Learned HVAC controllers rarely leave the simulator, and those that do are typically trained offline, frozen at deployment and retrained from the cloud. We present a production stack in which three learning components run on one NVIDIA Jetson Orin gateway per building, adapt continuously from the building's own data, and verify their own effect. (a) A compact transformer-encoder forecaster, one per equipment group with learned per-unit embeddings, predicts every air-side unit's next-cycle temperature and learns prequentially, one gradient step per five-minute cycle, behind five independent gates that bound the influence of any corrupt reading. (b) A distributional soft actor-critic agent, one shared policy across unit types, converts the measured state into a chilled-water valve position under a structural safety envelope that no learned component can soften; its second generation learns nightly on the device from recorded transitions alone, with a behaviour anchor and critic calibration terms, is scored by four model-free metrics and promoted by atomic hot-swap. (c) A change-point regression baseline held to ASHRAE Guideline 14 supplies the counterfactual against which savings are certified. On eighteen months of data from a hotel the forecaster converges from random weights within a week and reaches 0.37, 0.39 and 0.11 degree C MAE for air-handling, fresh-air and fan-coil units with no retraining event; on a different hotel the baseline reproduces a 46-day held-out period to within 0.07%. The RL agent has completed its in-simulator campaign and runs in shadow on 104 units; we report measured latency and memory of every service on a shared 15 GB device, the systems lessons of the campaign, and precisely what the evidence does and does not yet establish.
