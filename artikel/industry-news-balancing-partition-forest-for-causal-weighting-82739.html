Industry News: Balancing Partition Forest for Causal Weighting

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (174 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23524v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Balancing weights are used in observational causal inference to construct a population in which the treatment is independent of observed pretreatment covariates. The ideal weight is the ratio between the product of the covariate and treatment marginals and their joint distribution, for binary and continuous treatments alike. We propose Balancing Partition Tree (BPT) and Balancing Partition Forest (BPF), which estimate this ratio on a learned rectangular partition of covariate--treatment space. Each split maximizes the gain in a Bregman divergence computed from joint and marginal counts. Two choices of divergence yield criteria that maximize the mutual information captured by the partition or reduce the squared error of the weights. We show that the population residual of either criterion bounds the bias of inverse-probability-weighted and doubly robust response estimators that use the population partition weights. Across 17 synthetic and semi-synthetic settings and three response estimators, BPF obtains the lowest mean RMSE rank among seven weight estimators, and a single tree fits in 0.14 seconds on 16,512 observations.
