Latest Update: Simulation-Based Evaluation of Residual DDPG-Augmented Computed Torque Control for a Cable-Driven Lower-Limb Rehabilitation Robot Model under Disturbances and Parametric Uncertainties

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (205 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2608.26739v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Nominal computed torque control (CTC) relies on accurate inverse dynamics; parameter mismatch and disturbances can leave tracking error, while joint limits and unilateral cable forces constrain motion. This numerical study evaluates a bounded Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) residual correction around nominal CTC for a planar three-degree-of-freedom, three-cable lower-limb rehabilitation robot model. In four simulation cases, we examine tracking, joint limits, workspace, cable-Jacobian conditioning and inferred cable demands. In a representative combined uncertainty/disturbance case, Cartesian RMS error was 0.0221419 m with CTC and 0.0128462 m with residual DDPG-CTC (approximately 42% lower). On a different circular reference, the residual branch nevertheless increased joint-limit-violating channel samples from 44 to 101. Separate supplemental experiments analyze training variability and checkpoints, alternative compensators, reward/action sensitivity, independently varied parameters and disturbances, trajectory transfer, constrained cable-force allocation and force lag. These studies show both improvements and failures, including early terminations and force-capacity-dependent degradation. The verified original algebraic force-backprojection path and later bounded-force simulations are distinguished; numerical allocations do not establish physical pretension or motor performance. Simscape and MSC ADAMS checks are numerical cross-model consistency assessments. No hardware, hardware-in-the-loop or patient tests were performed, and no clinical efficacy or physical safety guarantee is claimed.
