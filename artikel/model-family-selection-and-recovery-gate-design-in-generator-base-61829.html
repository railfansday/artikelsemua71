Model-family selection and recovery-gate design in generator-based noise tomography

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (270 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.11748v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Family selection in generator-model noise tomography is a hypothesis test whose shot floor has closed form under a purely coherent noise model, $N^{*}=\lambda_{\mathrm{req}}/(r_{\mathrm{b}} S)^{2}$: set by the absolute bias $r_{\mathrm{b}} S$, not the relative residual, and computable from a calibration alone. The same computation prices the recovery gates, fingerprint and Fisher information coming off the same map, so there is no design-independent verdict and no family-independent design optimum. Whether a design can move that price is structural: a bias confined to the block every design measures identically is design-blind, one that reaches the code blocks is repriced by a factor of sixty-eight---from a dominated design to the cheapest, not between two optima---and the two platforms studied here are its extremes. At the reference point the floor is $1.1\times10^{7}$ and $6.7\times10^{6}$ shots per component, the easy corner of the sampled working points, whose median floor is eighty-three times dearer in one direction and less than twice in the other; at the $10^{5}$ shots a run spends, the power equals the level and the residual reported is shot noise. The design problem is conditioning, not rank: across the sixty-four placements the rms varies by factors of $2.7$ and $3.9$, while the optimal gate count is flat at two to four. At five qubits the whole sample moves to $10^{9}$--$2\times10^{10}$ shots, a design spread of twenty-one against nine at three qubits; on a measured three-qubit register the floor is a requirement on systematic control before it is a shot count, and where that control is missing no budget supplies it.
