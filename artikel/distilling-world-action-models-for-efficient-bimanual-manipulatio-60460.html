Distilling World Action Models for Efficient Bimanual Manipulation via Anchored Path Matching

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (229 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22592v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: World Action Models (WAMs) jointly generate future video and robot actions through iterative denoising. Few-step distillation reduces the number of model evaluations, but its coarse updates can move the student away from the teacher's trajectory. We propose Anchored Path Matching (APM), which supervises endpoint and short-transition predictions from states reached by the student. From each state, we continue the teacher to zero noise and use its endpoint and first intermediate state as targets. Modality-routed adapters learn to match both targets from the same starting state. When the two gradients conflict, anchor-priority updates preserve the endpoint gradient and remove the opposing component of the local gradient within each modality. One checkpoint supports 1-, 2-, and 4-step joint generation. The sampling budget can be chosen at deployment without retraining or teacher queries. On RoboTwin 2.0, we train only on Clean demonstrations and evaluate directly on the domain-randomized Random setting. At one step, APM improves closed-loop success over continued base distillation at matched inference budgets by 2.0 percentage points on Clean and 1.4 on Random. Video peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) rises from 30.169 to 32.317 dB, with gains in structural similarity (SSIM) and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS). On two real-robot tasks, APM improves success over the one-step teacher and reduces control-cycle latency by $21.3\times$ relative to the eight-step teacher.Project page: https://jialonglong.github.io/Anchored-Path-Matching/.
