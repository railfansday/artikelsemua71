Bookkeeping Is Not Judgement: EJBench, an Engineering Judgement Benchmark Built from a Conjugate Heat Transfer Design Campaign

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (198 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21707v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We introduce EJBench, a benchmark of engineering judgement: whether a model can predict how a design behaves at newly introduced conditions from a few earlier results, and decide on it. Unlike engineering benchmarks that test whether an agent can operate simulation or CAD software, EJBench is software-agnostic and asks what an engineer would conclude without running a simulation. Its tasks come from a conjugate heat transfer design campaign for battery cold plates, in three families: matching unlabelled test results to their operating points, choosing which stored runs to reveal before signing off a design, and ranking new duties by peak cell temperature. Every answer is taken from a converged simulation, not from an LLM judge. The best of ten frontier models, GPT-6 Astra, scores 0.71, and models state almost the same confidence on tasks they get wrong as on tasks they get right. We also post-train Qwen3.5-9B with LoRA and reinforcement learning, rewarded by the share of answer criteria the simulation results confirm; on the held-out test split its score rises from 0.071 to 0.106. We release the train, validation and test splits, the evaluation harness and the leaderboard.
