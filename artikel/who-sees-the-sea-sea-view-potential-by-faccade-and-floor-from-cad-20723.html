Who sees the sea? Sea-view potential by fa\c{c}ade and floor from cadastral LoD1 models on the Costa del Sol

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (284 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.21672v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Views are among the most valued and least measured attributes of a home. We present an open-data method that estimates the sea-view potential of every free fa\c{c}ade of every residential building at three floors (ground, middle and top; other floors take the nearest of them), from cadastral footprints with floor counts and a national terrain model. Run for 158,060 buildings of 21 municipalities on Spain's Costa del Sol in under an hour on two cores, it finds that 52 % of buildings see some sea from their top floor and 36 % have an open view (20{\deg} or more). Traced over a LiDAR surface in a pilot, scores agree with the model's inside cadastral footprints (96.7 % of viewpoints in the same class at 20{\deg}), but structures the cadastre does not record lower the sea seen from the top floor by 6.1--9.0{\deg} on average. Against the photographs of 93 homes for sale, labelled blind by AI models of two developers with ties decided by the author, the score has an AUC of 0.80 (95 % CI 0.70--0.89; 0.81 against the first-round labels); at 20{\deg} it is sensitive (0.90) but not specific (0.45). Dwelling figures depend on how dwellings are allocated to fa\c{c}ades and floors: 42 % of dwellings are on a floor with open-view potential under the main rule, and 12--45 % across the allocation, geometry and vegetation scenarios tested. As an application, higher-income census sections tend to have more modelled view potential within municipalities, with uncertain size and fragile inference (about two effective clusters); on terrain alone the difference largely disappears. Code and fa\c{c}ade-level data reproduce the main results.
