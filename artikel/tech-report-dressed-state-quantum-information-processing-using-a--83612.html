Tech Report: Dressed-State Quantum Information Processing Using a Multilayer Ion Trap Chip with an Integrated Radio Frequency Resonator

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (283 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.24910v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: We present the experimental preparation of a radio frequency (RF)-dressed-state qubit, the coherent operation of a single RF-dressed qubit, and the selective readout of RF-dressed hyperfine states of $^{171}$Yb${^+}$ ions trapped using a novel microstructured ion trap chip. We demonstrate the individual addressing of RF-dressed-state qubits in a two-ion Coulomb crystal. To this end, we developed, manufactured, and characterized a trap chip specifcally designed for quantum information processing with RF-dressed-state qubits. The chip consists of multiple layers, one of which includes an integrated resonator that creates a gradient of the RF magnetic field amplitude along the axis of a linear ion crystal. This RF gradient enables the individual addressing of dressed qubit states as well as their coupling. We characterize the ion trap chip experimentally using the hyperfine states of the electronic ground state of laser cooled $^{171}$Yb${^+}$ ions and by applying a single, global, modulated RF field oscillating near 12.6 GHz that drives dressed-state resonances. We present a microfabricated multilayer ion trap chip specifically designed for quantum information processing based on dressed-state qubits. This ion trap chip consists of multiple layers, one of which includes an integrated radio frequency (RF) resonator, creating a gradient of the RF magnetic field amplitude that allows for individual qubit addressing and qubit-qubit coupling. We experimentally characterize this ion trap chip demonstrating dressed-qubit state preparation, coherent single dressed-qubit operation, and dressed-state-selective read-out using hyperfine states of the electronic ground state of $^{171}$Yb${^+}$. For this purpose, a global modulated RF driving field oscillating near 12.6 GHz is applied to the ions. In addition, we demonstrate individual addressing of dressed-state qubits in a two-ion Coulomb crystal.
