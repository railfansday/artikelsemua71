No-Go Theorems for Ordered Universal Entanglement Purification

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.24603v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Entanglement purification is a fundamental tool for obtaining high-fidelity entangled states used in various quantum technologies. It aims to increase the fidelity of noisy shared entangled states with a fixed maximally entangled state through LOCC. In distributed quantum systems, such as networks employing quantum repeaters, limited memory, probabilistic entanglement generation, and unequal storage times can leave a finite collection of nonidentical pairs. Their states may remain incompletely characterized even when calibrated preparation and storage records establish their fidelity ordering. Recent work has investigated whether fidelity ordering permits nontrivial universal purification. A nontrivial universal $n$-to-1 protocol must, conditioned on success, never lower the highest input fidelity and strictly increase it for at least one allowed joint input. Although no-go results have been established for a restricted class of LOCC protocols, the corresponding question for general LOCC has remained unresolved. Here, we study ordered universal purification under separable operations. Specifically, for every fixed finite $n\ge2$, we prove that when independently prepared two-qubit pairs range over the full family of states above a fixed fidelity threshold $0\le F_*<1$, any separable success map that never lowers the highest input fidelity on the allowed ordered inputs must preserve that fidelity exactly whenever it succeeds. This excludes nontrivial universal LOCC purification even for arbitrarily high input fidelities, without imposing a positive lower bound on success probability. ...
