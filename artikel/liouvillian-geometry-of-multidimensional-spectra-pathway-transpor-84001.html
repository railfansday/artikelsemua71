Liouvillian Geometry of Multidimensional Spectra: Pathway Transport and Local Response Deformation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (174 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2606.22530v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Liouville pathways provide the conceptual foundation for interpreting multidimensional spectroscopies but, in open quantum systems, need not remain independent under the reduced dynamics. Environmental interactions can transport amplitude between pathway sectors during free evolution, producing measurable changes in the nonlinear response. We develop a differential framework for describing this transport under parametric deformation of the open-system dynamics. When the environment selects a pointer basis distinct from the observational basis, the resulting basis incompatibility mixes Liouville pathways and produces characteristic spectral distortions. Using a Duhamel expansion of the Liouvillian propagator, we derive local response derivatives that reconstruct this pathway redistribution. For a two-level model with a rotated dephasing basis, a second-order expansion about a single reference point retains the dominant spectral structure over a broad range of basis \Rconcept{misalignment, while the global error grows to approximately $37\%$ at maximal misalignment. These results show how local response information captures the spectroscopic consequences of Liouvillian deformation and establish pathway transport as a complementary organizing principle for multidimensional spectroscopy.
