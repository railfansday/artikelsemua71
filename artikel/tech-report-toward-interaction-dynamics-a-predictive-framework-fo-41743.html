Tech Report: Toward Interaction Dynamics: A Predictive Framework for Safe Physical Human Robot Interaction

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (282 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2606.08281v4 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Physical human-robot interaction requires yielding transiently to contact yet recovering the commanded reference under sustained load. Finite-stiffness impedance control retains a static deflection there, while predictive alternatives typically optimize a nonlinear model online. Operational-space cancellation instead exposes a translational error double integrator with a fixed transition matrix and a configuration-scheduled input map, making interaction a predictive quantity. We build on it a compact offset-free interaction-error MPC for torque-controlled manipulators: a force-domain random-walk state estimates persistent interaction and model error, and a 30-variable convex QP maps the correction through the current task inertia while constraining the applied joint torque. Conditional results establish impedance equivalence of the unconstrained passive feedback, offset-free regulation at feasible frozen configurations, and quadratic stabilizability of the scheduled backbone. In a 1 kHz MuJoCo simulation of a 7-DOF Franka FR3, the estimator cuts steady-state error under a repeated 15 N step from 2.73 mm to 0.033 mm when added to the otherwise identical 100 Hz MPC, at unchanged peak joint power. A stiffness-and-damping-calibrated impedance baseline attains 2.63 mm at the same actuator cost, and 1.36 mm with ideal measured-force cancellation, so constant-load rejection is not unique to MPC; the sensorless controller still reaches 0.033 mm. The torque constraint holds the command inside a budget tightened to the lowest the task can meet, at no tracking cost. On a physical UR5e, five alternated pairs under a nominal 1.5 kg hanging load give 1.505 mm without the estimator; with it the controller is offset-free, its 0.023 mm error being the noise level of the encoder-based position measurement. A tuned anti-windup integral also removes the offset, with larger transients.
