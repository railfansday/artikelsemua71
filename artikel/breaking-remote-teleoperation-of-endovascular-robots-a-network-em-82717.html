Breaking: Remote Teleoperation of Endovascular Robots: A Network Emulation Framework for Systematic Performance Evaluation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (214 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24280v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Remote endovascular teleoperation requires network performance to be evaluated in relation to physical navigation and operator experience, while the effects of network degradation on the usability of remote endovascular systems remain insufficiently characterized from the perspective of clinical users. We developed a network emulation and benchmarking framework that combines an endovascular robot with data streaming. We conducted a user study involving eight clinicians from interventional neuroradiology and general interventional radiology and eight engineering students, who performed 480 navigation attempts across 15 network conditions. Communication performance and received video quality were assessed alongside navigation outcomes, confidence, workload and clinician rated acceptability. Under the most impaired combined condition, success was 78.1\%, compared with 93.8\% under the reference condition, and mean attempt time increased from 63.5 to 94.3\,s. At 5\% configured packet loss, 87.5\% of attempts succeeded, although all eight clinicians rated the condition unacceptable. Perceived video quality was associated with clinician confidence and acceptability, with mean within participant Spearman correlations of 0.72 and 0.78, respectively. Framework integration on a second robotic platform additionally supported successful remote navigation over a real network. Our framework provides a reusable basis that can help identify communication bottlenecks and assess their effects on navigation and clinician acceptance for endovascular intervention telesurgery tasks.
