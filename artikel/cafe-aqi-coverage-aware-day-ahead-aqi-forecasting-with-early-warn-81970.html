CAFE-AQI: Coverage-Aware Day-Ahead AQI Forecasting with Early Warning and Conformal Uncertainty in Bangladesh

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (226 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.23815v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Next-day air quality forecasting often assumes city records are equally complete and independent. However, an audit of a large Bangladesh dataset revealed a sharp coverage expansion after August 2022 and indicated that 103 city labels correspond to only 61 unique AQI trajectories. To address these issues, this paper presents CAFE-AQI, a coverage-aware framework for forecasting next-day maximum AQI. It also estimates the probability of Unhealthy-or-worse conditions (AQI >= 151). Data were divided chronologically into training (2022-2023), calibration (2024), and testing (2025). To prevent duplicate records from dominating training and evaluation, identical trajectories were weighted. The framework combines temporal and pollutant features with a five-neighbor spatial context. It also includes a class-balanced warning head and risk-conditional conformal calibration aimed at providing early warnings and prediction intervals. Evaluation on the duplicate-balanced 2025 data gave an MAE of 9.76, an RMSE of 15.04, and an R-squared value of 0.910 for CAFE-AQI. Its MAE was 3.63 AQI lower than persistence (95% CI: 3.49-3.76), with lower errors in each of the 61 trajectory groups. For unhealthy-air warnings, the F1-score was 0.908 and the AUROC was 0.982. The nominal 90% intervals covered 92.09% of observations. When an entire spatial block was excluded from training, the MAE remained 10.66, showing that the lightweight model transferred reasonably well to unseen locations.
