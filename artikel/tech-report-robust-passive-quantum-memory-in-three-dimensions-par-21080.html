Tech Report: Robust Passive Quantum Memory in Three Dimensions, Part I: Thermal and Zero-Temperature Perturbative Stability

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (250 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.21749v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Whether a three-dimensional quantum many-body system with geometrically local, bounded-degree interactions can passively protect encoded quantum information has remained a central open question since Kitaev introduced topological quantum computation. Passive protection requires stability against both thermal noise at nonzero temperature and generic static Hamiltonian perturbations Balasubramanian, Davydova, and Lin (BDL) recently established unperturbed three-dimensional thermal self-correction using a recursively defined family of stabilizer Hamiltonians. However, they left open whether their construction remains stable against quantum perturbations, even at zero temperature. In this paper, we show that a slight variant of the BDL code family achieves stability along both boundary axes of the perturbation-temperature phase diagram as the system size grows. Following BDL, in the absence of perturbation it is thermally stable below a critical temperature, achieving a stretched-exponentially long memory lifetime when coupled to any local Markovian thermal bath generated by single-site system-bath interactions satisfying detailed balance with bounded coupling rates. At zero temperature under arbitrary weak, superpolynomially decaying quantum perturbations, the family maintains a uniform bulk spectral gap, superpolynomially small ground-space splitting, and a superpolynomially long closed-system unitary memory lifetime for quasi-locally dressed logical observables. To prove this zero-temperature stability, we establish local topological quantum order across the multiscale hierarchy using a linearly dilated relaxation of the usual Bravyi-Hastings-Michalakis criteria. In the companion paper, we combine the tools developed here with finite-temperature worldline cluster expansions to establish passive self-correction throughout the phase diagram's positive-temperature perturbed interior.
