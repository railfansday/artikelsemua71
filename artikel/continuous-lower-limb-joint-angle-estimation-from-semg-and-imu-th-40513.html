Continuous Lower-Limb Joint-Angle Estimation from sEMG and IMU: The Conditional Value of Cross-Activity and Cross-Subject Data under Enhancement-Matched Controls

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (247 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.19238v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Intent-driven exoskeleton control needs a continuous, causal estimate of the wearer's joint kinematics and an answer to a second question: whose data should train the estimator for a new user? Borrowing the user's recordings of other activities, or other users' recordings of the same activity, is usually evaluated as "more data" versus "less data," which confounds the information a source carries with the optimizer updates it adds and hides negative transfer behind averages. We separate these effects for hip, knee, and ankle angle estimation from high-density sEMG and an IMU across six locomotion activities on all ten participants of a public dataset. A compact residual causal TCN-GRU decoder is frozen, and an upgrade-matched replay control prices every added source against the same number of updates on the user's own data. A user's own other activities raise $R^2$ from 0.722 to 0.788, of which +0.048 is information beyond replay; stair descent is a reliably harmful donor and sit-stand-walk a reliably beneficial one. Other users' same-activity data help while the user has only the target activity ($G_\mathrm{T} = +0.053$) and half as much once own other-activity data exist ($G_\mathrm{W} = +0.028$); at full calibration, donor value collapses after one to two target records. Per-target gains from the two sources correlate, and leave-one-participant-out gates recover most of the cross-subject value only at low calibration. Data value is hierarchical, state-dependent, and directional; the bottleneck is deciding, without test labels, when to transfer.
