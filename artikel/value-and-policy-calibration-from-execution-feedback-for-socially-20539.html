Value and Policy Calibration from Execution Feedback for Socially Compatible Driving

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (157 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.19969v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Autonomous vehicle (AV) decisions at intersections affect the delay, braking, and motion smoothness of surrounding human-driven vehicles (HDVs). Learning socially compatible behavior requires connecting imagined interaction outcomes with feedback from executed actions. We propose a world-model reinforcement learning framework combines imagined policy improvement with direct value and policy calibration from finalized interactions, balancing AV progress with HDV interaction burden. An interaction-aware latent model supports imagination, while off-policy correction and replay updates enable observed rewards and transitions to supervise both the critic and actor. Simulation experiments at an unsignalized intersection achieve a 100% success and reductions in following-HDV acceleration variation of 65.5% and 44.0% relative to PIWM and DreamerV3, respectively. Ablations identify complementary roles for value calibration in reducing HDV burden and policy calibration in improving passage efficiency. Tests under out-of-distribution traffic shifts further show lower ego jerk and HDV acceleration variation than PIWM at comparable passage times.
