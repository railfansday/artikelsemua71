Tech Report: MorphQuad: Morphable Quadrotor for Superhuman Maneuverability, Manipulation, and Resiliency

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (251 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2607.02764v5 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Infrastructure maintenance, contact-based inspection, and emergency response can benefit from aerial vehicles that act as a flying human hand with extreme maneuverability, manipulation, and resiliency (MMR): maneuverability to fly in arbitrary orientations; manipulation to point sensors, turn valves, and press tools at arbitrary orientations; resiliency to maintain motion accuracy and force application despite disturbances from arbitrary directions, such as wind, ground effects, and friction. Realizing MMR requires more than omnidirectional flight; it also requires (I) vectoring of maximum thrust in any direction, (II) almost-everywhere stability, accounting for kinematic singularities, control singularities, and inter-rotor downwash interference, and (III) miniaturizable structural designs. Existing aerial vehicles satisfy a subset of I--III. We present MorphQuad, a morphable quadrotor that delivers MMR by realizing I--III on a quadrotor structure with independently articulated rotor systems through novel control and thrust allocation algorithms. On hardware, MorphQuad preserves a planar quadrotor structure, and independently articulates each of the four rotor systems, eliminating control singularities and enabling all rotors to align and vector the platform's maximum thrust in any direction; on control and thrust allocation, MorphQuad enjoys a closed-form null-space correction for gimbal lock and downwash interference that maintains almost-everywhere stability via coupling with a generalized geometric controller. With fully-onboard computation, localization, and trajectory execution, MorphQuad demonstrates continuous multi-revolution rotation while translating or hovering (maneuverability); valve turning, perching, and object pressing and pushing with human-wrist strength (manipulation); and push-pull recovery and wind rejection, even when directed to a single rotor (resiliency).
