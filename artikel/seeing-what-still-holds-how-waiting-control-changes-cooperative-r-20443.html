Seeing What Still Holds: How Waiting Control Changes Cooperative Recovery

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (196 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22368v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Changing the holding reference during a fixed-duration wait can reverse the ranking of visual recovery methods. We introduce AuditEmbodied, a paired execution and trajectory evaluation framework that independently varies holding references and waiting duration while keeping recovery executors frozen. A 960-execution factorial on 20 initialization seeds compares CAD-feedback and landmark-based recovery from identical post-wait physical states within each holding and waiting condition. Protected completion requires successful recovery under the declared execution constraints. At a 1 s wait with registered wrist references, their protected completion counts change from 6/40 versus 19/40 under registered holding to 34/40 versus 22/40 under position-only holding. Trajectory analysis localizes the main separation to grasp formation: small observation-time target residuals precede large tracking errors. Joint diagnostics and controlled interventions characterize persistent tracking constraints. On fresh seeds, gradual position reanchoring improves CAD recovery's long-wait completion by 42.5 percentage points and reduces mean complete recovery time by 6.06 s relative to registered holding. Additional evaluations with pretrained policies, handover, and a mass-matched geometry characterize holding-reference sensitivity across executors and physical regimes. These findings establish holding reference as an explicit evaluation condition for comparing visual recovery performance.
