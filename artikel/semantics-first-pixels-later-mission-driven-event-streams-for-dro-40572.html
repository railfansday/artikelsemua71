Semantics First, Pixels Later: Mission-Driven Event Streams for Drone Video over Bandwidth-Starved Links

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (337 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.19463v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: A surveillance drone on a bandwidth-starved link cannot send the video its operator would need, yet the operator's questions are often not about pixels: did three people converge, when, and where? We study what should cross a link of 1 Mbps down to 1 kbps instead, and in what order. BPS, a behavior-primitive stream, sends semantics first and pixels later: it runs a detector and a tracker onboard and sends short records (25 bytes on average) of multi-frame events selected by a mission card of 16-125 bytes, ahead of positions and pictures. We measure it by what a ground-side language model answers from it, under a preregistered protocol with a held-out set, against H.265 read by the vision fine-tune of the same model, H.265 on which the ground runs our own perception, a stream of quantized tracks, and onboard captions. On the development set (84% of questions about convergence, mostly one location) the event stream answers 74.3% of questions at 0.13 kbps. A reader that receives nothing answers 57.4%, and the video reader 59.6% at 100 kbps and 64.7% on the source frames. When the ground instead runs our perception on the video, video matches the stream at 100 kbps, about 800 times the stream's rate, and falls behind at 64 kbps and below. A track stream matches it at 96 times the bytes and loses 0.03 at 17 times. On held-out data the result is weaker: with events taken from annotated tracks, an oracle, the stream beats H.265 at 100 kbps (+0.13), but with onboard perception it does not (+0.01 [-0.04, +0.06]) and is no better than receiving nothing. Sent alone through emulated links the event records answer 0.739 at 1 Mbps and still 0.724 at 0.3 kbps; an every-frame track stream holds down to 1 kbps and video with ground-side perception down to about 100 kbps. On a Jetson Orin Nano the unchanged pipeline runs at 4.5 fps and misses most events.
