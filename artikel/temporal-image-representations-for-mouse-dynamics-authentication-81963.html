Temporal Image Representations for Mouse Dynamics Authentication

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (230 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23859v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Mouse dynamics authentication has traditionally relied on handcrafted features or time-series representations that capture both spatial and temporal characteristics of mouse movements. More recent approaches have modeled mouse data as images, enabling the use of powerful pretrained computer vision models. However, prior work primarily focuses on spatial-only representations where the cursor coordinates are plotted as a trajectory image. These representations discard the temporal characteristics which can help discriminate people. In this work, we propose image representations that incorporate speed and velocity information for mouse dynamics authentication. We investigate two mouse data representations, a XYPlot representation that intuitively shows the path of movement, and a Pairwise Distance (PWD) representation that utilizes the entire image canvas to encode relationships between mouse events. Both representations are evaluated under both location-aware and location-free settings using pretrained 2D-CNN and ViT architectures. We found that the incorporation of temporal information improved performance over spatial representations alone, and that the location-aware variants generally outperformed the location-free. We achieve state-of-the-art performance on two datasets, Balabit and TWOS, and extend our evaluation to two other datasets. We found that in general, location-aware PWD with temporal encoding combined with the 2D-CNN architecture provided the best results, with mean EERs of 5.21% and 12.70% on the Balabit Test-1 and Test-2 sets, respectively, 4.94% on TWOS, 6.16% on ChaoShen, and 0.59% on DFL.
