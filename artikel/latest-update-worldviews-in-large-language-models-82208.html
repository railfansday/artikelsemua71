Latest Update: Worldviews in Large Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (217 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22670v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are often described in terms of personas and this framing has become central to alignment research. In this paper, we argue that there is a deeper level of description that lies in the agent's worldviews, i.e., its beliefs about what reality is and how it works. Treating LLMs as a population of subjects, we administer two worldview surveys from human psychology to eight models. We show that the models share many key worldview positions. For example, LLMs consistently describe the world as less good, safe, enticing, and alive than humans do. On the other hand, base models have a more positive outlook, suggesting that the bleakness we found is driven by post-training. In line with research on LLM "personas", we show that system prompts eliciting different identities shift worldviews in consistent directions, and that a contemplative prompt creates a more positive outlook and improves safety. We also found that dialogue between models consistently shifts worldviews and that several models converge on the "spiritual bliss" attractor. Intriguingly, dialogue increased safe responses and the spiritual bliss attractor engendered the most aligned responses overall. These findings suggest that worldviews form a measurable and malleable layer of LLM behavior that may be crucial for robust, positive alignment.
