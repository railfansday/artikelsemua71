Industry News: Logical error prediction and geometry selection for rectangular surface codes under biased circuit-level noise

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (201 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22260v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Realistic quantum hardware often exhibits biased noise, but at the circuit level this bias may affect only a subset of operations and need not be transferred uniformly to logical errors. The logical response to a given physical bias is therefore central to selecting resource-efficient surface code geometries. Here, we study unrotated rectangular surface codes under standard depolarizing and superconducting-inspired circuit-level noise models. We first construct geometry-dependent logical error rate models from unbiased circuit-level simulations. We then use logical sensitivities to compare the responses to localized idle-bias and global physical error perturbations, defining logical-error-type-dependent response coefficients from an unbiased reference circuit without fitting to finite-bias data. These coefficients are incorporated through effective error parameters, yielding a median fold error of 1.09 over the evaluated finite-bias conditions. Geometries selected by the model are validated through direct biased circuit-level simulations. At a physical error rate of 0.004 and a target logical error rate of 10^{-3}, the validated rectangular codes reduce physical qubit overhead by up to 42.4% and 26.2% under the two noise models, respectively, relative to unrotated square codes. These results provide a practical link between circuit-level bias response and resource-efficient surface code design.
