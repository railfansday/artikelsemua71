Look Closer, Look Back, or Wait for Agentic Streaming Video Understanding

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (223 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24267v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Streaming video models must answer questions about a live video under strict memory and latency limits. Feeding only the latest few frames keeps present perception sharp, whereas adding past frames dilutes attention on the current scene and triggers hallucinations. Recent cascade methods retrieve past frames when uncertain, but they still fail when current frames are blurry or when decisive future clues have not appeared yet. We introduce ActiveForager, a streaming video agent that maintains a clean short working window and gathers extra evidence only on demand. For each query, the agent either answers directly, zooms into high-resolution recent frames, recalls past events from memory, or waits for upcoming frames. When recalling history, a shared-weight sub-agent verifies candidate clips in isolation and returns a compact evidence summary. A stream-decoupled attention mask then keeps historical evidence from interfering with present visual tokens. We train this policy using counterfactual information gain, which rewards a tool call only when the gathered evidence makes the true answer more likely and prevents tool collapse across all actions. Across Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B on OVO-Bench, ActiveForager improves real-time perception, historical recall, and proactive response at once, lifting overall accuracy on Qwen3-VL-8B from 47.6% to 65.9%. It also doubles zero-shot accuracy on the multi-hop Video-Holmes benchmark while adding minimal visual tokens per query.
