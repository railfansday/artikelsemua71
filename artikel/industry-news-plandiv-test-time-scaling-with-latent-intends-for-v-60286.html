Industry News: PlanDiv: Test-Time Scaling with Latent Intends for Vision-Language-Action Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (259 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.20010v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Test-time scaling improves vision-language-action (VLA) policies by drawing several action candidates and executing the best one. We find that its returns diminish quickly when the candidates are resampled from one decoder: they differ by noise rather than by plan, and for each of three resampling sources, going from four to eight candidates adds only 0.06-0.07 to the average number of completed tasks on CALVIN. We introduce PlanDiv, an action head that makes the candidates differ by plan: a lightweight planner proposes K latent plan hypotheses, a shared masked-diffusion executor decodes one action chunk per plan from a single backbone pass, and the policy executes the chunk that its own likelihood ranks highest. The prepares are learned from demonstrations alone, without plan labels or rewards. With the backbone, data and training recipe held fixed, eight plan-conditioned candidates raise five-task chain success on CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D from 56.9% to 69.9%, while eight candidates from autoregressive, plan-free or same-plan resampling reach at most 64.9%. The same-plan baseline is PlanDiv's own checkpoint with the plan held fixed, so this gap comes from the plans, not from drawing more samples. On LIBERO-Long, four plans remove 40% of single-candidate failures, against at most 21% for matched resampling. On a real Franka arm the gain appears where a task admits more than one solution: PlanDiv is on par with a single-output OpenVLA-OFT+ baseline on a one-solution task and succeeds in 51 of 54 trials against 40 of 54 on two tasks with two solutions.
