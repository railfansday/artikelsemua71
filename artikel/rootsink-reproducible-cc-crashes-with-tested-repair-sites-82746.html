RootSink: Reproducible C/C++ Crashes with Tested Repair Sites

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (194 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.23336v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: When a C or C++ program crashes on a crafted input, the line that crashes is often not the line that is wrong. Tools and models that repair or locate vulnerabilities need examples showing both, with evidence for each, which existing datasets rarely provide. RootSink provides 3341 records from 354 open-source projects. Most give the crash site a memory-error detector reported and a candidate location for its root cause, and label each with a weakness type and its evidence. For the 1954 records in the Reproduced tier, we re-ran the crash ourselves and confirmed that the fixed version runs cleanly. In 1275 of these, a single hunk of the developer's fix stopped the crash on its own. That marks a tested repair site, though not necessarily the defect, and 1273 records use it as the root. In a blind audit of an earlier version, two co-author raters called 68% and 92% of the tested repair sites they saw the defect, but only 37% and 51% of that version's located Reproduced-tier roots. RootSink ships with tasks, a patch scorer and run logs.
