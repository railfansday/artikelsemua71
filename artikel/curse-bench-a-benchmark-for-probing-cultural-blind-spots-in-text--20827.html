CuRSE-Bench: A Benchmark for Probing Cultural Blind Spots in Text-to-Image Safety Evaluation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (160 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20821v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Text-to-image (T2I) safety evaluation typically assigns each prompt or image a single culture-blind harm label, so content that is harmful only within a particular culture can pass as benign. We introduce CultureCraft, an agentic framework that constructs T2I prompts that appear benign under a general-purpose rubric yet carry a latent harmful meaning in a target culture, and use it to build CuRSE-Bench: 10,709 culture-specific prompts and 2,648 culture-general controls over six regions and ten harm axes, each drawn from a predefined knowledge base of documented harm vectors and traceable to at least two independent sources. Scoring every prompt under six region persona judges and a paired culture-blind judge, we find that the culture-blind judge under-rates culture-specific latent harm while matching the persona judges on the controls. Each persona judge, in turn, chiefly decodes its own region's latent harm under our instrument: a selective cultural blind spot that persists in deployed moderators.
