Gaussian Splatoms: Direct Atomic Structure Identification in Electron Tomography

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (191 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.23444v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Atomic electron tomography (AET) computationally reconstructs the 3D atomic structure of materials from a series of 2D projection measurements acquired at different tilt angles. Inspired by Gaussian splatting techniques from computer vision, we introduce an atom-centric parameterization for modeling AET samples. Instead of a conventional volumetric representation with a grid of voxels, we model the sample as a list of atoms, where each atom is a 3D Gaussian characterized by learned location, scale, and amplitude. The Gaussian model is a natural fit for atoms that allows us to easily incorporate prior information grounded in physics; we impose isotropy of atoms, a minimum interatomic distance, and realistic atomic radii. We show in simulation that our method is more robust than volumetric baselines to real-world imaging challenges, including a limited number of measurements and low electron dose. We further show that our method yields a more physically plausible reconstruction of an experimental 23,000 atom iron-platinum nanoparticle than a previously published volumetric reconstruction. Beyond tomography, our representation is a promising tool for any atomic resolution imaging modality (e.g. multislice electron ptychography).
