Tech Report: Cache or Denoise Less? Residual Reuse in a Flow-Matching World Action Model

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (250 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.19709v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Flow-matching robot policies generate each action chunk through repeated network evaluations, one per denoising step. Cross-cycle caches such as C$^3$ache reuse computation from earlier replanning calls, but whether they beat simply taking fewer steps is unclear. We compare the two in Fast-WAM, a 6B-parameter world action model, on LIBERO-Goal. Two uncached steps keep the success of ten: on all 500 initial states they are non-inferior within two percentage points, for one checkpoint and noise seed. Under PyTorch's default compile mode, they also cut a replanning call on an RTX A6000 from 202 to 92 ms. This leaves little room for caching. A cache that keeps two fresh steps cannot be faster than two uncached steps: every call still encodes the images, runs the video expert and computes those two steps. Caching the first of two steps, refreshed every fourth call, saves at most 11--14% of a call by our cost model. Cached policies are also fragile. Their losses tracked the share of the flow path the cached steps cover: under Fast-WAM's current time shift, C$^3$ache's eight cached steps cover 80% of the path instead of 44%, and the cached policy succeeds on 63% of states against 95% uncached. Resampling the noise on every call cost caches covering 60% or more of the path 14--40 points. These results argue for fewer-step baselines and for testing reuse under the deployed sampler and noise settings. We release our evaluation harness, logs and a checklist.
