4D-TEMPO: Differentiable 4D-STEM ptychography via fast Physics Optimizers

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (282 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.24776v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Electron ptychography recovers atomic structure from 4D-STEM data: diffraction patterns at multiple positions of a scanned probe. Current codes reconstruct an image and read atoms off it, or fit atoms under an $L_1$ loss; neither gives per-atom uncertainty. We propose 4D-TEMPO, a differentiable physical renderer that fits the atoms, the microscope, and the detector directly to the recorded electron counts under a Poisson likelihood. The output is a list of atoms, each a Gaussian primitive with a 3D position, a thermal width, and a model-conditional error bar from the Fisher information. The Fisher information also conditions the solver, gives the dose (electrons per {\AA}$^2$) each parameter needs, and separates calibration from imaging. On a simulated WSe$_2$ bilayer, starting from the published reconstruction, fitted atoms cut in-plane error by a third (3.2 vs. 4.8 pm) and depth error by 40-61% against its 3D peaks. Depth comes from multiple scattering into the dark field, the faint counts outside the direct beam. The two layers, $10\times$ closer than the depth of field, separate; per-atom depth then follows the layer geometry. When the model is well specified, the bars are calibrated (69% of position coordinates within $1\sigma$). On experimental WSe$_2$, fitted atoms predict held-out patterns better than the three published codes we test. With free probe modes where atoms are incomplete, the same likelihood predicts best on all three held-out benchmarks, matches every WSe$_2$ baseline in 5 min against up to 20.5 h, and passes torchslice at its released setting 180$\times$ sooner on PrScO$_3$. Per-atom error bars, planned doses, and fast reconstruction point to low-dose, real-time 3D imaging of beam-sensitive and dynamic materials.
