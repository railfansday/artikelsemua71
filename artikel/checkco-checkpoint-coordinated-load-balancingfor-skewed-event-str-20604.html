CheckCo: Checkpoint-Coordinated Load Balancingfor Skewed Event Streams at Scale

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (202 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22476v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Event streams are rarely uniform: a few dominant keys can generate orders of magnitude more events than the rest, overloading the workers that receive them and capping throughput. We present CheckCo, a checkpoint-coordinated load-balancing algorithm built on a dynamic oracle that estimates per-key frequencies from the live stream, with no prior statistics. Workers count events per key locally; at each checkpoint barrier, the counts are merged into a partitioning function that divides each key into a number of splits roughly proportional to its estimated frequency and packs the splits onto workers, and this function routes the next checkpoint window. We derive approximation bounds on the resulting load imbalance from classical scheduling results and bound its total key fan-out. On a production pipeline processing over 100 billion events per day, the most loaded worker receives only 9.8% more events than the average over a day (imbalance ratio 1.10), against 8.03 for key-based routing in simulation; in simulation, CheckCo also needs less than half the fan-out of earlier partitioners that reach comparable balance. Shifts in the key distribution are reflected in routing from the next checkpoint on, and the load stays balanced through job restarts.
