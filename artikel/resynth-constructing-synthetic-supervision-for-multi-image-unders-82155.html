ReSynth: Constructing Synthetic Supervision for Multi-Image Understanding

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (211 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23996v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: People often understand the world by comparing different views of it. Vision-language models (VLMs), however, receive these views as separate images and still struggle to relate them. One problem is data. Training on these tasks requires groups of images with a known relationship: the same object from different viewpoints, or the same scene before and after a change. Such examples are hard to collect at scale from existing photo collections. We introduce ReSynth, which turns single-image perception and controllable image generation into cross-image relational supervision. Localized editing produces before-and-after pairs, viewpoint generation produces multiple views of the same instance, and verified scene contents support comparisons of counts and attributes. ReSynth combines Qwen3-VL-8B's captioning, grounding, and counting capabilities with Qwen-Image-2.1's generation and editing capabilities. It verifies single-image facts such as object counts and attributes, then combines these facts to construct multi-image QA. Using GRPO on approximately 440K ReSynth questions, with no real images during training, Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B improve by 17.73 and 6.10 average points on BLINK, MUIRBench, and VLM2-Bench, while single-image performance is broadly preserved on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and improves across all four evaluated benchmarks on Qwen3-VL-8B. Notably, this supervision enables the 8B perception model itself to outperform Qwen3-VL-235B on BLINK and MUIRBench.
