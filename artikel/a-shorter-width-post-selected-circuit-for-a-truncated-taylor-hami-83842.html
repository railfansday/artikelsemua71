A shorter-width post-selected circuit for a truncated-Taylor Hamiltonian-evolution segment

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (155 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2511.09461v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We present an ancilla-efficient, post-selected circuit construction for a truncated-Taylor approximation to short-time Hamiltonian evolution. The construction combines a binary Taylor-order register with mid-circuit measurement, reset, and reuse of the Hamiltonian-index register. Conditioned on the prescribed all-zero outcomes, the circuit implements the truncated Taylor approximation while maintaining coherence among the Taylor-order branches while permitting early termination after a failed post-selection. For Taylor cutoff $K$, a Hamiltonian containing $L$ terms, and an $n$-qubit system, the circuit uses $\left\lceil\log {(K+1)}\right\rceil +\left\lceil\log L\right\rceil+n $ qubits, and executes $\mathcal{O}(K)$ controlled Hamiltonian blocks in a complete segment. We analyze its post-selection probability and expected per-segment cost, and implement and evaluate the construction in Guppy[1,2]. The present work focuses on the low-width implementation of individual short-time propagation segments; coherent composition into a deterministic long-time simulation requires an additional amplification or compression mechanism and is left for future work.
