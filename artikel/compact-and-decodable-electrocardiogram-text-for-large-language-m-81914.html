Compact and Decodable Electrocardiogram Text for Large Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (258 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.22827v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: When a twelve-lead electrocardiogram is written as text for a large language model, the number of tokens has to be balanced against the information lost through compression. This paper presents a deterministic encoding that writes eight independent leads as amplitude letters and exact time gaps and recovers the remaining four limb leads algebraically. The key points are selected with a cardiac-cycle-dependent tolerance, the amplitudes are parameterized by a 26-letter logarithmic codebook learned from a training set, and a 10-step tolerance schedule keeps all records within a fixed token budget. The encoding was applied to 21,388 records from the public PTB-XL database and evaluated using an exact decode-and-re-encode round trip, waveform reconstruction error, and a paired comparison of convolutional classifiers trained with 5 different seeds on signals from the original and decoded databases. All records were contained in 4,080 tokens (average: 2,866 tokens, 64.3% fewer than a per-sample transcription), and all stored lead strings were exactly reproduced. The average correlation between the decoded and reference signals was 0.982. The macro-averaged area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was 0.9167 for the reference signals and 0.9157 for the decoded signals (paired difference -0.0010; 95% patient-level bootstrap interval, -0.0028 to 0.0007), within the pre-specified non-inferiority margin of -0.010; this is not a confirmatory result, because the test fold had been used during encoder development. The diagnostic content of a compact text can therefore be preserved, while the language model's ability to read the text is yet another matter.
