Latest Update: Sensor Monitoring Towards Industry 4.0: Bridging Concepts on the $C_n$ Multivariate Control Chart

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (225 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23820v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The main challenge in monitoring multiple sensors is the high dimensionality that a process can easily reach. From a decision-making perspective, converting these multivariate dynamics into an informative visualization is also not trivial. Statistical Process Control (SPC) techniques detect changes in quality characteristics, but they are difficult to apply beyond the normality (Gaussian) assumption. For instance, the most well-known SPC multivariate control chart is based on Hotelling's $T^2$ statistic, which assumes that the process data follow a multivariate normal distribution (which rarely occurs in practice) and that the observations are serially independent. This study focuses on the multivariate case (dimension $d \geq 2$), regardless of the data dependence or marginal asymmetry. We propose a new control chart for change detection in copula-based multivariate processes, called the $C_n$-chart, which is based on a density level-set tolerance region built from a copula function, and we derive its run-length properties. The methodology also quantifies the (not necessarily linear) dependence among the variables. Two case studies are presented, one on water quality control and the other on monitoring brass-steel thermostats, complemented by a simulation study of run-length performance and computational cost. The results show that the proposed methodology captures both the association among variables and the anomalies, and that it accommodates variable selection through conditional (regression) modeling of the margins.
