Slice Isolation-as-a-Service: An Isolation Mechanism to Proactively Defend Cross-Slice Attacks in 5G-Advanced and Beyond

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (213 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23826v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: As cellular networks transition toward 5G-Advanced and Sixth Generation (6G) architectures, Network Slicing (NS) remains essential for multiplexing heterogeneous services over shared physical infrastructure. However, deploying critical Ultra-Reliable Low Latency Communications (URLLC) alongside standard broadband introduces severe cross-slice vulnerabilities. The implicit assumption that logical slicing inherently guarantees security fails against data plane exploits, as shared User Plane Functions (UPFs) remain susceptible to resource starvation and protocol tunneling. To address this, we propose the Slice Isolation-as-a-Service framework, which treats topological data plane isolation as an on-demand, monetizable capability. We formulate the multi-UPF resource allocation problem as a Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) model to strictly enforce physical and virtual isolation boundaries based on tenant security tiers, minimizing provisioning costs and end-to-end latency. To validate the framework, we engineered a novel containerized Fifth Generation (5G) testbed integrating Free5GC with Nokia Containerlab. Empirical evaluations under active volumetric flooding and the recently discovered GTP-in-GTP reflection attacks reveal that shared and Virtualized Network Function (VNF)-only isolation fail catastrophically, suffering up to 30% packet loss and complete bandwidth starvation. Our results prove that MINLP-driven strict physical isolation successfully neutralizes cross-slice injection and preserves 99.999% reliability for mission-critical slices, confirming our isolation framework as a crucial proactive defense for next-generation core networks.
