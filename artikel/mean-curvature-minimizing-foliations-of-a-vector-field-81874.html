Mean Curvature Minimizing Foliations Of A Vector Field

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (229 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.23765v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Streamline patterns are ubiquitous in nature. Prominent examples include white matter fibre tracts in the mammalian brain and arrangements of elongated cells, such as cardiomyocytes in the heart wall. It has been posited that such organisations foliate into locally smooth surfaces to optimise function. However, the recovery of these foliations from a single vector field aligned with the local orientation of the underlying sheets remains an open problem. We address this challenge by introducing a mathematical model of a sheet and developing an algorithm for sheet inference, from measurements of the oriented structures. Given an input vector field, our model estimates a second vector field that is orthonormal to it, while also requiring the Lie bracket of the two to vanish. To balance sheet likelihood against low surface area, we also reduce the mean curvature of the recovered local surface. We compute the first variation of an associated energy and provide a numerical method which admits an efficient, parallel implementation for sheet estimation. We demonstrate our approach on both synthetic and biological examples, motivated by applications to medical imaging. These include white matter fibre tracts from a brain atlas and confocal microscopy images of cleared heart wall tissue. For the latter we are able to reconstruct sheets aligned with local cardiomyocyte directions at unprecedented (micrometre) scale.
