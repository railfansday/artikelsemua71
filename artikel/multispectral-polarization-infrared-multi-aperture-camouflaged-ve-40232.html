Multispectral-Polarization-Infrared Multi-Aperture Camouflaged Vehicle Detection

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (163 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.22246v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Camouflaged vehicle detection is challenging because camouflage suppresses discriminative cues across sensing modalities. Existing multimodal methods often require precise pixel-level registration and feature-level fusion, creating a trade-off between accuracy and latency. We propose a spectral-polarization-infrared tri-modal detection framework co-designed with a multi-aperture imaging setup to exploit complementary imaging physics. A consensus-guided decision-level fusion strategy combines cross-modal detections within bounded registration tolerances, reducing dependence on precise alignment while maintaining accuracy and real-time performance. We construct a tri-modal camouflaged vehicle dataset containing infrared, polarization, and spectral data. Experiments show that the proposed method achieves 82.7% mean Average Precision (mAP) with an end-to-end latency of 16 ms, outperforming representative feature-level fusion approaches. On the public MID-1K dataset, the framework generalizes to RGB-Depth-Infrared modalities without architectural modification and improves mAP@0.5 by 5.7% over state-of-the-art methods. These results demonstrate the potential of decision-level consensus fusion and multi-aperture sensing for robust and deployable camouflaged vehicle detection.
