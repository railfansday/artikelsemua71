Noise redistribution by stabilizer codes: an organizing view of concatenated three-qubit codes

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (264 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2610.31007v4 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We formulate stabilizer-code error correction as a code- and recovery-dependent transformation of the noise channel. For a fixed recovery map, syndrome projection produces a logical channel that is block diagonal with respect to the syndrome partition, whose diagonal Pauli weights are fixed by the code-induced error classes, with no physical Pauli twirl required. Concatenation is then iterated redistribution, with the dominant logical error guiding the next code. The bit-flip and phase-flip three-qubit codes with standard recoveries supply a closed-form benchmark, and logically inequivalent recoveries appear as a design degree of freedom for shaping the channel presented to the next decoder. For amplitude damping, the rules select $C_2(\hat\sigma_y)C_1(\hat\sigma_x)$ within this family: one level converts the damping into a nearly pure logical-$X$ channel at the price $f_1=1-3\gamma/4$, the second level suppresses that channel to $f_2=1-\frac94\gamma^2$, and the concatenated protocol overtakes the unencoded channel below $\gamma_c=0.2905$ ($f_c=0.8485$) under ideal encoding and recovery; with a per-gate error rate $\varepsilon$ the crossover survives only for $\varepsilon$ below about $5\times10^{-4}$, and then only inside a window of $\gamma$ that contracts as $\varepsilon$ grows. Across the $n$-qubit repetition family with odd $n$, the suppression order rises as $\gamma^{w+1}$ with $w=(n-1)/2$, while the gate budget grows as $n^L$. Correlated noise requires joint class statistics, whereas coherent noise retains intra-class coherences that make the same transformation usable in reverse as a tomographic fingerprint, whose local identifiability is established in a companion paper. The construction remains an analytic benchmark, not a universal prescription for an inner code beneath a surface code.
