Complex quasiprobabilities as measurable temporal interference

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (189 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.22643v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Quantum dynamics can encode interference information that conventional correlation functions leave unresolved. Here we show that the imaginary component of the extended Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobability reveals dynamical distinctions absent from out-of-time-order correlators (OTOCs) formed from two repeated Hermitian observables at infinite temperature. We interpret its real and imaginary components as complementary quadratures of interference between many-body echo amplitudes. We prove that the imaginary component vanishes for every unitary evolution if either projective measurement has two outcomes, whereas nonreal values arise for generic unitary dynamics when it has at least three (e.g., spin-1 systems). We develop an algorithm to reconstruct the full quasiprobability from covariances of local probe signals over repeated preparations of randomised initial states, using forward evolution and probe readout only. In a spin-1 chain, a nonzero imaginary signal distinguishes a real Hamiltonian $H$ from $-H$ despite identical corresponding OTOCs, and identifies opposite sides of a pair-conversion resonance at short times while dipolar and quadrupolar OTOCs remain nearly unchanged. These results show how temporal interference resolves dynamical ambiguities and provides additional constraints for Hamiltonian inference.
