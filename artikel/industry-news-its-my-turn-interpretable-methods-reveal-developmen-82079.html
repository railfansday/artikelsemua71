Industry News: It's My Turn! Interpretable Methods Reveal Developmental Differences from Mother-Child Conversational Dynamics

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (228 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.21969v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Understanding conversational interactions between mothers and children is an important challenge for computational behavioral modeling. Existing methods use traditional statistical approaches and subjective measures of conversational dynamics, without incorporating temporal or probabilistic modeling. We developed an interpretable computational framework that combines sequence analysis, information-theoretic measures, and Bayesian hierarchical modeling to characterize temporal conversational dynamics in parent-child play. We applied this framework to interactions from 94 mother-child dyads with two-year-old children. Sequence analysis revealed that dyads with lower language development (as defined by scores on the Bayley developmental test) exhibited more heterogeneous conversational organization and fewer serve-and-return exchanges than higher-development dyads, a reciprocal turn-taking pattern in which one partner initiates communication and the other responds. Mutual information analyses suggested differences in conversational predictability between lower- and higher-development dyads, although the bootstrap confidence intervals overlapped. Bayesian hierarchical models estimated temporal trajectories for maternal consecutive turns, child responses following maternal speech, and overlapping responses; HDIs indicated that trajectories differed in whether they excluded 0, but there were no statistically credible differences between the Bayley groups. Overall, these results indicate that children with higher developmental scores have more organized and predictable communication styles, with more ``serve-and-return" patterns with their mothers. These results demonstrate that interpretable computational representations of conversational dynamics capture developmental differences beyond simple measures of speech quantity.
