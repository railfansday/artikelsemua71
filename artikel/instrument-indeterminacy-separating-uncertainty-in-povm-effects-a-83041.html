Instrument indeterminacy: separating uncertainty in POVM effects and its neutrosophic reading

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (215 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23566v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Neutrosophic annotation frameworks for quantum measurement attach an external triple $(t,i,f)$ to each Born probability, and explicitly leave open how indeterminacy could be defined operationally. We show that indeterminacy cannot be recovered from outcome probabilities, but can be computed from the pair (state, effect). For a binary POVM $\{A, I-A\}$ we define the instrument indeterminacy $\mathcal{I}_\rho(A) = 4 \mathrm{Tr}(\rho(A-A^2))$, which vanishes exactly when the state only sees the sharp part of the effect. The normalized Gini index of the outcome splits as $4TF = \mathcal{I}_\rho(A) + 4 \mathrm{Var}_\rho(A)$, a law of total variance that separates the instrument contribution from the state contribution; the identity is a special case of Massar's POVM uncertainty operator, and our contribution is its neutrosophic reading. Moving the system-apparatus cut to a Naimark dilation transfers exactly $\mathcal{I}_\rho(A)$ from indeterminacy to variance, which yields the missing operational definition. We extend the decomposition to $m$-outcome POVMs, relate the minimum indeterminacy of two incompatible unbiased qubit observables to their commutator through Busch's joint-measurability criterion, and show that $\mathcal{I}_\rho(A)$ equals twice the disagreement probability of a repeated L\"uders measurement. The resulting estimator needs neither the state nor the effect and, in simulation, requires 2.5 to 6 times fewer shots than detector tomography for the same error.
