ExiST: Existence-Aware Self-Training for Semi-Supervised Multi-Organ Segmentation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.23581v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Semi-supervised multi-organ segmentation should learn more than a dozen organs from a handful of labeled scans, and the smallest organs occupy less than one percent of the foreground. We identify a failure mode of self-training that is specific to this regime. When a teacher misses a small organ in a scan, every voxel of that organ receives the confident pseudo-label background, and the student is trained toward the organ absence. Repeated over many scans, this loop extinguishes tail organs. In a pilot study, removing the gallbladder from the pseudo-labels reduced its Dice on AMOS from 36.5 to 1.0. We argue that a missing organ should be treated as unresolved rather than absent and propose Existence-aware Self-Training (ExiST). It builds pseudo-labels by leave-one-out consensus of independently trained networks, screens them with a quality gate on validation scans, and infers, per scan and per organ, whether the organ was found. For every unresolved organ, the background voxels of that scan receive the set-valued target, trained with a marginal cross-entropy that accepts either answer and a Dice term that skips uncertain voxels. We show that this loss never pushes a candidate organ toward absence and never favors one member of the set, which leaves the decision to labeled evidence. ExiST outperforms the current state-of-the-art methods and reaches 77.1 mean Dice on Synapse with 20% labeled scans and 75.9 on AMOS with 5% labeled scans.
