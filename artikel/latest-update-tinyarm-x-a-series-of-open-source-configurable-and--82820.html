Latest Update: TinyARM-X: A Series of Open-Source, Configurable, and Transparent Torque-Controlled Desktop-Scale Robotic Manipulators

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (182 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.24554v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Visuomotor policies have demonstrated that imprecise low-cost robots can autonomously complete complex manipulation tasks. However, most inexpensive manipulators rely on compact and rigidly position-controlled servos for actuation, which complicate simulation, operation, and safety. This work introduces TinyARM-X, a framework for easily manufacturable, configurable, and torque-controllable low-cost (<350 USD) serial manipulators. By using a single hardware/software architecture, a <7 Degree of Freedom (DoF) TinyARM can be manually reconfigured to an operational >3 DoF robot within 10 min. Actuator modeling and dynamic compensation enable torque-controlled applications like mixed-reality impedance control teleoperation and simulator force feedback rendering. Experiments include mechanical benchmarking and real-world force-aware telemanipulation. The TinyARM-7 has a payload of 33.5 g, a max speed of 0.77 m/s, and a reach of 210 mm. Moreover, with dynamic compensation, the robot has a backdrive-breakaway force of 1.4 N and a backdrive-running force of 0.7 N. Additionally, Cartesian-Impedance Control (CIC) attains a translational stiffness of >15 N/m. Lastly, the TinyARM-X stack is open-sourced to disseminate accessible desktop-scale torque-controlled robotic manipulation to the community.
