Distributed Shaping of Robot Swarms Through Spatial Moments of Arbitrary Order

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (256 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.19272v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: This paper shapes a swarm into a target pattern, such as a figure taken from an image, by controlling its spatial moments. The image is sampled into a reference configuration whose centroid and covariance set the position and principal spreads of the formation, while its higher-order moments, expressed in covariance-normalized (whitened) coordinates, encode the remaining shape. In contrast with geometric formation control, which prescribes distances, bearings, or relative positions between specific agents, this specification concerns the swarm as a whole and assigns no agent to a particular place. The key mechanism is orthogonalization in the swarm's own empirical inner product: projecting negative-gradient velocities onto the complement of low-degree polynomials sampled at the agents makes every higher-order correction preserve all lower-order moments exactly. The resulting hierarchical controller has lower triangular error dynamics and converges locally exponentially for any positive gains, so orders can be added without undoing those already achieved. A joint alternative regulates the higher moments together through a regularized Gauss--Newton step while preserving the centroid and covariance dynamics. We give bounds on the number of agents and sufficient conditions for regularity. Both controllers admit a distributed implementation that uses only relative positions and neighbor communication and retains local exponential convergence when estimation is sufficiently fast. In a distributed simulation on a sparse graph, 350 agents form an airplane from an unstructured swarm through moments up to order ten; the hierarchy reaches the full specification, while the joint law approaches it with smaller velocities.
