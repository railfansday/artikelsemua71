Latest Update: Hierarchical Personalized Federated Learning for Fine-Grained Intrusion Detection: A Multi-Class Analysis in Heterogeneous Attack Environments

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (221 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.21970v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) play a vital role in protecting modern network infrastructures. Although Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative training without sharing raw network traffic, global models struggle with non-IID attack distributions. Moreover, practical security operations require fine-grained attack classification for targeted threat responses. To address these challenges, this paper investigates the convergence dynamics and performance of Hierarchical Personalized Federated Learning (HPFL), which combines multi-tier aggregation with client-level personalization, for 8-class, 6-class, and 4-class intrusion detection using Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) models implemented in NVIDIA FLARE on the CICIDS2017 dataset. Experimental results show that personalized HPFL models achieve micro F1-scores up to 0.9911 in the 6-class task, while the overall local accuracy reaches 0.9975 in the 8-class task. On 8-class local test sets, HPFL achieves a micro F1-score of 0.9901 and a macro F1-score of 0.8324, outperforming FedAvg (0.7176) and FedProx (0.5624) in macro F1. Cross-client evaluations demonstrate out-of-distribution generalization, with HPFL achieving a 4-class micro F1-score of 0.9294 compared with 0.9140 for standalone local models. Furthermore, distance-weighted network analysis estimates a 48.9% reduction in transmission overhead compared with conventional FL (194.4 MB vs. 380.3 MB for the 8-class task). These findings demonstrate HPFLs potential as an accurate, privacy-preserving, and communication-efficient approach to fine-grained intrusion detection in heterogeneous networks.
