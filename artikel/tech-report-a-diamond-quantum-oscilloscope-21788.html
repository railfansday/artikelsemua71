Tech Report: A Diamond Quantum Oscilloscope

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (194 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2105.06037v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum-enhanced metrology has achieved remarkable precision in parameter estimation, but extending these advantages to continuous time-dependent fields (waveform sensing) remains a formidable challenge due to the infinite-dimensional nature of the signal. Here, we report the first experimental realization of a room-temperature solid-state quantum oscilloscope based on a sequential quantum difference amplification strategy. Within a zero-order-hold reconstruction framework, our method completely bypasses the need for massive entanglement. Crucially, we demonstrate that the overall waveform reconstruction error unequivocally follows the theoretically predicted Heisenberg scaling of \(O(N^{-1/2})\), decisively surpassing the classical \(O(N^{-1/3})\) limit. This transformative precision requires approximately 30-fold fewer quantum resources than conventional methods to achieve the same waveform error. Furthermore, we demonstrate the reconstruction of a multicomponent waveform and distinctly resolve weak high-frequency components that are entirely drowned in the shot noise of classical sampling. These results transition quantum metrology from parameter estimation into a practical instrument, establishing a genuine quantum advantage in continuous waveform reconstruction and paving the way for critical applications such as nanoscale nuclear magnetic resonance imaging, where detecting extremely weak signals is crucial for resolving molecular structures.
