Breaking: Reconfiguring Procedural Knowledge for Compositional Robot Skill Adaptation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (203 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22174v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: General-purpose behavior models increasingly internalize broad procedural knowledge during training, making their adaptation to new goals and skill interfaces less a problem of learning entirely new behaviors than of reconfiguring existing competence. We study this capability as reconfigurability, the ability to realize new behaviors by cost-efficiently restructuring existing components and relations under sparse governing information, where task specifications provide high-level goals and procedure specifications expose a limited set of reusable factors, leaving the concrete bindings and compositions required for execution underdetermined. We propose RecPo, a framework that represents skills task-agnostically through multiple procedure views and materializes executable skill sequences by composing stored transitions to realize task-specific changes. RecPo maintains a procedure knowledge base of skill-induced transitions, with each view exposing a reusable factor and preserving local skill connectivity. At deployment, it interprets task specifications through the same views and resolves the resulting compositional queries over the stored relations to bind skills for execution. On long-horizon tasks in an extended Franka Kitchen benchmark, RecPo achieves 91.9% ordered completion with 1.0 ms of task inference per episode across varying procedure support and task-record sparsity. Real-robot studies further demonstrate adaptation through local memory edits without task-specific retraining.
