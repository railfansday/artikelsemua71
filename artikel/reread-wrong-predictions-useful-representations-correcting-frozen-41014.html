ReRead: Wrong Predictions, Useful Representations - Correcting Frozen Robot Policies by Re-reading Their Own Representations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (204 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.20465v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: When a pretrained robot policy fails on challenging tasks, common remedies include retraining the backbone, adding an external visual encoder, or introducing a memory module. We argue that wrong predictions do not imply wrong representations: a policy can retain the required information yet fail to read it out as the correct action. To test this view, we propose ReRead: with the backbone frozen, a corrector re-reads the current and two previous caches to predict an action residual. The corrector sees no images, is trained solely on offline demonstrations from all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, and adds only 4.17% latency. Ablations and interventions show that the correction relies on the backbone's own representations: none of four external visual encoders adds further gain, and swapping in past caches from other trajectories sharply increases action error. On the 11 hardest RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, ReRead improves the success rates of LingBot-VLA, $\pi_{0.5}$, and Fast-WAM by 23.45, 13.09, and 8.00 percentage points, respectively. In paired evaluation on all 50 tasks, it raises Fast-WAM's average success rate from 90.73% to 92.66%. On a Flexiv dual-arm robot, it raises success on a phase-ambiguous task from 0% to 36%.
