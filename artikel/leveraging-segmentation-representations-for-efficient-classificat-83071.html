Leveraging Segmentation Representations for Efficient Classification: A Self-Configuring Framework Based on nnU-Net

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (198 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22380v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We propose a self-configuring framework that extends the nnU-Net workflow to both segmentation and classification in 3D medical imaging. Building on nnU-Net's self-configuring method, the framework reuses representations learned by a segmentation model for image-level classification. Its encoder features and predicted segmentation probabilities are used by a lightweight classifier. Two information-sharing strategies are investigated. In the frozen-backbone branch, features are augmented with region-of-interest information, predicted foreground volume, and segmentation confidence. In the adaptation branch, the final encoder stage is partially fine-tuned for classification, with an auxiliary segmentation loss to preserve segmentation-related information. Additional five-fold experiments revealed that more complex configurations did not consistently improve classification performance. On the public validation leaderboard, our selected framework achieved an Overall score of 0.8056. In the official FLARE 2026 Task 2 final evaluation, our framework ranked first overall, achieving the highest DSC, NSD, and AUROC across all four testing tasks, including two hidden testing tasks. Local inference measurements also remained below the challenge limits of $60 \mathrm{s}$ per case and $4 \mathrm{GB}$ GPU memory. These results demonstrate the practical value of reusing segmentation representations for classification while maintaining a simple and efficient framework.
