Fuse to Recognize the Unseen: Class-Relative Infrared-Visible Fusion for Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (198 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24316v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Visible-infrared image fusion leverages complementary thermal cues to enhance visual perception, particularly under challenging illumination. However, such perceptual complementarity does not readily translate into semantic gains in open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS), where CLIP-based models are predominantly pretrained on RGB imagery. Directly introducing IR information may instead disrupt RGB-language alignment, turning promising thermal cues into semantic interference that undermines unseen-class recognition. Our pixel-level RGB-IR semantic analysis reveals that class-relevant thermal cues can correct certain RGB misclassifications while preserving reliable RGB predictions, motivating RGB-anchored, class-relative thermal cue selection. Based on this insight, we propose EmergeFuse, an infrared-visible fusion framework for OVSS that selectively converts complementary thermal evidence into unseen-class semantic corrections. Specifically, class-relative cue selection evaluates RGB-IR semantic responses and calibrated thermal evidence relative to RGB predictions, generating class-relative correction requests through competitive selection. Text-contrast image rectification then translates these selected requests into semantic rectification directions through text contrasts, guiding evidence-gated feature updates to realize complementary thermal evidence for unseen-class recognition. Experiments on three benchmarks demonstrate gains of up to 4.755% in unseen-class mIoU and 15.730% in strict complementary conversion rate over existing fusion methods, while maintaining competitive fusion quality.
