Latest Update: "I Don't Think It's a Scam": A First Look at Family Intervention Barriers in Elder Abuse Discussions on Reddit and Baidu Tieba

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (232 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.24899v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: When older adults trust scammers despite warnings, families face obstacles to intervention. Complainants aged 60 and older reported \$4.9 billion in losses in 2024 to the U.S. Internet Crime Complaint Center. To investigate family intervention barriers to elder scams, we collected 2M Reddit posts from May 2021 to June 2026 and 2B Baidu Tieba posts from 2007 to 2026. The final corpora contain 13,082 Reddit posts and 3,933 Baidu Tieba posts, labeled using platform-specific codebooks, manual reference annotations, and LLM coding. Through a mixed-methods analysis, we find that scams often extend beyond a single incident and are reported alongside debt problems and broken family relationships. In 68.0\% of Reddit posts recording reactions, older adults demonstrated resistance or continued involvement; in Baidu Tieba family-confrontation posts, 41.9\% showed continued belief. Relatives identified fraud in 74.4\% of Reddit posts and narrated cases on Baidu Tieba, but without the victim's consent, relatives may lack the legal authority to intervene. In Reddit posts recording reactions, account locks or credential changes appeared in 8.9\% under resistance or continued involvement, compared with 33.3\% under acceptance. Measures such as phone or network monitoring and financial takeover were reported more often under resistance. Recovering funds is very limited in posts recording follow-up results of fraud; Baidu Tieba users reported that police classified older adults' voluntary transfers as civil disputes.
