How Many Persistent Features Does a Biometric Need? A Law Linking Equal Error Rate to Feature Count and Persistence

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (255 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21973v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A biometric designer controls the number of features and their temporal persistence. Earlier empirical work linked the equal error rate (EER) to persistence and to the required number of features by straight lines. We derive the law behind these regularities. For $N$ independent standardized Gaussian features with test-retest correlation $\rho$, scored by cosine similarity, Fisher's $z$-transform turns the genuine and impostor scores into two normals, giving $\mathrm{EER}=\Phi(-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}\,\operatorname{atanh}\rho)$ and, inversely, $N=(2q/\operatorname{atanh}\rho)^2$ with $q=\Phi^{-1}(1-\mathrm{EER})$; intercorrelated features enter through an effective count $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The central extension lets persistence vary across comparisons with spread $\tau$ on Fisher's scale: $\mathrm{EER}=\Phi(-m/(\sqrt{\tau^2+v}+\sqrt{v}))$ with $v=1/(N_{\mathrm{eff}}-2)$, which for a normal spread has a floor $\Phi(-m/\tau)$ that no number of features can lower. With no fitted parameter the simple law reproduces published simulations and the EER-ICC line, and predicts the KCC-EER relation of eye-movement embeddings on GazeBase ($R^2=0.88$), though less well on GazeBaseVR ($0.63$). Read from the genuine and impostor scores, the general form matches the EER of eye movements, keystroke dynamics, the ECG and face embeddings over 101 conditions with $R^2=0.98$ and a median error of $0.6$ points, against $0.87$ and $1.9$ points for the simple law. The 61 eye-movement, keystroke and ECG conditions check consistency on the same scores; the 40 face conditions use held-out subjects, with the same accuracy. The product $\tau\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{eff}}-2}$ gives a design rule: adding or decorrelating features pays fully while it is well below one and little once it exceeds about three, where only higher or more uniform persistence helps.
