HospitalBench: Diagnosing Where and Why Embodied Agents Fail on Long Hospital Task Chains

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (246 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.19274v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Long-horizon embodied tasks fail in ways that end-task success cannot see. HospitalBench is a diagnostic benchmark of ten hospital chores in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, each two unscored warm-up steps followed by a causal chain of five scored sub-tasks in one continuous simulation: the state one step leaves (an object pose, a choice, a fact seen once) determines what the next step must do, unlike benchmarks that place sub-tasks side by side. Vision-language agents act through skill-level tools from camera images. A scripted robot that makes only the calls a model gets is the solvability check of a chore. From each trajectory the benchmark reads where a chain breaks, what the agent did after a failed call, and how far its own position estimate drifts from the true one. This version reports first results only: 15 of 60 planned runs (three models at high effort, one run each on chores T1 and T10 from both start states and T3 from one), with scripted-robot proofs on three of the ten chores. Of the 15 runs, 2 passed all five sub-tasks. Runs still going after sub-tasks 1 to 5 were 15, 8, 4, 2, 2. Of 189 failed calls, 168 were followed by a different call and 111 by a call that did not fail. These are first results and not a full benchmark. We will release the scenes, task chains, scripted robot, evaluation harness and trajectories.
