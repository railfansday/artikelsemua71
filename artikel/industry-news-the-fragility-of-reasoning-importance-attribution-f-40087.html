Industry News: The Fragility of Reasoning-Importance Attribution for Pruning Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (270 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.22234v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Attribution-guided pruning scores components by their causal contribution and protects the important ones, and it is increasingly applied to compress reasoning language models without destroying their chain of thought. Its reliability rests on an unexamined assumption: that reasoning importance can be faithfully measured. Across four small reasoning models, we show this measurement is fragile in three ways, each of which changes pruning decisions. First, scoring final-answer tokens, the natural choice, saturates in every chain-of-thought model we test (answer-token negative log-likelihood, NLL, of $10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$) because the model states its answer inside its own reasoning; guiding head pruning with this target costs 27.6 and 32.2 accuracy points relative to scoring reasoning tokens on the two models we power with five seeds, while perplexity barely moves and even reverses sign at 7B. Second, causal importance is not localizable to single units: single-neuron effects sit at the measurement floor, their cross-split ranking is noise, and they cancel: a group's ablation effect disagrees in sign with the summed effects of its members in 90\% of groups. Reproducible importance emerges only at groups of $\sim 100$ neurons. Third, contrastively isolated ``reasoning-specific'' importance is not separable from generic importance, and protecting it is worse than protecting raw importance. We additionally pre-registered the hypothesis that distillation-induced concentration of importance predicts the pruning cost; it is not supported, and since our grid confounds concentration with scale, we report it as unresolved rather than reversed. The result is a short set of measurement rules: score reasoning tokens, attribute at coarse granularity, and evaluate with accuracy rather than perplexity.
