Non-Imaging Optical Tactile Sensors for Robotics: Transduction Principles, System Integration, and Robotic Applications

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (208 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20770v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Non-imaging optical tactile sensors encode contact through changes in optical intensity, wavelength, spectra, or photon counts, offering diverse architectures for robotic touch. Their usefulness depends on whether contact variables can be reliably distinguished from coupled mechanical and environmental effects, and whether the resulting information can be delivered in time to guide action. This review develops a unified framework linking mechanical load transmission, optical encoding, readout, and tactile inference to robotic task requirements. It examines reflective and waveguide-based devices, micro/nanofibers, fiber gratings, interferometric and resonant structures, and mechanoluminescent composites. Three considerations emerge across these approaches. First, high optical sensitivity supports accurate tactile estimation only when target variables remain identifiable under the relevant contact and calibration conditions. Second, spatial coverage and feedback timing must be assessed at the system level, accounting for multiplexing, acquisition, demodulation, and inference. Third, device characterization, offline recognition, and closed-loop robotic performance provide distinct levels of evidence and require different evaluation criteria. Representative studies illustrate how these considerations shape force estimation, contact localization, slip correction and distributed interaction. This review concludes with priorities for calibration transfer, reliable interfaces, scalable readout, and task-based validation, providing a basis for selecting and developing non-imaging optical tactile architectures for robotics.
