Integrated Generation and Purification of Entangled Coherent States for Non-Gaussian Teleportation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (233 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2512.10225v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Entangled coherent states (ECS) provide a powerful non-Gaussian resource for continuous-variable (CV) quantum communication, but their generation in scalable architectures remains challenging. We propose an integrated photonic scheme that creates high-fidelity quasi-entangled coherent states (quasi-ECS) from a two-mode squeezed vacuum state (TMSVS) via photon subtraction in a symmetric waveguide trimer. The resulting non-Gaussian entanglement is further enhanced by single-photon catalysis, which purifies the distributed state after transmission through lossy channels. The improvement in conditional teleportation fidelity is accompanied by a reduced heralding probability. Using these purified quasi-ECS resources, we analyze a photon-number-based teleportation protocol and demonstrate high-fidelity transfer of both coherent states and Schrodinger cat states. For the logical cat qubit, we use an orthogonal even/odd-cat basis, measurement-dependent phase corrections, and the success-probability-weighted fidelity over six logical input states. For completeness and benchmarking, we also study the Braunstein-Kimble (BK) teleportation protocol using Gaussian TMSVSs. In particular, the teleportation fidelity for cat states exceeds the benchmark value of 2/3 over a broad range of realistic channel and squeezing parameters. By contrast, the underlying TMSVS performs poorly within the photon-number-based protocol, whereas in the BK protocol it exceeds this benchmark only in a restricted regime of high squeezing and high transmissivity. Our results show that integrated photon subtraction and catalysis enable practical, chip-compatible generation of non-Gaussian entanglement suitable for advanced quantum teleportation and CV quantum networks.
