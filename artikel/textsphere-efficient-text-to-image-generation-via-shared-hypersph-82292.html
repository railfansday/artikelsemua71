TextSphere: Efficient Text-to-Image Generation via Shared Hyperspherical Alignment and Uniformity

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (222 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23155v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Recent advances in text-to-image generation have substantially improved image quality, particularly through diffusion-based methods that require multiple inference steps. In contrast, Sphere Encoder offers an efficient generative framework capable of producing images with fewer forward steps. However, extending it to free-form text is nontrivial, because its class-conditioned design lacks cross-modal alignment. To address this challenge, we propose TextSphere, an efficient text-to-image generative framework that extends Sphere Encoder with language conditioning and cross-modal alignment. We incorporate pretrained CLIP text embeddings through adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) to guide image generation while retaining the original spherical sampling mechanism. To bridge the cross-modal representation gap, we introduce a Spherical Semantic Alignment and Uniformity (SSAU) objective that jointly promotes semantic alignment and representation uniformity on the hypersphere. Experiments on CUB-200-2011, MS-COCO-2014, and Flickr30k demonstrate that TextSphere consistently reduces generation FID (gFID) over the text-conditioned baseline while achieving reconstruction FID (rFID) below 1.0. With only 4 and 6 sampling steps, TextSphere achieves lower gFID than the compared diffusion- and GAN-based models trained from scratch on CUB-200-2011 and MS-COCO-2014, respectively. We further observe that improved reconstruction fidelity does not necessarily yield better generation quality. In addition, we use Partial Least Squares Singular Value Decomposition (PLS-SVD) to analyze cross-modal alignment and modality gap, offering insight into the effectiveness of our approach.
