Hierarchical Admittance-Impedance Control for Mobile Manipulator Load Transfer

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (194 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.24771v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This paper presents a hierarchical admittance-impedance controller that combines an inner Cartesian impedance controller, an outer 6D admittance supervisor, and workspace-aware mobile-base assistance for guided load transfer. The method is motivated by assistive tasks such as airport baggage handling, where a robot should support a payload while a human worker guides its motion through small physical forces. Following an interaction-role interpretation of admittance and impedance, the torque-controlled Franka Research 3 manipulator behaves as a Cartesian impedance at the robot-object interface, providing compliant load support around a commanded equilibrium pose. At the human-facing interaction level, the outer admittance supervisor converts measured human wrench input into smooth Cartesian reference motion. The workspace-aware assistance layer shares the admittance-generated translational motion between the manipulator and a velocity-controlled base when larger motion is required, while front-side load alignment keeps the carried object near the robot's frontal region for safer and more intuitive guidance. The system was implemented in ROS and validated through a suitcase transfer experiment, showing that the robot can support the load while allowing the human to guide the transfer with relatively little effort.
