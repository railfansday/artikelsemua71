VSCO Is Gunning for Adobe Lightroom With Its New Desktop App

🌐 Source: WIRED • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (690 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, upcoming photographers looking for inspiration often start with an app like VSCO. The popular mobile app, launched more than a decade ago, is known for one-click presets that emulate film and make it easy to elevate a photograph before posting it to social media. But as newbie shutterbugs level up their craft, they often graduate to an app like Adobe Lightroom and its more professional tool set.

VSCO's pitch is simple: Keep photographers in the ecosystem by building a desktop app to rival Lightroom. On Tuesday, the organization revealed that its Studio Pro Desktop app is officially landing on macOS, following the launch of Studio Pro on iOS earlier this year. Neither version is available on Android or Windows, though the San Francisco–based company looks to port the apps in the future.

“We're helping shape these folks in their formative years,” VSCO CEO Eric Wittman tells WIRED. “We've got to keep giving them more bricks to build upon their own personal, creative journey—not just their creative journey, but also help them build a career.”.

The desktop client is one part of VSCO's growing portfolio that caters to full-time photographers. Subscribing to VSCO One, for example, nets you access to Studio Pro, additional presets, and VSCO Workspace—a customer relationship management tool for handling contracts, booking, and scheduling. It also includes VSCO Galleries to send finished work directly to clients and VSCO Sites, a portfolio website builder to find latest clients. All of these apps and services rolled out in the past year.

Sources indicate that access to the broad suite costs $500 a year, or you can get the Studio Pro mobile and desktop apps alone with a VSCO Pro membership for $5 per month ($60 annually). There is also a free tier, but some tools are locked behind the subscription. (Naturally, the original VSCO app is still around for enthusiasts.).

According to recent reports, the Studio Pro Desktop app is speedy, features more than 200 presets, and supports RAW editing for 20 camera brands. Batch editing is the highlight, letting photographers edit up to 500 images simultaneously to speed up workflows while preserving the original files. Previews are rendered in real time, and at the end, customers can export the final images directly to VSCO Galleries (or wherever they'd like).

According to recent reports, I've been trying the app for a few weeks, and it is snappy. It loads RAW files promptly, and it even supports keyboard shortcuts. The sheer breadth of presets is also really nice, even if I tend to tweak colors myself. But there's a catch. If you're like me and coming from Lightroom, you'll miss a few features that Studio Pro Desktop doesn't offer yet. For example, Masking, Curves, and the Erase function (aka the spot removal tool) are “coming soon.”.

Industry observers note that “We want to be simple, intentionally, but we want to be powerful with our tools,” says Kyle Hale, VSCO's senior product manager. He says the app will feature around 70 percent of what people need for desktop editing, with the rest coming through regular software updates. While he doesn't expect full parity with a competitor like Lightroom by the first quarter of 2027, Studio Pro Desktop will have “the meat of what those programs offer.”.

Companies like Adobe have heavily leaned into generative AI in their creative editing tools. While VSCO uses AI and machine-learning models in some tools like the upcoming Erase and retouch tools, the manufacturer says it doesn't train its own models on anything that people post on the VSCO app. VSCO also relies on some third-party models, like those from Black Forest Labs, but it has agreements in place that third parties cannot train on its user data.

“I don't think all AI is bad,” Wittman claims. “But to be very, very clear: Generative AI, or if you're replacing the photographer—like what you're seeing with what Meta is doing, what Adobe's doing, like almost every other tech giant is doing—we are not doing that. We stand by and champion photographers first and foremost, and that's not going to change.”.
