Considering Context: When World Models Need Context Encoders

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (211 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2610.06651v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Methods for generalization in model-based reinforcement learning typically assume that an agent cannot recover the latent context governing the environment dynamics from its own experience, and therefore supplies it externally. We formalize and test this assumption with \emph{predictive sufficiency}, which quantifies what access to the context adds to next-step prediction under the visitation distribution an agent induces, and separates that quantity into a history-recoverable part and a residual requiring the true context, both distinct from a finite model's deficit. We classify context-aware algorithms by the predictive risk their conditioning set can target and demonstrate across environments of increasing identification difficulty that the headroom does not follow the MDP class. The same task under different priors leaves predictive headroom in one setting and nothing distinguishable from zero in another, where the agent's behavior implicitly identifies the context and any benefit of such a mechanism cannot be attributed to missing one-step predictive information. Where headroom persists, the learned state exposes it only partially, and adding the true context still lowers the risk. Our contribution is an analytical framework, instantiated by a practical criterion for matching contextual mechanisms to the predictive information available to them, estimated from the ordinary trained agent.
