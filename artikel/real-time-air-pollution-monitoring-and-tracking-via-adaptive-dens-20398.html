Real-Time Air Pollution Monitoring and Tracking via Adaptive Density-Driven Multi-UAV Coordination

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (228 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.19703v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Accurate and timely air pollution monitoring and tracking are critical for protecting public health and supporting effective environmental management. This paper presents an adaptive Density-Driven Control (D$^2$C) framework for coordinating multiple unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to track and map dynamically evolving pollutant distributions. The proposed framework extends the D$^2$C strategy to guide UAV trajectories based on predictive pollutant propagation and dynamically updated reference densities. A sensor-driven surrogate plume revision mechanism is employed, in which reference samples are propagated using locally sensed concentration gradients and wind estimates through gradient attraction, wind advection, inter-sample repulsion, and stochastic diffusion, enabling UAVs to maintain an accurate spatio-temporal representation of the pollution field. The framework utilizes a coordinated structure where individual UAVs share their local measurements with a central coordinator to maintain a unified set of reference samples, facilitating systematic target allocation and trajectory planning. Simulation results under both mild and strong wind conditions demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of Sinkhorn distance. Specifically, in scenarios where the plume drifts beyond a fixed monitoring domain under high-velocity, time-varying wind fields, the tested baseline methods fail to maintain tracking, whereas the proposed D$^2$C framework successfully adapts to the evolving distribution. These findings highlight D$^2$C as an efficient and resilient solution for atmospheric monitoring in dynamic environments.
