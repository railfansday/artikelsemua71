Perception into Imagination: Executable Memory for World Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (185 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22294v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Physical agents must use what they previously saw or touched after visibility or contact is lost. Their actions change the implications of these remembered facts. We introduce executable perceptual memory (EPM), a world-model framework that advances explicit perceptual state alongside neural latent dynamics during imagination. Its evolving read-out informs reward prediction, value estimation and policy learning. We align reward inputs with the transitions that produced them and supervise the reward head on open-loop rollouts of recorded actions. On Memory Maze $13\times13$, EPM reaches 30.37 return at 1M interactions, compared with 23.37 when the same evolving read-out informs only policy and value on a common perceptual front end: a gain of 7.00 [3.80, 10.21]. The complete method also improves control on $9\times9$ and in MiniWorld. Probes of remembered, out-of-view arrivals show stronger reward anticipation in both visual implementations. Concentration, tactile recovery and real tactile frames demonstrate complementary roles of remembered facts in imagined-policy training, motion-dependent decisions and sensory-content retention. EPM connects persistent perception to the predicted consequences and learning signals of a world model for Physical AI.
