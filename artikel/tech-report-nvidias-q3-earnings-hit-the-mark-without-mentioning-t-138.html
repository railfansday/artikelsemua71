Nvidia’s Q3 revenue has more than doubled, with income from data centers up 110 percent from last year, to $107.6 billion.

Interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress confirmed the company’s Q3 earnings on Tuesday, without initially mentioning former CEO Jensen Huang or the details of his departure, or possible attempt at a return. On the earnings call accompanying the release, board chair Harvey Jones kicked things off by saying the board “does not intend to reinstate Huang as CEO,” and that “we will not be addressing or taking questions on today’s call regarding the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation and the ongoing investigation,” before Kress started with her comments on the results.

Industry observers note that as it predicted in the last financial upgrade, Nvidia is now on the list of companies that have pulled in over $100 billion in a single quarter, with revenues of $115.8 billion, up 103 percent from the same period last year and higher than its expectation of $108 billion. This quarter’s figures include data center revenues of $107.6 billion, which were up 110 percent, and edge computing (which includes the consumer gaming PC business and devices like the newly introduced RTX Spark laptops) revenue of $8.2 billion, up 42 percent.

The firm reported $65.3 billion in profit, a latest record.

“Our momentum is real. NVIDIA is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people,” said Colette Kress, interim CEO and executive vice president and chief financial officer of NVIDIA. “Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater.”.

In the fourth quarter, Nvidia expects revenues of $130 billion.
