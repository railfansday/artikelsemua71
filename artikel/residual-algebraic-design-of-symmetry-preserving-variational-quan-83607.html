Residual-Algebraic Design of Symmetry-Preserving Variational Quantum Circuits

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (239 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.24890v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Symmetry-preserving (SP) variational quantum circuits restrict evolution to physically relevant sectors of Hilbert space, but symmetry preservation alone does not guarantee accessibility within those sectors. We show that a restricted generator set can possess a residual dynamical Lie algebra whose commutant further decomposes the target symmetry sector into invariant blocks. The associated block populations are conserved and impose a kinematic upper bound on the fidelity with a target state. We then invert this diagnostic into a circuit-design principle: candidate SP generators can be screened before variational optimization by asking whether they break the residual commutant, which invariant blocks they couple, and what adjoint module they generate under the residual algebra. For two orthogonal residual blocks we prove that a single elementary real inter-block rotation generates the full orthogonal algebra. We further derive a complex escape criterion: if the real and imaginary parts of an inter-block seed are independent over the residual intertwiner algebra, the generated module is the full complex coupling space and the enlarged algebra becomes special unitary. A connected residual-block graph yields a natural multi-block generalization and suggests minimum-cost spanning-tree circuit design. We demonstrate this design principle in the XXZ and Hubbard models, where residual-algebra screening identifies SP circuit extensions that remove the relevant accessibility obstructions. These examples establish a unified route from residual-symmetry diagnosis and fidelity bounds to predictive quantum circuit design.
