One Measurement, Many Fronts in Kitaev and Cluster Chains

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (249 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.10912v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: A projective measurement of one site in the ground state of a quantum chain disturbs the chain in two ways: learning the outcome shifts distant expectation values at once, through ground-state correlations, and the measurement erases the correlations between the measured site and the rest. Both changes travel outward. We solve this problem exactly for the Kitaev chain, the cluster chain in a transverse field, and the Kitaev chain with power-law couplings, and we find that far from the measured site every quadratic observable responds through a front. Where the front is and how fast it broadens depend only on the quasiparticle dispersion. How strong it is depends on two operators: how strongly the measured one creates the fastest quasiparticles and how strongly the observed one detects them; if either factor vanishes, the front is weaker and decays faster. One measurement therefore rolls out many fronts, one per observable, with a common position and width but different decay laws. In the Kitaev chain the density and the bond energy, the two terms of its nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian, respond to one density measurement with decay laws that trade places between the topological and the trivial phase; in the cluster chain, measuring a symmetry-odd spin instead of a symmetry-even one changes the critical decay law at a fixed Hamiltonian; and with power-law couplings the width law itself changes. All decay laws and profiles are obtained in closed form.
