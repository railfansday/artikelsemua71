Latest Update: Making Koopman Control Guarantees Hold on Robots

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20402v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A Koopman controller can stabilize its learned latent-space linear model yet fail to stabilize the nonlinear plant. The learned model propagates predictions in latent space, while the deployed controller repeatedly re-encodes the measured physical state. We show that nominal closed-loop stability transfers to the nonlinear plant when the learned and true closed-loop dynamics agree to first order on the embedding's tangent space at the target. We derive a robustness margin for approximate matching and show that a stable open-loop latent model cannot exactly match an unstable equilibrium. We introduce Tangent-Invariant Koopman (TIK), which estimates a local plant linearization from offline transitions and enforces first-order matching to this estimate. TIK recovers the fitted LQR gain at the target, while a bilinear predictor captures state-dependent actuation during large maneuvers. Across four simulated unstable benchmarks, all 40 Standard Koopman controllers have stable latent closed loops, but their deployed nonlinear systems are locally unstable. In contrast, all 40 TIK controllers locally stabilize their respective nonlinear plants. An offline stability test using empirical curvature estimates identifies stable TIK controllers on two cart-pole systems, but is inconclusive under high noise. Under explicit model-error and curvature bounds, an invariant terminal set guarantees constraint satisfaction after entry under local feedback. TIK achieves 88\% constrained cart-pole swing-up success with a 0.25 ms planning budget and controls simulated seven-joint arms within 1-5 ms per step.
