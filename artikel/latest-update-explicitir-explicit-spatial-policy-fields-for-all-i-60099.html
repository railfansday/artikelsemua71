Latest Update: ExplicitIR: Explicit Spatial Policy Fields for All-in-One Image Restoration

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (195 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22222v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: All-in-one image restoration aims to recover clean images blindly from inputs with various degradation types and levels using a single model. However, the adaptation policy in most existing methods remains implicitly encoded in feature representations, leaving the restoration decision neither explicitly represented nor independently controllable. Since real-world degradations vary spatially, applying uniform updates across the entire image leads to over-smoothing of clean areas and under-correction of localized degradations. To address these limitations, we present ExplicitIR, which represents the restoration policy as two explicit per-pixel fields: Budget, which determines where and how strongly to patch, and Style, which selects between narrow and wide context windows. Specifically, ExplicitIR incorporates a Spatial Policy Estimator (SPE) to predict these fields, explicitly representing the restoration policy and capturing spatially-varying degradations, which are then applied by Policy-Adaptive Decoder Blocks (PADB) through adaptive fusion of narrow- and wide-window responses, enabling independent control and precise local modulation. This design enables spatially-adaptive restoration in a single forward pass, without requiring task labels, prompts, or test-time degradation estimators. Extensive experiments on three-task and five-task benchmarks demonstrate that ExplicitIR achieves performance competitive with the strongest all-in-one baselines.
