Latest Update: Symmetry-selected entanglement death at Floquet resonances of parametrically coupled qubits

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (264 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22192v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Periodic modulation of a qubit--qubit coupling is a standard way to engineer quantum interactions, but a strong modulation also reorganizes the transitions through which the qubits dissipate into a comb of Floquet channels. We study two identical, strongly coupled qubits whose transverse coupling is modulated anharmonically, as by a nonlinear coupler, with each qubit damped by its own reservoir. The modulation raises the ground-state concurrence of the cycle-averaged Hamiltonian from 0.45 to above 0.8, yet the steady-state concurrence stays close to its undriven value except at Floquet resonances selected by symmetry. There it undergoes entanglement death, vanishing at all times in the drive cycle over a finite range of modulation depths. Parity and exchange symmetry split the Floquet spectrum into an even sector, whose branches anticross at multiphoton resonances, and an exactly solvable odd sector, whose branches cross. Every dissipative channel connects the two sectors, so only the even anticrossings cause entanglement death, provided their gap exceeds the damping rate, whereas the nearby odd crossings leave the concurrence unchanged. Comparing time-independent and Floquet master equations, with and without the secular approximation, we show that the basis in which the dissipator is built changes the entanglement by up to 0.2 for an Ohmic bath and 0.5 for a flat one, whereas the secular approximation matters only near anticrossings with gaps comparable to the damping. The basis matters most when the bath has spectral weight at low frequency, where the drive moves dissipative channels through zero frequency. A transverse field that breaks parity lifts the selection rule.
