Beyond the Sink Token: Sink-Guided Key Editing in Vision-Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (170 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.23516v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In vision-language models (VLMs), a few sink tokens attract a disproportionate share of attention. While previous studies have examined why these tokens attract attention, we investigate how the sink-key direction affects attention to image and question tokens. Across four probed VLMs, boosting the magnitude of positive sink-aligned components increases attention on average, whereas boosting negative components decreases it; these responses persist through later layers. This identifies the native sink key as a reference for controlling attention beyond its own position. Motivated by this finding, we propose SHRINK, a training-free method that partially reduces sink-aligned components across visible keys during generation. On nine benchmarks under sampled reasoning, it improves mean accuracy by 5.1 and 2.7 percentage points on LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with smaller gains on the other two main models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Gemma-3-4B. Beyond SHRINK, rescaling sink-aligned key components can replace the attention operations of two existing attention-steering methods, GIFT and ACG, with comparable accuracy on hallucination benchmarks.
