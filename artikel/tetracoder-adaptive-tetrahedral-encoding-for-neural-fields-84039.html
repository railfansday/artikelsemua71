TetraCoder: Adaptive Tetrahedral Encoding for Neural Fields

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (187 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.18267v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Implicit and explicit representations of geometry are central in computer graphics applications and come with their pros and cons. With surface sampling being faster on explicit representations and spatial queries being faster on implicit representations, finding efficient conversions between them is a highly desirable property. We present TetraCoder, a single resolution hash-free adaptive tetrahedral encoding that links an explicit tetrahedral spatial encoder with an underlying implicit signed distance field (SDF). The resulting hybrid implicit-explicit space is continuous piecewise affine (CPWA) by design, allowing to obtain an exact correspondence between the implicit SDF and the explicit analytic mesh. TetraCoder's design comes with additional perks, in particular: (a) it scales better than multi-resolution encoding methods due to its single resolution optimizable grid, (b) unlike hash-based methods, it is not affected by collisions, (c) it enables fast tetrahedral ray-tracing, geometry and appearance editing, directly through its adaptive tetrahedral scaffold. We demonstrate the versatility of TetraCoder by showing an improvement over state-of-the-art methods on multiple tasks: 2D/3D fitting from images and point clouds, 3D novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and geometry/appearance editing.
