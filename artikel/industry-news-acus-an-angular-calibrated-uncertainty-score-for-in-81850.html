Industry News: ACUS: An Angular Calibrated Uncertainty Score for Incomplete-View CT

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (230 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.23752v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Sparse-view and limited-arc computed tomography (CT) are commonly treated as projection completion: a network fills an unmeasured view, and the result is ranked by similarity to one ground-truth image. That score can be high even when the query angle is far from every acquired measurement, because a learned prior may invent anatomy the scan never constrained. Existing protocols therefore leave a basic question unanswered: how does predictive reliability change with distance from the acquired views? We propose the Angular Calibrated Uncertainty Score (ACUS) to answer that question. ACUS is the Winkler interval score of a nominal 90% predictive interval on held-out projections, binned by distance to the nearest acquired view. It charges interval width and adds a large miss penalty when a held-out pixel falls outside the interval, so a narrow band helps only when coverage holds. We use collapse on acquired angles as a necessary consistency check on the predictor. On chest and dental cone-beam orbits, interval width grows with the angular gap. Empirical coverage ranges from 77% to 92%, and the peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) of the point estimate falls as the width rises. Nearest-neighbor, interpolation, and point completion, each given one residual scale, have zero reported far coverage and incur outside-distance penalties. ACUS exposes gap-dependent overconfidence that a point-estimate PSNR alone does not measure.
