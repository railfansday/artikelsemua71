Certify, Then Select: Viability-Aware Runtime Steering of Frozen Diffusion Policies

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (281 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.19047v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Diffusion policies, which generate short action sequences by denoising random noise, are a leading way to learn robot skills from demonstrations. Once deployed, they meet constraints the demonstrations never contained, and retraining for each is impractical. A safety filter edits the policy's action to satisfy them, but the edit ignores the task, and a large edit can leave the policy where it cannot continue. We propose CAST (Certified Action Selection at Test time): draw a few action chunks from the frozen policy, certify each with a barrier condition, and execute the certified chunk that a learned value ensemble ranks highest. We prove that a certified step is safe whatever the critic does; that, whenever a certified sample exists, the executed chunk stays within log N nats of the policy conditioned on feasibility, while a safety-filter projection can be singular to it; and that an exact closed-loop identity splits the gain of ranking into availability, spread and the critic's target. Certifying the next chunk is not enough: a generative controller can have safe actions available now and none later. We characterise the safe control authority of a frozen policy under finite sampling, and CAST-V preserves it through a policy-conditioned viability, estimated with a model under a finite-sample guarantee or learned from the controller's own logs (CAST-VL). On robomimic manipulation, a planar quadrotor, a MuJoCo humanoid and a unicycle, with frozen policies, CAST raises joint task-and-constraint success above the output filter in nearly every setting; certifying before ranking is decisive where a filter's edit is large, and viability removes most dead ends that one-step certification cannot see. Project page: https://clmoro.github.io/cast.
