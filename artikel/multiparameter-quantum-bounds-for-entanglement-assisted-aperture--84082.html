Multiparameter quantum bounds for entanglement-assisted aperture synthesis

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (274 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2609.10111v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Entanglement-assisted optical interferometry promises diffraction-limited imaging over baselines where light cannot be physically combined, but its estimation-theoretic bounds exist only for a single visibility on one baseline. This work formulates the multiparameter quantum estimation problem for an M-station array. A reduction lemma indicates that the multimode quantum Fisher information (QFI) equals the mean photon number times the QFI of a single delocalized photon, making the problem finite-dimensional. The local photon-number superselection rule erases all phase information from the bare state; shared entanglement restores a fraction f(r)=r/(1+r) of the QFI on a baseline supplied with r pairs, a benchmark that applies to every pair of the array. The QFI matrix and its mean Uhlmann curvature show numerically that a point source is measurement-compatible, whereas every extended-source model examined is incompatible; a semidefinite-programming evaluation shows that the Holevo bound exceeds the symmetric-logarithmic-derivative bound by up to ~24% for three stations and by 4-8% for larger arrays and for CHARA. An explicit collective receiver, a numerically optimised global linear-optical network with photon counting, attains a weighted variance within 2-9% of the Holevo bound, over an order of magnitude below an explicit pairwise receiver, and a noisy-resource threshold gives the break-even baseline (~22 km for illustrative parameters) beyond which distributed entanglement beats direct transmission. Finally, allocating a finite entanglement budget has a closed-form proportional (square-root-law) solution for a per-baseline fringe cost, whereas for the source-parameter variance under an explicit photon budget the routing of photons matters far more than the entanglement split. These results recast quantum aperture synthesis as collective multiparameter estimation, illustrated on CHARA.
