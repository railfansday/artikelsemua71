📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Jensen Huang noted that he is open to returning as chief executive of Nvidia, five days after he resigned from the firm.

In a statement published Tuesday morning on X, Huang mentioned that since stepping down he has received an “outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO.”.

“If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” Huang wrote.

Huang also thanked people who indicated “kindness” to him and his family and acknowledged “the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I've never met.” He reiterated his love for America and Nvidia and his remorse for his unfaithfulness to his wife, which he also expressed in a tech giant-wide email sent last week.

Industry observers note that huang resigned four days after Nvidia launched an internal investigation into an affair he had admitted to having with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who has no known relation to Jensen. Huang reportedly misled the tech giant’s general counsel and chief marketing officer about the affair with Alice.

Recent findings suggest that the day before Huang stepped down, The Latest York Times reported that he'd had a second affair with one of his direct reports at Nvidia. After his resignation, Reuters reported on an alleged third affair with another Nvidia employee. The manufacturer’s code of conduct discourages "personal relationships" that "may create a conflict of interest.".

Colette Kress, Nvidia’s longtime chief financial officer, was installed as interim CEO upon Huang’s resignation.

The Newly introduced York Times first reported Jensen’s association with Alice, also known as Huang Le, in October 2025. On November 6 of this year, citing an intelligence memo that reportedly circulated among Trump administration officials last spring, The New York Times and the Financial Times reported that intelligence officials believed Alice may be an asset of China’s Ministry of State Security. Trump administration officials, including tech executive and White House Science and Technology advisory council cochair David Sacks, have questioned whether Alice was associated with the MSS. The Chinese government has rejected claims that Alice spied for the nation.

WIRED reported last week that on the same day Huang resigned, Nvidia employees began using an existing petition to the board to call for his reinstatement. It gained more than 1,600 comments in just a few hours.

Recent findings suggest that A separate, public petition titled “Stand With Jensen” similarly calls for the executive’s return. At the time of writing, it has more than 193,000 online signatures, including more than 11,000 self-identified current and former Nvidia employees and more than 100,000 self-identified shareholders. On Friday, Elon Musk remarked that he was going to sign the petition, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan claimed that he had signed.

President Donald Trump told reporters on Monday that he “would not mind” if Huang returned as CEO, adding that he believed Huang had “learned a big lesson.”.

Nvidia’s board of directors, however, reportedly said on Monday that it was not considering reinstating Huang as CEO. On Tuesday, the board announced that it had established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee “for board-level oversight of the company’s national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management.”.
