Breaking: AVD: A Multi-Sensor Teleoperated Wheelchair Platform with Safety-Aware Control

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (215 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20720v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Electric wheelchairs predominantly rely on manual joystick interfaces, which present critical barriers to independent mobility for individuals with severe motor and visual impairments. This paper presents AVD (Autonomous Vehicle Drive), a dependable teleoperation architecture for assistive mobility that unifies perception, communication, embedded control, and remote supervision in a single low-cost platform built entirely from commercially available hardware. The central engineering contribution is a safety-oriented design framework: control authority follows a static hierarchy, a manual emergency stop and the occupant's joystick pre-empt remote commands, and the on-board sensing layer has stop-only authority. The compute design partitions primary and safety functions and specifies a heartbeat-driven safe-state transition for the fault cases considered. The sensing stack this framework depends on is characterized against measured data: per-channel acquisition latency, a 2,025-scan LiDAR degradation campaign spanning five target configurations and a control under water mist, airborne dust, and direct aperture occlusion, and attitude measurements across static inclines to 47 degrees. The lowest pooled environmental-condition mean retained 76.6% of the clear-baseline forward-sector yield, and no scan lost the sector completely. A logged vibration-induced inertial dropout occurred while LiDAR and camera logging continued, illustrating complementary channel behavior. The result is a documented prototype and an explicit validation path for a deployable wheelchair implementation.
