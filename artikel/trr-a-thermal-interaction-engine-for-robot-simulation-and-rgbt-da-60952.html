TRR: A Thermal Interaction Engine for Robot Simulation and RGBT Data Generation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (248 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20792v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A robot's actions change the thermal conditions of its next interaction: heat retained after release, carried with objects or stored beneath the surface can change a later contact's outcome despite similar current temperatures. Training trajectories must capture how these effects connect earlier actions to later observations and task outcomes. We present TRR, a \emph{Thermal Robot Renderer} that attaches spatial and depth-resolved thermal state to both contact partners, updates local heat exchange through contact, transport and separation, and generates synchronized RGB--thermal (RGBT) observations and event-local task checks on one execution clock. Controlled interventions show why retained heat, spatial transport and internal storage matter for subsequent contact outcomes. From the same state, TRR produces a paired six-task benchmark of 2,224 static--dynamic pairs, that is, 4,448 trajectories with synchronized dual-view observations, states and actions. Visible thermal history raises safe contact-site selection from 75.0\% to 91.7\% on a dedicated learning corpus, and training on dynamic histories raises simulated transfer completion by 50 percentage points under a fresh heating procedure with shared feedback execution. We demonstrate RGBT observation adapters on MuJoCo and Isaac Sim, report an engineering inventory of ten prepared robot configurations and six demonstrated camera viewpoints, and describe a developer workflow that carries an existing robot scene to exported RGBT records. The benchmark, the workflow and the platform resources described in Section 6 form the first items of a planned public release. Demonstrations and resource updates are available on the project page.
