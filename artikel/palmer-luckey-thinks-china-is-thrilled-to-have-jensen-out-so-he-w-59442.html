Palmer Luckey thinks China is ‘thrilled to have Jensen out,’ so he wants the ousted Nvidia CEO reinstated

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anduril CEO Palmer Luckey thinks Nvidia’s board is giving China exactly what it wants by removing the firm’s longtime CEO and co-founder, Jensen Huang.

Nvidia, the leading semiconductor designer whose chips power much of the AI industry, is undergoing a critical, unexpected leadership change. Huang, who led the company for 33 years, stepped down last week amid several controversies, including his extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy who has been investigated for possibly smuggling Nvidia chips into China to circumvent U.S. export restrictions.

“China wants Jensen out, so I want him in,” Luckey said onstage at The Fresh York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, repeating a statement he posted on X last week. “They’re thrilled to have Jensen out… There’s a reason it’s knocking a trillion dollars off Nvidia’s market cap. They know it makes America weaker to knock him out.”.

Luckey disagrees with Huang’s longstanding position that Nvidia should have more leeway to export chips to China. But he also recognizes that China stands to benefit if its domestic chipmakers can challenge Nvidia’s dominance.

“China doesn’t want Nvidia to sell GPUs to Chinese companies,” Luckey said. “There are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs… China will not give them an import permit because they want to build an internal domestic processor industry… not build one that is dependent on the United States.”.

Nvidia has said as much itself — the U.S. government granted the manufacturer license to ship some of its powerful H200 chips to certain Chinese customers, but those sales were restricted by the Chinese government. Nvidia’s interim CEO Colette Kress said during the company’s quarterly earnings call on Tuesday that it will no longer sell H200 chips in China at all.

Luckey is far from alone among industry peers in his defense of Huang.

More than 200,000 people — including Elon Musk and the CEOs of Y Combinator, Databricks, Epic Games, Shopify, and Replit — have signed a petition calling for Nvidia to reinstate its founder. But Nvidia executives remarked during the firm’s earnings call on Tuesday that the company does not have plans to reappoint Huang, even though the founder offered to return.
