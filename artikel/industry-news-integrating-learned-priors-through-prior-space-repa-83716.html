Industry News: Integrating Learned Priors through Prior-Space Reparameterization for Physically Based Inverse Rendering

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (211 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.07573v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Physically based inverse rendering is an ambiguous problem, allowing independently optimized material variables to absorb appearance variations caused by illumination. Learned material predictions provide useful cues about which surface points have similar materials. Existing approaches often incorporate these cues through auxiliary regularization losses. Instead, we propose a novel prior-guided reparameterization of the material optimization space. We fuse multi-view material predictions onto a mesh and construct a permutohedral lattice in the resulting prior feature space. Surface points with similar predictions share lattice variables, restricting the material field to a prior-defined subspace that is optimized jointly with geometry and illumination using a purely photometric objective. Experiments on three synthetic benchmarks demonstrate improved material decomposition and relighting, with results on par with the strongest baseline on Stanford-ORB. As a complete system, our method improves relighting PSNR by 1.4-1.5\,dB over the strongest evaluated mesh-based baseline on Synthetic4Relight and TensoIR-Synthetic. Analysis of the optimization reveals that shared variables pool per-vertex photometric gradients into updates that are more consistent across sampled views. In a controlled comparison within the same pipeline, the restricted subspace, despite its reduced degrees of freedom, yields more accurate material decomposition than both unrestricted per-vertex optimization and a regularization loss built from the same prior.
