Meaning Reaches Object Choice but Not Completion: Where Is a VLA Vulnerable to Landmark Synonyms?

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (263 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24076v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: VLA policies are fragile to rewording. Success rates alone cannot say whether a policy fails to choose the object or to complete the task, nor where in the policy to intervene. We study the released GR00T N1.7 on two LIBERO-Spatial tasks differing only in the landmark word telling which of two bowls to take. Synonyms absent from the training instructions send the policy to the bowl they name, unlike made-up words, yet complete 67.6% of one task's closed-loop episodes, against the original word's 100%. Before the connector between the vision-language model and the action generator, replacing only the landmark tokens' vectors with the other landmark's (pasting) sends nearly every episode to the other bowl. After the connector, pasting at the landmark, the remaining instruction, or the image tokens alone rarely moves the choice, but any two do. Hiding the landmark tokens from the action generator after the connector still lowers completion for the synonyms that fall short. Finetuning on diversified landmark words repairs that one task's completion for synonyms absent from finetuning; finetuning on the original instructions does not. Training only the connector on the diversified words suffices, as does copying the connector of a policy fully finetuned on them into one finetuned on the original instructions. An open-loop score on starting frames misses that weights outside the connector also carry part of the repair. For now, on this evidence, robustness to rewording should be judged in closed loop, with actions executed, intervening on both sides of the connector, scoring choice and completion separately.
