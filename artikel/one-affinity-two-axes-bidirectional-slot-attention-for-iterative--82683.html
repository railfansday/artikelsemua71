One Affinity, Two Axes: Bidirectional Slot Attention for Iterative Aggregation across the Structure--Detail Spectrum in Visual Retrieval

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (265 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.24371v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Aggregating dense patch tokens into compact and discriminative global descriptors is central to visual retrieval. Although foundation models provide rich local features, existing aggregation heads still rely on single-pass mappings and lack an explicit mechanism to coordinate structural and fine-grained cues. Therefore, we propose Bidirectional Slot Attention (BiSA), which reformulates aggregation as an iterative coupled system. Cross-Axis Consensus (CAC) progressively refines learnable slots by interpreting a single shared token--slot affinity along two complementary axes. Along the competition axis, slots vie for each token under a temperature shared across all of them, determining token ownership and inducing a non-redundant scene decomposition. Along the selection axis, a slot-specific temperature governs the breadth of each slot's response, spanning a spectrum from broad layouts to sparse fine-grained details. Their consensus couples non-redundant decomposition with multi-granular slot specialization. Furthermore, the two axes expose intrinsic token-reliability cues at no extra cost: a token's claim strength under competition and its salience under selection. Progressive Token Filtering (PTF) converts these cues into a retention probability that down-weights unreliable tokens before the next CAC iteration, without any segmentation branch or external prior, forming a self-reinforcing loop of competitive decomposition, selective focusing, and feedback purification. Across all three retrieval tasks, BiSA leads on five of six place recognition benchmarks (+4.2 R@1 on Baidu Mall), tops all 16 metrics of drone--satellite geo-localization (+8.4 R@1 at 150 m), and improves instance-level retrieval on ROxford-Hard by 4.5 mAP points. Notably, the selection axis adds only 20 learnable scalars, yet improves average R@1 by 2.8 points.
