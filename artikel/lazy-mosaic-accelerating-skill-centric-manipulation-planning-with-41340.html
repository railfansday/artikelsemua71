Lazy-MOSAIC: Accelerating Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Learned Skill-Effect World Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21517v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long-horizon manipulation tasks require a robot to perform a sequence of physical interactions with its environment, typically combining several different skills. Planning such behaviors efficiently is challenging: given a library of parameterized skills, solutions lie in a vast hybrid space of skill sequences and their parameters, and evaluating each candidate skill's feasibility and effect with a high-fidelity world model such as a physics simulator is computationally expensive. MOSAIC, a recent skill-centric approach, lets the skills guide a non-sequential search toward the regions of this space where they are likely to succeed; however, it pays the full cost of simulation for every parameterized skill it considers. In this work, we introduce Lazy-MOSAIC, a lazy planning framework that instead searches over hypothesized skill outcomes. A cheap learned skill-effect world model predicts, in a fraction of a millisecond, a skill's feasibility and the world state it produces. Lazy-MOSAIC then uses these predictions to focus expensive simulation on promising candidate skills and, when a hypothesis is invalidated, repairs only the affected part of the search, while ensuring that every skill in a returned blueprint is validated in simulation. We evaluate Lazy-MOSAIC on cluttered-shelf retrieval tasks of increasing difficulty and demonstrate it on a physical robot, showing that it intends substantially faster than baselines with fewer simulator queries, while matching or exceeding their success rate in every setting.
