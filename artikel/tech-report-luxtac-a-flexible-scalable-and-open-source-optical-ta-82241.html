Tech Report: LuxTac: A Flexible, Scalable, and Open-Source Optical Taxel Array with Distributed Three-Axis Force Sensing for Tactile Robot Learning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (264 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.23575v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Low-cost tactile arrays have made touch a practical input for robot learning, but piezoresistive designs sense only pressure, while camera-based sensors that also resolve shear are too bulky to tile over a gripper. To solve this problem, we present LuxTac, a flexible, scalable optical taxel array, about 3.5 mm thick and built from off-the-shelf parts. Each taxel is a digital RGBC light-to-digital converter under a translucent white silicone layer capped by an opaque black film, so the back-scattered LED light falls monotonically with local indentation depth. The same board accepts two interchangeable gel cartridges: a plain one for normal sensing, and one with colored walls that encodes the local shear in the color of the returned light, with no camera, magnets, or bump overlay, making LuxTac, to our knowledge, the first optical taxel array to resolve shear at individual taxels. Characterized with an automated CNC indenter and a six-axis force/torque sensor, the normal cartridge responds linearly to force ($R^2$ = 0.999) from 104 mN to at least 5.8 N, with 2.0% repeatability, 5.9% hysteresis, and a taxel-to-taxel variation of 6-10% after normalization. With the shear cartridge, per-taxel models estimate the three-axis force at every taxel; across three indenter shapes and nine held-out sessions, the summed estimates reach $R^2$ = 0.96/0.96/0.92 in x/y/z, each taxel's estimate 0.92/0.93/0.94, and the median shear-direction error is 4.8 degrees. Adding LuxTac pressure readings to an Action Chunking Transformer policy substantially improves peg-in-hole success over a vision-only baseline. Design files, code, and videos are available at https://luxtac.github.io.
