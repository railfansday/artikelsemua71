ECGGuard: Deep Watermarking of Electrocardiogram Signals With Error-Correcting Codes

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (260 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2610.13357v5 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Wearable electrocardiogram (ECG) recordings pass through multiple networks and systems before automated interpretation, leaving patient metadata vulnerable to loss. Digital watermarking can embed this metadata in the signal, but conventional methods fail under time shifts, and high waveform similarity does not guarantee the same downstream diagnosis. We present \rev{ECGGuard}, a deep watermarking framework for single-lead ECG. A fully convolutional generator embeds the payload without signal quantization, a detector decodes the message frame by frame and checks for watermark presence, and during training a frozen arrhythmia classifier penalizes changes that would alter its predictions. Because high bit accuracy does not ensure whole-message recovery, the payload is protected by extended Hamming codes, \rev{a validation-selected channel ordering}, and soft-decision decoding. On the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, \rev{averaged over three training runs, ECGGuard} keeps bit accuracy above \rev{98\%} under time shifts of 10 to 50 samples, where transform-domain baselines drop to chance. The coded 16-bit payload is recovered exactly in \rev{99.95\%} of clean segments\rev{, in over 96\%} under time shifts, 10\% sample deletion, or 10\% packet loss\rev{, and in 99.1\% for unseen subjects}. \rev{For the guiding classifier, the diagnostic loss limits the drop in ventricular-beat sensitivity to 1.6 percentage points on the test records (35.0 without it) and to 12.2 points for unseen subjects (67.0 without it); the drop grows to 10.3 points for independently trained classifiers, and the protection does not transfer to an external database.} \rev{ECGGuard} has 0.56 million parameters and needs about \rev{19}~ms per segment on a desktop CPU.
