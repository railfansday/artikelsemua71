Physically Admissible Generative Flow Matching for Sparse Flow Reconstruction

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (215 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22549v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reconstructing full fluid velocity fields from sparse, localized observations is a severely ill-posed inverse problem: most of the spatial domain remains unobserved, discrete conservation laws must hold strictly, and predictive uncertainty must be reliable. Existing generative models typically enforce physical constraints only at the terminal output or via soft penalties, leaving intermediate generative trajectories unconstrained. We introduce Feasible Subspace Consistency (FSC), a framework that preserves physical admissibility as an invariant of continuous generative dynamics: tangent-space matching confines the entire ODE trajectory to the discrete conservation manifold, while mass-orthonormal coordinates preserve the kinetic-energy metric. Sparse reconstruction then reduces to amortized posterior transport in the latent space of a frozen FSC prior, ensuring every posterior sample inherits incompressibility and boundary conditions without per-sample projection loops. We validate FSC on a periodic Kolmogorov flow, where exact spectral projection isolates path tangency and metric isometry, and a wall-bounded cylinder wake with immersed obstacles, realized via matrix-free iterative projection. FSC prevents the mode-coverage collapse induced by terminal projection (increasing mode coverage from $0.000$ to $0.790$) while eliminating high-wavenumber spectral distortion. In sparse reconstruction, amortized FSC reduces global relative error by $50\%$ and $46\%$, and sensor residuals by $63\%$ and $55\%$, relative to the unconditioned prior, while delivering calibrated, spatially heterogeneous uncertainty.
