Beyond Appearance: Where Forest Visual Place Recognition Actually Fails

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (177 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.21996v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Visual place recognition (VPR) in forest environments remains challenging because repetitive vegetation, and changes in illumination, season, and viewpoint can make distinct places appear similar and revisited places appear substantially different. In this paper, we investigate whether temporal, linguistic, structural, and geometric context can improve on an appearance-only descriptor, and what limits each representation when it does not. Using MegaLoc as the appearance-based baseline on the WildCross benchmark, we evaluate score-space and descriptor-space sequence matching, vision-language captions and reranking, scene-graph representations, and depth-derived stem graphs across same-direction revisits, extended routes, and reverse traversals. On a same-direction revisit, both sequence matching strategies reach similar Recall@1 (94.1% vs. 91.5%), but recall at 100% precision rises from 50.9% (single-frame MegaLoc) to 71.6% in score space and falls to 36.5% in descriptor space. The remaining context representations provide no consistent improvement over the appearance-based baseline. Same-place captions show limited place-specific discrimination, reranking is constrained by the initial candidate shortlist, and stem-graph matching remains weak even with LiDAR depth.
