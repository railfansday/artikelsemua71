Latest Update: Variance-Optimal Counterfactual Policy Design for Sample-Efficient Online A/B Testing

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (275 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.24757v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Online A/B testing is the standard approach for evaluating changes to user-facing systems, such as a new recommendation or ranking policy, before deployment. While the difference-in-means estimator of a standard A/B test is unbiased, it often suffers from high variance. When the treatment and production policies choose the same actions with similar probabilities, the resulting observations can add noise while providing little information about the difference between the policies. Consequently, A/B tests can require large amounts of traffic and long durations to detect small treatment effects. Existing variance-reduction methods mainly improve the estimator after the data-collection scheme has been fixed, and recent overlap-aware designs only optimize a single traffic split between the two policies; thereby, they cannot direct traffic to the actions on which the policies disagree. In this paper, we jointly optimize how actions are sampled and how the resulting observations are used to estimate the treatment effect. Our contributions are two-fold. First, building on pairwise off-policy evaluation, we derive the variance-optimal action-level logging policy for estimating the average treatment effect between two policies, which has a closed-form solution for any fixed control variate. Second, we use historical logs to jointly optimize the logging policy and a control variate to further reduce variance. Our experimental results in simulated policy A/B tests show that our designs estimate the average treatment effect more accurately than existing variance reduction methods on every evaluated setting, while generally improving statistical power and reducing how often the worse policy is selected. As a result, our designs enable practitioners to reach reliable deployment decisions with considerably less online traffic.
