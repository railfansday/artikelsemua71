Industry News: Corrupting Ties: Investigating Robustness to Bias Injection Across Multi-Agent Network Topologies

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (195 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.21804v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: AI systems are increasingly deployed in high-stakes decision-making contexts, where interactions among components can introduce fairness risks that remain undetected when auditing components or collective outcomes in isolation. As LLM-based multi-agent systems become more prevalent, understanding how biased information propagates through agent interactions is essential for assessing their fairness. We introduce a controlled bias injection framework that replaces selected agent responses with predefined biased ones, enabling an intervention-based analysis of how network topology and injection configurations shape system behavior. We propose decision divergence metrics that quantify the effects of these interventions at the agent, network, and system levels, and evaluate five topologies on three binary classification tasks using LLMs from the Qwen and Gemma families. Sequential networks show the greatest divergence, whereas fully connected and mesh networks show less. Injection position also matters: in star networks, injecting the central agent has larger effects than injecting peripheral agents. Models further differ in whether divergence follows the injected bias. These findings highlight the role of network structure in the propagation of biased information and motivate topology-aware auditing of fairness risks in multi-agent systems.
