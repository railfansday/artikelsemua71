Optimal Measurement Rounds for Perfect Encoded Fusion

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (216 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.22412v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Encoded fusion combines probabilistic physical fusions to perform a logical Bell measurement. Adaptive choices depend on earlier measurement outcomes and therefore require multiple measurement rounds. We determine the minimum worst-case number of rounds for perfect fusion of two blocks of an irreducible [[n,1]] stabilizer code. A strategy is perfect if the logical Bell measurement succeeds on every branch containing at least one successful physical fusion. Even after fixing the past history, including measurement values, we consider all success/failure branches, including those with zero probability for a particular input state or measurement device. In a lossless model with fixed coordinate pairing, arbitrary Pauli failure bases, and each pair fused exactly once, the optimum equals the minimum root eccentricity over the local complementation orbit of rooted graphs representing the code. The lower bound applies to arbitrary perfect strategies that choose batches and bases using previous outcomes. We also characterize the batch sequences that can occur on an all-failure branch of a perfect strategy by a necessary and sufficient graph adjacency condition. A matching strategy fuses distance layers from the outside in and, after the first success, completes the remaining fusions in at most one further round by changing the failure bases along an induced path.
