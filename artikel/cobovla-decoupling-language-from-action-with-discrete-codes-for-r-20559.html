CoBoVLA: Decoupling Language from Action with Discrete Codes for Robust and Interpretable Robot Manipulation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (272 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.22395v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models achieve high success rates on the LIBERO benchmark but remain sensitive to paraphrasing. Existing VLAs ranging from 0.6B to 7.5B parameters experience a 22-52 point performance drop on LIBERO-Para while SmolVLA exhibits an even larger decline, from 83\% to 8\%. Because most VLAs jointly learn language understanding and action generation, it is difficult to determine whether a failure originates from misunderstanding an instruction or executing it incorrectly. To address these challenges, we introduce CoBoVLA, or CodeBookVLA, a 250M-parameter VLA that decouples language understanding, planning, and control through learned discrete interpretable codes. A text encoder maps the instructions to shared step codes, which a visual planner then converts into action code sequences executed by a language-independent actor. This design improves robustness to instruction paraphrasing while allowing failures to be traced to individual stages. It also enables efficient real-time control on resource-constrained hardware by encoding instructions only once and using compact discrete representations to keep each component lightweight. CoBoVLA achieves 91.4\% success on LIBERO-Goal, compared with 82.8\% for SmolVLA, under a matched protocol, with 17 ms per control step latency. On LIBERO-Para, it achieves 81.1\% across 4,092 unseen paraphrases and 78.5\% on matched episodes, compared with 36.5\% for SmolVLA LoRA fine-tuned on paraphrases. Without two-step demonstrations, CoBoVLA completes 48.8\% of 160 free-form two-step tasks. Finally, success reaches 91\% when instructions are correctly encoded but falls to 27.2\% otherwise, identifying language understanding as the main source of remaining failures. Together, these results demonstrate that decoupling language understanding and control can improve instruction robustness while making VLA failures easier to diagnose.
