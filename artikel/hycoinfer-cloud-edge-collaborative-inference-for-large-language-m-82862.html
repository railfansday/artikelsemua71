HyCoInfer: Cloud-Edge Collaborative Inference for Large Language Models on Heterogeneous GPU-FPGA Architectures

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (214 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.22086v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large language model (LLM) serving relies predominantly on cloud GPUs, yet autoregressive decoding is memory-bound and often leaves their arithmetic units underutilized. Edge FPGAs offer attractive decoding efficiency, but are ill suited to sustaining a high-concurrency serving workload alone. This paper presents HyCoInfer, a heterogeneous cloud-edge serving system that assigns prefill to cloud GPUs and distributes decoding across cloud GPUs and edge FPGAs. The design addresses three barriers to practical offloading: capacity balancing across heterogeneous accelerators, the large KV cache crossing a constrained wide-area link, and the latency added by heterogeneous execution. HyCoInfer incorporates FPGA decode capacity into GPU prefill/decode role partitioning and places requests according to queue load, prompt length, and network state. It further combines layer-wise pipelined transfer, decoder-side precomputation, and bandwidth-adaptive KV cache compression based on residual vector quantization. We implement HyCoInfer using two NVIDIA V100 GPUs and an AMD Xilinx Alveo U280 and evaluate it over 100-1000 Mbps links. At 500 Mbps, HyCoInfer delivers up to 4.11 times the request goodput of cloud-only vLLM and reduces mean end-to-end latency by up to 61.1%. Across different network conditions and workloads, the system improves overall serving capacity, with the largest gains on communication-intensive tasks, and reduces per-request energy.
