What Does Weight-Space Programming Add to Diffusion Editing? Program Construction versus Score-Guided Refinement

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (210 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22228v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Weight-space programming (WSP) lets a language model construct executable weight updates for another network. We study diffusion concept editing through an interface in which programs define training contexts, preservation constraints and enhancement directions, while a shared numerical fitter selects their coefficients. We compare generated programs with published editors, a shared-panel hand-written baseline and budget-matched search over conventional edits on 24 TIMED test edits for Stable Diffusion 1.4. The preregistered rewrite pipeline highlights no advantage over the strong baseline. In an exploratory comparison, one-shot Astra programs improve fresh-scene VLM joint success, which requires subject presence together with whole-image target matching, by +10.0 points over the shared-panel baseline, while CLIP relative match shows no detectable change. These programs organise adjustable directions around protected text-state subspaces. Probing and rewriting the Astra programs then raises CLIP by +6.2 points while joint success changes by -9.7. On a fixed 144-image sample, five additional evaluators give point estimates in the same directions under whole-image target matching; checks of subject-local change give less decisive differences. Together, these results identify useful behaviour in complete generated programs and show that score-guided revision need not preserve it: program construction and revision should be evaluated as distinct stages of a WSP pipeline.
