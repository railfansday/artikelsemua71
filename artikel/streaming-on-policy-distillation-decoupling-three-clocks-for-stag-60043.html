Streaming On-Policy Distillation: Decoupling Three Clocks for Stage-Wise Supervision Consumption

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (189 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22298v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: In on-policy distillation (OPD), response completion, supervision delivery, and parameter updates need not share the same granularity. We develop streaming OPD by decoupling these three clocks: a response repeatedly delivers newly generated segments while generation continues, retaining the full causal prefix at each delivery and computing loss only on new positions. A consumption protocol then organizes supervision budgets, position selection, staleness handling, and patch scheduling, allowing control over quantity, coverage, and version lag across training stages. In GSM8K experiments distilling Qwen3-4B into Qwen3-0.6B with VERL, supervised-budget matching reduces the early gap between best test accuracies over seven common candidates from 4.55 to 2.42 points. Synchronous continuation from the same checkpoint reaches 73.39% best test accuracy over seven common candidates at u=384,...,576, versus 74.30% synchronous and 71.95% budget-matched (differences -0.91 and -2.35 points); an unpaired extended record reaches 76.12%. Freshness filtering and disagreement-overlap selection improve local peaks but do not establish sustained advantages. A system prototype indicates lower data-supply waiting times without establishing an equal-update speedup. Quality recovery and supply-side benefits are assessed separately; end-to-end gains must account for continuation cost.
