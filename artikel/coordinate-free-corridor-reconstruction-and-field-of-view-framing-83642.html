Coordinate-Free Corridor Reconstruction and Field-of-View Framing Attribution for 3D Bilateral Coronary Artery Trees in CT Angiography

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.05900v5 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Bilateral coronary tree extraction from cardiac computed tomography angiography (CCTA) must transfer from cropped benchmarks to uncropped scans without territory swaps or lattice staircases. Using 35 training scans, we fix a coordinate-free three-dimensional network (597,032 parameters) and a flush-admissible 8.0 mm corridor solver refined by a learned medial offset. We evaluate once on 50 ImageCAS scans against Lee94 thinning skeletons and zero-shot on 40 ASOCA scans (20 healthy, 20 diseased; 80 trees) against Vascular Modeling Toolkit (VMTK) Voronoi consensus centerlines. On uncropped pericardial ASOCA scans, our method leads all comparators on per-artery Chamfer distance (6.90 mm automatic despite 14.22 mm ostium error; 7.05 vs. 9.11 mm seeded, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0047$). It also leads 1 mm centerline F1 (51.42% and 51.56%, +13.21 to +29.08 percentage points [pp], $p_{\text{Holm}} \le 9.0 \times 10^{-4}$) at $1.66 \pm 0.07$ s/scan on an NVIDIA L4 graphics processor. In cropped ImageCAS boxes, it leads F1 (76.98%) while trailing 18--22 mm bridgers on mean distance (3.37 vs. 2.72--3.20 mm; none significant after Holm). Pitch-matched 1.19 mm zero-clutter controls show coarse pitch degrades matched U-Nets whereas fixed-frontend distance reversal requires pericardial clutter. Flush collar gating rescues 30/32 seeded ASOCA stubs (-7.97 mm), and the learned medial offset raises node (+2.72 to +5.11 pp; bias-corrected and accelerated 95% confidence interval [+2.42, +3.01] on ImageCAS) and curve (+1.87 to +4.40 pp) F1. Short corridors curb pericardial chaining while learned offsets lift centerlines off the lattice.
