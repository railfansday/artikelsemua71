RESCUE Jumper: A Centimeter-Scale Rolling and Jumping Robot for Search and Rescue

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (255 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23531v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Centimeter-scale robots are promising for urban search and rescue because they can enter confined spaces that are inaccessible or unsafe for humans. However, their small size limits obstacle traversal after deployment. This paper presents the RESCUE Jumper, a 125 g mobile robot with a 6.2 cm width and 6.2 cm diameter wheels that combines ground rolling with jumping to traverse steps and gaps that exceeds its rolling capability. The RESCUE Jumper uses its two actuators for both rolling mode and jumping by reversing the direction of wheel motion. Forward wheel motion enables rolling, while reverse wheel motion winds a spring steel tail to store elastic potential energy and release it for jumping. The RESCUE Jumper varies tail winding speed to control jump direction, transitioning from backward to forward jumps as winding speed increases from 0.75 rad/s to 8.73 rad/s. The launch angle of the jumps ranges from 45 degree to -11 degree with respect to the vertical axis, reaching a maximum height of 9.2 cm and a maximum lateral displacement of 11.2 cm. We model the winding speed-dependent jumping behavior in three phases: tail release to determine the contact point and robot orientation, tail straightening until liftoff, and ballistic flight to predict jump height and lateral displacement. Finally, qualitative tests highlight the robot's locomotion in cluttered, real-world environments. The model and experiments presented in this work can inform future designs of space-constrained jump mechanisms in centimeter-scale robots for search and rescue and inspection in unstructured environments.
