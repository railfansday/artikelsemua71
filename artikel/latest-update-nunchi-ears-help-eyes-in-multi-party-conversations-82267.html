Latest Update: Nunchi: Ears Help Eyes in Multi-Party Conversations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (197 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.23115v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Identifying whom a person looks at is central to understanding multi-party conversations, yet gaze geometry alone becomes ambiguous as more people appear. Conversations offer cues that geometry lacks, such as who is speaking and what is noted, but no gaze dataset provides them for many multi-party scenes. We introduce SORBET, a benchmark of 3,340 samples from 1,578 clips with synchronized speech, transcripts, and attended person labels, covering more multi-party scenes than existing gaze datasets. Prior gaze models struggle to use these cues, which come in different forms, must be linked to the right person, and can conflict with gaze geometry. We propose Nunchi, a framework that constructs a text prompt from the transcript and the outputs of gaze and speaker models, links every cue to the candidates with shared labels, and lets a vision-language model (VLM) weigh gaze geometry against conversational cues to identify the attended person. Nunchi outperforms the strongest existing method by 4.8 percentage points. On SORBET, conversational cues help whereas additional visual cues do not, and the gains hold across three VLMs, with automatic transcripts, and on two existing datasets.
