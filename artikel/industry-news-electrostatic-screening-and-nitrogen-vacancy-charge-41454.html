Industry News: Electrostatic screening and nitrogen-vacancy charge states in boron-doped/nitrogen-doped diamond junctions

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (190 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.22074v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Boron-doped diamond (BDD) enables the integration of electrical functionality with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers, but the influence of junction electrostatics on NV charge-state stability remains unclear. In this study, we investigate the equilibrium electrostatics of BDD/nitrogen-doped diamond junctions using self-consistent Poisson-Fermi calculations. The results reveal two distinct electrostatic length scales in the nitrogen-doped substrate: a near-interface region of ionized donors that produces dominant band bending and determines the NV$^{0/-}$ charge-state boundary, and a much longer screening tail associated with weak donor ionization in the neutral bulk. The resulting NV$^-$ depletion depth depends strongly on the nitrogen concentration but exhibits a more moderate dependence on boron doping. At $N_B = 10^{21}$ cm$^{-3}$, varying the interface grading scale $w$ from 0.5 to 8 nm changes the depletion depth by approximately 22 nm, whereas variations in the modeled bulk BDD charge response produce a shift of only 1.27 nm. These results indicate that, under the conditions studied, the nitrogen donor concentration and local interface structure primarily govern the equilibrium NV charge-state profile, rather than the bulk electronic response of BDD alone.
