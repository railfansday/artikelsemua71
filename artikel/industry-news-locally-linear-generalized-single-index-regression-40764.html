Industry News: Locally Linear Generalized Single Index Regression

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (267 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22011v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: In this work we propose and analyze a procedure for estimating a generalized single-index regression model. This generalized single-index regression framework models the conditional mean of the responses $Y$ as a function of the covariates $X$ where the mapping is assumed to have the form $f \circ g$, where $g$ is $\beta$-H\"older with $\beta \geq 2$ and $f$ can be a discontinuous function of bounded variation. We propose a general procedure that operates under minimal assumptions about the distribution of covariates $X$, requiring only boundedness, sufficient smoothness and absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue. Our procedure jointly estimates the gradient vector $\hat v(x) \approx \nabla g(x)$ and the conditional mean $\mu(x) = f(g(x))$ by solving a localized weighted linear least squares problem. We derive central limit theorems for gradient vector estimates $\hat{v}_x$ and regression estimates $\hat{\mu}(x)$, and we derive rates of convergence for our approach, illustrating that it indeed achieves a stochastic convergence rate matching the minimax mean squared error rate when $\beta = 2$ and $f$ has a locally Lipschitz derivative. Unlike prior approaches our velocity estimation results accommodate link functions in the local bounded-variation class, allowing for nonsmooth and even discontinuous links while retaining consistency and asymptotic normality at almost every inference point. Finally, we instantiate this model in the situation where $g = \Pi$ is a closest point projection onto a compact and regular embedded $C^3$ curve $\gamma$, a setting recently studied in the literature. We also supplement our theoretical results with experiments on real and synthetic data to demonstrate the utility of our procedure.
