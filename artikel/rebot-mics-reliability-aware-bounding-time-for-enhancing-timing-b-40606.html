ReBoT-MiCS: Reliability-Aware Bounding Time for Enhancing Timing Behavior of Embedded Mixed-Criticality Systems

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (233 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.22588v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: In Mixed-Criticality (MC) systems, considering multiple Worst-Case Execution Times (WCETs) for the same task, corresponding to different system operation modes, presents a fundamental challenge in MC system design. Identifying suitable WCETs for lower (LO) criticality system operational modes is inherently difficult. Tight low WCET values may trigger frequent mode switches that degrade Quality-of-Service (QoS) by dropping/degrading the lower-criticality tasks and reducing their functionality. However, more conservative values reduce resource efficiency. Moreover, low WCET selection affects exposure assumptions under the adopted transient-fault model, which is used in reliability-aware scheduling analysis. Although some studies have considered low WCET determination to make a reasonable trade-off between processor utilization, mode switching probability and QoS, reliability constraints are typically addressed separately through fault-tolerance mechanisms. This separation overlooks the opportunity to use execution-time bounds as part of design-time reasoning about exposure-related reliability constraints. In this regard, we propose a reliability-aware analytical scheme, named ReBoT-MiCS, to determine low WCETs at design time, jointly balancing utilization, QoS, mode switches, and probabilistic reliability constraints of each task criticality level under a transient-fault exposure model. Experiments conducted with various synthetic and embedded benchmarks demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed scheme, in which utilization can be improved by up to 27.6% and 11.67% on average, while bounding the mode switching probability by up to 5.48%, and satisfying the specified reliability constraints.
