Geometry-bridge flow matching for blind rigid-motion correction in head cone-beam CT

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (224 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23788v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Rigid motion of a patient head during head cone-beam CT (CBCT) scan can degrade image quality and complicate recovery of accurate anatomical structures from the measured projections. We present a 3D flow-matching prior approach that learns the image changes induced by motion. Its geometry-bridge connects motion-corrupted and motion-free Feldkamp-Davis-Kress (FDK) reconstructed images of the same source volume through progressively reduced simulated motion. The forward projector's geometry derivative supplies analytic velocity targets along this path. A plug-and-play loop encompasses the learned image updates together with spline-based pose estimation, conjugate-gradient data consistency, and image total-variation regularization. Both the refined image volume and the FDK reconstructed image volume at the estimated poses are evaluated. On simulated 360-view scans from 30 held-out CQ500 patients with nominal per-component motion amplitudes within 10 mm / 10 degrees, the framework achieved state-of-the-art image reconstruction quality and motion accuracy in comparison with other methos such as learned autofocus and diffusion-based joint reconstruction. Mean peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) reached 37.1 dB and mean detector-plane reprojection error (RPE) was 0.27 mm. With matched endpoints at the inference settings, the geometry bridge improved PSNR by 1.6 dB and reduced RPE by 33% relative to image-linear training. With 50 prior updates and coarse-to-fine motion estimation, total processing time averaged 9.1 minutes per patient on an RTX A6000.
