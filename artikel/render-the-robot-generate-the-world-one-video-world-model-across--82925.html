Render the Robot, Generate the World: One Video World Model Across Embodiments

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (217 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.24925v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Predicting the consequences of an agent's actions requires modeling both the body's motion and the environment's response. Most video world models attempt to learn both from numerical actions, even though geometry and kinematics already specify the intended motion. We introduce Doppel, an embodiment-agnostic video world model conditioned on camera-aligned renderings of future body configurations and numerical action trajectories. By explicitly providing the intended motion, Doppel can focus on predicting object motion and interaction-driven changes in the environment. Rendered motion also provides a common visual interface across different embodiments and action spaces. To train on robot datasets without camera extrinsics, we recover camera poses and align rendered trajectories with recorded observations. We jointly train a single shared video backbone and conditioning adapter on eight datasets spanning eight robot and human embodiments, from single- and bimanual robot arms to a dexterous humanoid and human hands and bodies, using about 600 hours of video without embodiment labels. Across three robot-arm datasets, Doppel improves PSNR over action-only baselines by 1.00 dB on held-out trajectories and 1.20 dB under out-of-distribution evaluation, while predicting more consistent object motion. Training on all eight embodiments (Doppel-8) improves robot predictions over the three-robot model (Doppel-3) on four of six evaluation sets. Project page: https://doppelwm.github.io/.
