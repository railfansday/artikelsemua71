Analysis of a Homodyne-Like Measurement Scheme Using Photon Counters and Weak Local Oscillator

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (247 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23968v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: In standard homodyne detection, an unknown optical signal state is combined with a local oscillator (LO) at a beam splitter (BS), and the appropriately integrated intensities at the two outputs of the BS are subtracted to obtain an approximate quadrature measurement. Accurate quadrature measurements require a high-amplitude LO and high-efficiency detectors. By varying the LO phase, the optical state in the mode matching the LO can be reconstructed. In many practical settings, a high-intensity LO is not desirable, which has led to the exploration of homodyne-like measurement schemes that replace photodiodes with photon-counting detectors and use an LO with a low average photon number. In this paper, we investigate what features of the unknown state can be determined by counting the total-photon number in one or both outgoing paths after the BS, keeping the mode of the LO fixed but adjusting its phase and magnitude. We find that it is possible to determine the unknown optical state in the mode matching the LO conditional on the number of photons in the orthogonal modes on the same path. If both the phase and the intensity of the LO can be varied, the statistics of just one of the detectors is sufficient to infer these parameters, but both detectors are required when the LO has a fixed intensity. Our analysis is carried out in the infinite-data limit, assuming access to the full photon-number distribution from one or both detectors.
