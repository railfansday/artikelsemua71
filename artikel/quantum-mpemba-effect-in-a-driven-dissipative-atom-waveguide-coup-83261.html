Quantum Mpemba effect in a driven-dissipative atom-waveguide coupled system

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (239 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.23971v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: The quantum Mpemba effect (QME) describes the counterintuitive phenomenon in which a more strongly perturbed quantum system relaxes toward its steady state faster than a less perturbed one. Here, we investigate the QME in a weakly driven-dissipative atomic array coupled to a one-dimensional chiral waveguide. Starting from the steady state, we introduce a local quench by resetting a single atom to its ground state and examine the subsequent relaxation dynamics. We find that quenching atoms near the array boundaries induces faster initial relaxation through enhanced coupling to superradiant modes and direct photon emission into the waveguide, giving rise to an edge-enhanced QME. Strong directional decay further amplifies this effect by promoting the spatial accumulation of superradiant modes near the preferred boundary, leading to a skin-enhanced QME. At later times, relaxation is increasingly governed by long-lived subradiant modes localized predominantly in the bulk of the array. By coupling two atomic arrays with different interparticle spacings, we further demonstrate an interface-induced QME, where quenching an atom near the interface makes the system relax more slowly at early times but approach its steady state faster at later times, owing to weak participation in both superradiant and subradiant modes. Our findings reveal how the spatial structure of collective decay modes and directional dissipation jointly govern anomalous quantum relaxation, offering potential routes toward accelerated steady-state preparation in waveguide QED systems.
