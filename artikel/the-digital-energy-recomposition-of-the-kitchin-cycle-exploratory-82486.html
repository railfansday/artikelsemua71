The Digital-Energy Recomposition of the Kitchin Cycle: Exploratory Evidence from U.S. IT and Irish Data-Centre Series (1995-2025)

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (246 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.33122v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Using quarterly FRED and CSO Ireland data over 1995Q1-2025Q4, we examine whether U.S.\ IT investment and Irish data-centre electricity are recomposing the Kitchin cycle around energy- and compute-backed assets. Three findings stand out. First, a Quandt-Andrews sup-Wald test locates a significant break in the U.S.\ IT-GDP correlation at 2014Q3 (sup-W $= 9.69$; $r$ falls from $+0.45$ to $-0.12$); the Fisher $r$-to-$z$ test survives Bonferroni adjustment ($z = +3.11$, $p_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.006$), whereas the analogous test on the imposed Cloud-vs-AI boundary does not ($p_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.164$), so ``Strategic Decoupling'' pre-dates the AI Era by approximately six years. Second, in Ireland the synchronous AI-Era correlation between metered data-centre electricity and Real GDP is large ($r = +0.41$, $N = 16$) but \emph{collapses to $r \approx 0$ when Real GDP is replaced by Real GNP}, suggesting the headline is largely an artefact of the shared multinational tax-structure footprint between hyperscaler operations and Irish gross output. Third, the Irish cross-correlation profile is asymmetric in the opposite direction to a leading-indicator reading: the largest positive coefficients cluster on the $k < 0$ side (GDP precedes data-centre load), so compute capacity is better read as coincident or moderately lagging. Given the small AI-Era samples and the absence of a quarterly multinational-adjusted aggregate such as GNI*, all coefficients are exploratory; subject to these caveats, the U.S.\ Kitchin cycle is best dated to 2014Q3, and the Irish Energy-Data Nexus is dominated by multinational firm activity.
