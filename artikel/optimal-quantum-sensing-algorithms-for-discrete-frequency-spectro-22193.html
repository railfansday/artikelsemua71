Optimal Quantum Sensing Algorithms for Discrete-Frequency Spectroscopy

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (231 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.21560v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Spectroscopy is a versatile tool that drives scientific discovery and underpins modern technologies. Quantum sensing offers promising opportunities to advance spectroscopy through enhanced sensitivity and spectral and spatial resolution. However, existing approaches largely focus on improving general measurement capabilities, often overlooking the structure of specific sensing tasks and leaving potential performance gains untapped. Here, we develop an algorithmic approach to quantum sensing tailored to various spectroscopic tasks, providing latest avenues for improvement. Specifically, we consider general time-varying signals supported on a known set of $N$ candidate frequencies, a setting we call discrete-frequency spectroscopy. We formulate three types of practically motivated sensing tasks---Detection, Search, and Learning---that require progressively more spectral information. For all tasks, we design optimal quantum sensing algorithms achieving a $\sqrt{N}$ speedup over conventional frequency-scanning approaches. Optimal Detection and Search need only a single-qubit sensor under $\pi$-pulse control, whereas optimal Learning provably requires coherent processing with a quantum computer. End-to-end numerical simulations incorporating various experimental imperfections predict up to two-orders-of-magnitude speedup in Detection. As an example, we analyze the feasibility of our Detection and Search algorithms for real-world molecular screening based on state-of-the-art nitrogen-vacancy centers. We also show how quantum-computing-enhanced Learning enables wireless communication in a weak-signal regime where conventional methods fail. Our results establish a practical route toward realizing quantum advantages via designing task-specific sensing algorithms.
