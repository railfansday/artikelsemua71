Towards Physics-Constrained Rectified Flow for Climate Downscaling: What Projections Can Certify?

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (272 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.22283v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Statistical downscaling restores the regional detail behind flooding and crop stress, but diffusion and flow-matching generators can produce sharp fields that break the conservation laws of their coarse input. Physics-Constrained Rectified Flow (PC-RF) is a conditional flow-matching downscaler. Its training penalties target wind divergence, negative precipitation, and mass drift from the coarse total, and a projection after sampling removes the last two exactly. On one corrupted synthetic field with no trained network, the projection drives the Non-negativity Violation Rate (NVR) from 0.236 and the Mass-Conservation Error (MCE) from 0.184 to zero, and the penalties lower the Divergence Error (DE) by about 30%. On ERA5 the projection gives PC-RF and RF-base, the penalty-free rectified-flow baseline, an NVR of exactly zero and an MCE of zero up to float32 rounding. The penalties add no perceptual gain that we can separate from training budget or run-to-run variation. At a matched budget on 2019-2021, PC-RF has no edge over RF-base (SSIM 0.939 vs. 0.942, PSNR 31.81 vs. 31.79, CRPS 0.771 vs. 0.772). Our unprojected CorrDiff reimplementation beats both flow models on PSNR (28.38) and CRPS (1.092) on 2020, yet 45% of its ensemble-mean precipitation cells are negative. On ERA5 a sign error in the divergence operator made that penalty and DE act on a deformation-like term. The fine reference is interpolated from ERA5 and accumulates precipitation over a longer window, so the perceptual scores cannot establish downscaling skill. NVR and MCE need one pass and no fine reference, so we argue they should run first as pass-or-fail checks, with DE as a diagnostic.
