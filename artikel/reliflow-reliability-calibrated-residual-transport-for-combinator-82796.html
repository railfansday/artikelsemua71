ReliFlow: Reliability-Calibrated Residual Transport for Combinatorial Single-Cell Perturbation Prediction

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (169 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.23999v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Predicting the transcriptional response to combined perturbations requires distinguishing interactions between interventions from errors in their individual effect estimates. The latter become more pronounced when single-perturbation evidence is sparse or withheld. We introduce a conditional flow-matching model that estimates the reliability of single-gene effects from training-only cross-fitting and uses those estimates to calibrate the additive source distribution. The velocity is decomposed into a known correction of the additive reference and a learned residual transport field. On Norman genetic perturbations, reduces mean absolute error (MAE) from 0.142 to 0.131 for unseen combinations of observed singles and from 0.198 to 0.178 when constituent singles are withheld. On held-out ComboSciPlex drug combinations, MAE decreases from 0.191 to 0.176. Non-additive residual correlation improves from 0.574 to 0.638, and component ablations identify the contribution of reliability weighting. The results indicate that accounting for uncertain constituent effects improves generalization to unseen genetic and chemical combinations, with a moderate increase in training cost.
