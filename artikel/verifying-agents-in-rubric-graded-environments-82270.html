Verifying Agents in Rubric-Graded Environments

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (213 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22841v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: As AI agents take on increasingly open-ended and complex tasks, verifying their outputs has proven to be difficult. Rubric-graded agent environments, where a verifier judges multiple natural-language criteria against the deliverables and environment state changes produced by the agent, have emerged as a popular paradigm. We conduct the first systematic study of verifiers for rubric-graded agent environments. First, we create BankerVerifierBench (BVB), a meta-evaluation dataset of 2,464 human-judged rubric criteria across 24 investment-banking tasks. Next, we develop a methodology that derives from any rubric corpus the capabilities a verifier should possess, yielding a nine-capability taxonomy that we distill into three general design principles---reactive verification, environment alignment, and domain guidance---which we implement in Gandalf, an open-source verifier. Finally, we evaluate Gandalf against three existing verifiers on BVB. Gandalf matches or exceeds every baseline's F1 at lower cost: with Gemini 3 Flash it matches the strongest baseline's F1 (0.864 vs. 0.852) at one-fifth of its cost, and with GPT-5.4 models it improves F1 by over 0.12 at lower cost. Gandalf also generalizes to OpenClaw, a structurally different personal-productivity environment, where it again achieves the highest F1 (0.955 vs. 0.934 for the best baseline). We release BVB, Gandalf, and the code and data to reproduce our experiments.
