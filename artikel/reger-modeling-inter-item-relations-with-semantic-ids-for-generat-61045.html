REGER: Modeling inter-item Relations with Semantic IDs for Generative Recommendation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (243 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2610.37584v4 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Personalized recommendation depends on learning semantic and collaborative relations among items from online content and user interactions. Generative recommendation (GR) offers a promising paradigm by autoregressively generating discrete semantic IDs (SIDs) of items. However, randomly initialized SID token embeddings and GR models trained from scratch do not directly inherit the semantic relations inherent in items, while inter-item collaborative relations are learned largely implicitly through token-level attention and next-token supervision. To address this limitation, we propose REGER, a unified framework that explicitly incorporates inter-item relations into semantic pretraining, history encoding, and SID generation. We pretrain the GR model through generative semantic retrieval, enabling it to inherit inter-item semantic similarity by learning to generate semantic neighbors' SIDs. We combine global attention with local attention aligned with SID boundaries to capture historical context and directed transitions between adjacent items. We formalize item-level preference distillation on the SID prefix tree, deriving an exact factorization of item preference distributions into prefix conditionals and a weighted decomposition of item-level KL divergence into conditional KL terms. Guided by this decomposition, we renormalize the generator's probabilities over legal next tokens and match the resulting distributions to the teacher's conditional preferences along the ground-truth SID path, jointly optimizing the distillation objective with cross-entropy. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets validate the effectiveness of the proposed preference distillation and demonstrate that REGER consistently outperforms existing GR baselines while retaining efficient inference.
