MitoWorld: A Stochastic World Model of the Single-Cell Cycle under Genetic Perturbation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (207 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.23953v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: A cell observed after a gene knockdown has an uncertain future, both in how its appearance will evolve and when it will divide. Existing approaches predict endpoints or population-level changes without jointly modeling individual trajectories and division timing. In this work, we present MitoWorld, a stochastic world model that generates these future appearances as continuous evolution in a Gaussian reference latent, terminated by a division hazard and converted into images by a conditional diffusion renderer. The ordinary transition provably preserves its Gaussian reference, tying its noise to its dynamics; paired observations identify its discrete transition where stationary marginals cannot. The hazard turns division timing into a survival likelihood that can be tested against real cycle lengths. On 325 held-out MitoCheck tracks spanning 17 knockdowns and a control, MitoWorld lowers FID by 34.6\% and the video metric JEDi by 45.6\% relative to the strongest baselines. Its latent-space prediction intervals achieve near-nominal 90\% coverage, are 35\% narrower than those of a model with freely fitted noise. The hazard also yields cell-cycle length distributions with KS distance close to the model-based finite-sample reference. Testable single-cell stochastic dynamics thus offer a generative tool for time-lapse perturbation screens.
