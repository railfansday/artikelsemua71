Breaking: Physics-Aware Zero-Shot Learning for Low-Dose CT from a Single Sinogram

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (217 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2504.07927v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Low-dose computed tomography (CT) imaging remains a persistent challenge, especially under heterogeneous acquisition conditions. Zero-shot learning, which enables models to learn directly from individual images without external supervision, represents a compelling approach to mitigate distribution mismatches arising from variations in scanner hardware, imaging protocols, and patient demographics. However, most existing zero-shot approaches employ down-sampling operations that reduce spatial resolution and cause domain shifts, which become particularly evident when applied to raw sinogram data. Here, we propose a physics-aware zero-shot CT denoising framework to exploit the conjugate symmetry property intrinsic to CT sinograms. Conjugate X-ray pencil beams traverse identical anatomical paths and ideally yield the same expected attenuation values but with independent noise realizations. By randomly mapping sinogram values between conjugate-ray pairs, we synthesize structurally consistent yet noise-diverse sinogram pairs, enabling self-supervised network training without external data. The resulting lightweight denoising network demonstrates strong generalization across low-dose simulation, clinical helical CT, and preclinical photon-counting CT datasets. Quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate competitive denoising performance, with favorable noise suppression and structural preservation across diverse acquisition settings. This work establishes a generalizable and data-efficient paradigm for CT denoising that integrates physical redundancy into learning-based image reconstruction. The code for the proposed method is openly available at: https://github.com/shiyongyi/Physics-Aware-Zero-Shot-CT-Sinogram-Denoising.
