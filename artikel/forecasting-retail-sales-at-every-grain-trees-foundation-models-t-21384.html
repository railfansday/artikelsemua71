Forecasting Retail Sales at Every Grain: Trees, Foundation Models, Transformers and World Models Compared on Four Public Data Sets

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (292 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2610.25170v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: A retailer makes decisions from the same demand at different grains: store orders by item and day, promotions by item and week, category reviews and supplier terms. This paper asks whether one forecast, built at the finest grain and summed to every coarser grain, can serve them all, and which model does that best. It compares LightGBM, pretrained foundation models (TimesFM, Chronos-Bolt, Timestral), the Temporal Fusion Transformer and a world model that carries each item's state forward under the price and promotion strategy, on M5, six Dominick's categories, Breakfast at the Frat and Corporacion Favorita, with the world model and LightGBM tuned by a common search. When to use the average: the average of the tuned world model and LightGBM, summed to every grain, has the lowest error in 22 of 27 grain and data set cases and is never the least accurate on the final held-out periods. When to use the world model: on existing items kept out of training it loses at most 0.040 in scaled error, against up to 0.311 for LightGBM, and on lines with short history it is clearly more accurate on six of nine data sets. When to use LightGBM: on heavily promoted lines, fast sellers and lines with long history (six of nine data sets each) and in price-cut weeks. Inconclusive: lines never promoted and slow lines are close to even, and 52 weeks ahead the average is the most accurate on five of seven data sets, with the world model ahead of LightGBM on two of seven. The findings hold for these data sets and the price and promotion levers; anyone adopting them should rerun every comparison on their own data.
