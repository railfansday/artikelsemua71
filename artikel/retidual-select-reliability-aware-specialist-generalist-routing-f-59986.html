RetiDual-Select: Reliability-Aware Specialist-Generalist Routing for Retinal OCT under Image-Quality Shift

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (250 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.22117v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Adaptive fusion can repair a static ensemble's errors, but can also overturn correct decisions. RetiDual-Select combines retinal specialist RETFound with the image encoder of the medical vision-language model MedSigLIP, separating candidate fusion, routing safety and predictive review. Calibrated product-of-experts (PoE) fusion is the static reference, a small gate proposes an alternative for each image, a validation-frozen harm guard decides whether that candidate may replace the reference, and a separate score prioritizes review. Static PoE reached the highest or joint-highest clean accuracy on all three datasets. At a clean 2% accepted-error target the review policy accepted 94.3% of Kermany-4 images at 99.89% selective accuracy, and 92.4% of OCT-C8 at 98.65%. Both branches were correct on 95.0% and 91.8% of clean images; only 4.4% and 6.2% had exactly one correct branch. Across 15 prespecified synthetic conditions, adaptive gains were concentrated in severe contrast reduction, where this routing opportunity widened roughly ninefold; percentile rescaling largely removed the effect, linking it to compressed dynamic range. In post-hoc validation-only analyses, gain was higher when the gate followed the more reliable branch, and a frozen guard cut the worst-condition OCT-C8 loss from 1.39 to 0.36 percentage points. Adaptive routing was useful when image quality created a reliability opportunity and the gate followed it; otherwise the system retained Static PoE or deferred for review. The findings are bounded by the synthetic perturbations evaluated and do not establish robustness to natural acquisition or scanner shift.
