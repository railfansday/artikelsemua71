Evaluating Semantic Insensitivity in Financial Sentiment Analysis: Implications for Sustainable Financial Decision-Making

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (180 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.21653v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Financial sentiment analysis is critical for automated trading, portfolio management, and risk assessment, heavily relying on models like FinBERT. Despite high benchmark accuracy, these models remain vulnerable to subtle adversarial text modifications, threatening the integrity of algorithmic decision-making. In this paper, we propose Agent-driven Semantic Inversion (ASI), a novel adversarial attack framework targeting financial sentiment models through domain-specific semantic manipulation. Utilizing an LLM-powered multi-agent pipeline, our approach identifies eligible financial news sentences and applies targeted lexical substitutions. We empirically evaluate ASI against FinBERT, one of the most widely deployed financial sentiment models. Our results reveal a striking vulnerability: the attack successfully deceived FinBERT in 90.85% of eligible cases, with the model frequently maintaining its original sentiment prediction despite the fundamental inversion of financial performance indicators. Through qualitative analysis and token-level investigation, we demonstrate that FinBERT critically over-relies on contextual keywords and syntactic patterns rather than true logical comprehension of financial semantics. Our findings highlight the urgent need for logic-aware, robust financial NLP models to ensure the reliability of automated decision-making systems.
