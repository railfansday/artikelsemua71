Subsystem evolution in the history state formalism

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (210 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23784v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: We present a relational framework for characterizing the evolution of quantum composite systems, based on the correlations within a history state describing the whole evolution relative to a quantum reference clock. While total system-clock entanglement entropy consistently measures the unitary evolution of the closed global system, a different picture emerges for the reduced state of an open subsystem, whose evolution is generally non-unitary due to environmental coupling. We introduce a general formalism that quantifies open-subsystem dynamics using a subsystem-clock mutual information derived from the history state. We demonstrate that this quantity serves as a consistent quantifier of subsystem evolution with respect to a given clock basis, can be applied to arbitrary dynamics, and reduces to system-time entanglement entropy in the global limit. Furthermore, maximizing this measure over all clock bases yields a basis-independent bound on the maximum evolution achievable relative to the clock, directly linked to the difference between the subsystem's entanglement with the rest of the history state and its entanglement with the environment. Finally, we derive analytical expressions for qubit clocks, illustrate our results using spin 1/2 and spin 1 quantum walks, and present a full matrix formalism of Kraus operators for describing the most general subsystem evolution.
