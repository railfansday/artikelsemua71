Fe$^{2+}$:forsterite Qubit System

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (188 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23550v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Quantum Computing uses quantum mechanical phenomena, such as superposition and entanglement of quantum bits (qubits), to represent and manipulate information in multidimensional state spaces. This approach allows complex mathematical functions to be executed simultaneously rather than sequentially, making them much more efficient than classical computers. However, current quantum computers suffer from quantum decoherence, where small changes to their environment, such as thermal fluctuations and vibrations, affect the systems' fragile states. The goal of this study is to discuss the functionality of a proposed Fe$^{2+}$:forsterite qubit system by solving the unitary time-evolution of a quantum state vector for the system's Hamiltonian, using known axial and traverse anisotropy terms at temperatures up to 10 K. The system can effectively transfer population between its two lowest states at rates predicted by the Rabi half-period, with 99.9% fidelity. Additionally, it can hold the population at a desired state without docohering. The results remained consistent for temperatures up to 10 K, meaning, unlike many common qubit systems, the proposed qubit system could function without a dilution fridge and is not fragile to thermal noise.
