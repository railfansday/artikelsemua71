Industry News: From syllable representation via gesture generation to articulation: A spiking neural model

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (232 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22499v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Speech production requires a transformation from phonological representations into coordinated vocal-tract movements. This paper presents a spiking neural sensorimotor model within the Neural Engineering Framework and Semantic Pointer Architecture. Each syllable occurrence has a neural oscillator whose evolving phase coordinates its gestures and initiates the next syllable. Phase-dependent commands recruit separate neural populations for vocalic, oral-constriction, velopharyngeal, and glottal actions (gestures). Neural ramp integrators shape the rise and fall of gesture activation. Together with articulatory targets, these trajectories determine the extent and time course of articulatory movements. Opponent neural populations provide control signals to a geometric articulatory model. Thirty reference recordings cover an input corpus of eight lexical words, an alternative realization of one word, a syllable sequence, and isolated syllables. Two additional paired simulation subcorpora of sixty runs each, covering two words and one syllable sequence, assess activation reliability across neural realizations. Adjusting gesture command amplitudes increased successful realizations from 57/60 to 60/60 under the specified criteria, with ramp rate factors held constant. The model demonstrates how neural oscillator and ramp dynamics generate articulatory timing under specified phasing rules. It complements a preceding mental-lexicon model through an ordered syllable interface. Continuous online execution across all components and connection to the acoustic extension remain further integration steps. Accessible code and synchronized visualization support inspection of the neural activation trajectories and resulting vocal-tract movements.
