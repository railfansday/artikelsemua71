Breaking: A Generative Model for Higher-Order Network Dynamics

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (246 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24638v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Conventional network representations inferred from multivariate time series describe statistical relationships between pairs of components, even when the underlying dynamics involve group interactions that cannot be represented by pairwise interactions alone. Several multivariate information-theoretic measures have been proposed to characterise dependence beyond pairs. However, due to a lack of systems with known generating mechanisms, these measures have not been carefully validated or compared for empirical use. To address this, we here introduce a generative model of stochastic network dynamics with pairwise and higher-order interactions specified by the user. The higher-order interactions are represented by directed hyperedges, and the model provides a controlled benchmark for evaluating information-theoretic measures. Removing only the higher-order term gives a pairwise-only reference. The experiments test whether a measure detects changes induced by the higher-order term relative to this reference and recovers the participating nodes and their source-target roles. A separate experiment tests discrimination between a product coupling and a specified nonlinear pairwise control, with equal root-mean-square coupling magnitude under the pairwise-only reference. Several measures detected sufficiently strong higher-order interactions across the tested network topologies and coupling functions. In the separate discrimination experiment, the measures also separated the product coupling from the specified nonlinear pairwise control. Recovery, however, became harder as the number of candidate interactions grew. The framework therefore provides a testbed where the link between higher-order signatures and mechanisms can be tested, rather than assumed, before measures are applied to empirical data.
