Asymmetric Optical Image Watermarking

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (226 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23350v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Watermark detectors held by a provider cannot be checked by third parties, and a party that can detect a mark can usually also forge it. We study a two-layer image watermark in which the Fourier transform at the core of the pipeline is performed by a coherent optical 4F unit. Layer 1 carries an Ed25519 signature bound to the image content in Fourier magnitudes, so anyone with the public key verifies offline. Layer 2 is a key-defined carrier written by a secret phase hologram and read by a matched-filter correlator. The optics is a scalar-diffraction simulation with finite modulator bits, pixel aperture, pupil, defocus, misalignment, and sensor noise. No hardware is built. All criteria were fixed in a hashed specification before any optical result. The simulated optical signer reproduces the digital pipeline (field error 1.7e-15, bit agreement 99.997%), verifies at least 99.5% of 877 images under each of 8 post-sensor settings, one unattacked, and accepts none of 40,670 unmarked images. The optical carrier is key-selective: the matched key detects it on 99.8% of the unattacked images, and detection falls to 0% under a scale error of 1% and to 0% under a mask shift of 1 bin. Security rests on the signature and the content binding, for which we separate what is provable from what is measured.
