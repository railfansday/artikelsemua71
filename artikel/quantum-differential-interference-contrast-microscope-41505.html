Quantum differential interference contrast microscope

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (195 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.22579v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Optical sensing and imaging of transparent unstained biological and material specimen makes use of optical interference to translate phase shifts into intensity measurements, that are readily available in the optical domain. This is the case of Differential Interference Contrast (DIC) microscopes. However, environmental disturbances such as acoustic vibrations, air currents, temperature shifts, and mechanical drifts cause random phase noise that degrades the precision attainable in the estimation of the unknown phase shift. Although there are tools to compensate for these random phase drifts, such using common path interference techniques or phase stabilization systems, these are not always an option and its introduction is generally at the expense of device simplicity and robustness. Here we introduce a quantum version of Differential Interference Contrast microscopy (qDIC), that makes use of quantum interference for polarization analysis. When compared with standard schemes that make use of coherent light, our approach reveals that the transfer of classical DIC microscopy into the quantum domain offers a route towards quantum-enhanced resilience to environmental disturbances, allowing the enhancement of phase sensitivity without sacrificing the simplicity and robustness of the optical setup.
