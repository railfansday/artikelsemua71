RoboTeam: Benchmarking Vision-Based Multi-Agent Control of Many Robot Arms in Real Time

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (264 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.22138v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multimodal agents can now control robot arms by calling tools and writing code. Existing benchmarks fall short in three ways: most test one or two arms on short tasks, many pause the world while the agent thinks, and many give it ground-truth object positions. We present RoboTeam, a simulated benchmark where agents control 4 to 16 robot arms in real time from camera images alone. Its 36 tasks are long jobs that the arms must do together, such as passing timbers or building a picture from hundreds of cubes. We start from the most flexible agent system, the adaptive system, where one agent decides when to fork delegates. With it, gpt-6.1-sol and opus-5.5 complete 28 and 27 tasks, and four other models at most 3. But at a high price: a typical task takes gpt-6.1-sol 3.5 hours and 218 million input tokens, and a third of its steps fail at least once and must be retried. We then compare it with three fixed systems: single-agent, hierarchical, and decentralized. All four complete a similar number of tasks but differ in time and tokens. The hierarchical and decentralized systems are faster but use up to 1.9 times the tokens, and the single agent uses fewer tokens but takes 1.6 times as long, so the adaptive system lies between them. Even so, no model or system is yet fast, cheap, and reliable at once. RoboTeam measures these limits where earlier benchmarks cannot: long multi-arm jobs, camera images only, and a world that never pauses.
