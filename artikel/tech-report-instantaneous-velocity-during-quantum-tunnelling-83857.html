Tech Report: Instantaneous velocity during quantum tunnelling

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (185 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2512.16385v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum tunneling, the phenomenon by which particles traverse regions forbidden by classical mechanics, underpins a range of processes including nuclear fusion, photosynthesis, and superconducting qubits. Its fundamental dynamics, however, remain heavily debated. A prominent open controversy concerns the apparent energy--speed paradox reported by Sharoglazova et al., where particles with lower incident energy exhibit a higher tunneling "speed". Here we resolve this paradox using a dynamical framework of tunneling. We show that, at the rising edge of the incident wavepacket, the particle velocity inside the barrier continuously decreases from an initially large value; it stays constant across the wavepacket plateau and reverses sign at the falling edge. In tandem with this velocity evolution, the in-barrier probability density gradually accumulates, holds steady over the plateau, and subsequently decays. We further derive closed-form expressions relating velocity and dwell time to barrier width and height, and investigate how coupling between internal and external states modulates the tunneling of massive particles. This dynamical picture reconciles the counterintuitive lower-energy--"higher-speed" paradox and provides a theoretical basis for time-resolved tunneling measurements.
