Industry News: ZERITH V2.5: From Predictive Power to Policy Learnability

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23746v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: When humans learn to manipulate an unfamiliar object, they may already be able to anticipate how its state will change while still being unable to control it skillfully. This gap between prediction and control motivates us to study how the priors acquired by video generation models can be used effectively by an action policy. We present ZERITH V2.5, a video-action model (VAM) in which the action policy reads intermediate features of a video generation model, and use it to study, under fixed demonstrations and a fixed total number of updates, what kind of predictive training improves subsequent action learning. We find that the way video predictive training is supervised affects subsequent action learning. In our experiments, adapting at many noise levels yields a large gain, while adapting only at the noise level used at deployment yields none. Weight-transfer experiments further show that the gain stays with the adapted weights when they remain in the video generation model and disappears when they are used only to initialize the action policy. Initial loss and gradient measurements at the start of joint training indicate that target-domain predictive training gives the video model a better starting state, making subsequent joint learning of prediction and action easier. Under matched data and training budgets, predictive adaptation improves control. We evaluate the complete policy on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, Meta-World MT50, and real-robot tasks.
