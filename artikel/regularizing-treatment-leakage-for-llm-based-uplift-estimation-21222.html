Regularizing Treatment Leakage for LLM-based Uplift Estimation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (176 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20794v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) provide semantic features for uplift estimation, but treatment-revealing text can induce prediction shortcuts that do not transfer to deployment. We propose \emph{LLM-based Uplift Leakage Regularization} (\textsc{LULR}) to address this risk. With the LLM encoder frozen, \textsc{LULR} penalizes dependence between the causal head's trainable shared representation and treatment while retaining separate outcome heads. The penalty uses a variational approximation to the contrastive log-ratio upper bound (CLUB) of mutual information, targeting treatment dependence rather than generic parameter shrinkage. Controlled proxy experiments provide exploratory evidence of improved ranking scores under injected treatment information relative to no regularization and the tested weight-decay setting. Structured-only benchmarks show modest, dataset-dependent effects. On an industrial pricing randomized controlled trial, mean offline $\text{lift@}0.3$ is $0.258$ for \textsc{LULR}, compared with $0.106$ for the same-backbone baseline. A separate one-week online A/B test of the complete deployed pricing policy records $5.45\%$ higher sales volume and $1.91\%$ higher gross merchandise value than the control arm. These short-term policy-level gains do not isolate the regularizer's effect.
