Tech Report: Transient State Preservation of a Dissipative Qubit via Lyapunov Control

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (256 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.21778v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Preserving quantum states in the presence of environmental decoherence remains a fundamental challenge for quantum information processing. This work investigates whether Hamiltonian Lyapunov feedback can transiently preserve the state of an open qubit by reshaping its trajectory under Markovian dissipation. A state-error Lyapunov function is adopted to derive an analytical feedback law for Lindblad dynamics, ensuring a nonincreasing Lyapunov function whenever the control sensitivities are nonzero. The analysis identifies the geometric conditions under which the feedback becomes ineffective, showing that control authority vanishes when the current and target Bloch vectors are collinear. Numerical simulations are performed for amplitude damping and pure dephasing using selected target states. Performance is evaluated through fidelity, accumulated infidelity, dwell time above a fidelity threshold, coherence, and control effort. The results demonstrate that the proposed feedback cannot eliminate irreversible dissipation or achieve universal asymptotic stabilization, but it can provide transient benefits for selected target-channel combinations. In particular, under amplitude damping, the controller significantly reduces accumulated infidelity and substantially extends the interval during which the system remains above a prescribed fidelity threshold. In the pure dephasing cases examined, improvements in fidelity are generally limited, although transient coherence preservation is observed for the oblique target considered, revealing a trade-off between coherence and state fidelity. These findings indicate that Lyapunov-based Hamiltonian feedback is best suited as a transient preservation mechanism and motivate its application to gate operations and preservation of prepared states during finite time intervals before their use or readout.
