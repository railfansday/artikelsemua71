STAR++: A Physical-Aware Benchmark and Framework for Astronomical Super-Resolution across Multiple Filters

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (243 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24023v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Astronomical super-resolution (ASR) aims to reconstruct high-resolution star fields from low-resolution telescope observations across multiple filters for astrophysical analysis. However, developing a physics-aware ASR framework faces two challenges: 1) Data view: no large-scale benchmark supports field-level ASR, especially on unseen filters. 2) Method view: Existing ASR methods often fail to preserve stellar flux across spatial resolutions. Moreover, ASR requires reconstructing high-frequency components, including accurate star locations and profiles, making it more ill-posed than natural-image SR. To tackle these problems, we introduce STAR++, a large-scale benchmark comprising approximately 1.7 million flux-consistent image pairs across 36 filters, derived from Hubble Space Telescope (HST) observations. STAR++ uses a flux-preserving data generation pipeline to ensure photometric consistency across resolutions and provides the Flux Error (FE) metric to evaluate ASR models from a physical perspective. To address method-level challenges, we propose a Flux-Invariant Super-Resolution Plus (FISR++) model that generalizes to unseen filters while accurately reconstructing flux-consistent high-resolution images. Beyond the flux-preserving modules, FISR++ introduces two novel components. Specifically, a Filter-Aware Adapter (FAA) incorporates filter metadata to adapt image representations to varying spectral and optical conditions. In addition, a Spatial Ambiguity Disentangle Loss (SADL) models spatial ambiguity as pixel-shift uncertainty, mitigating the smoothing of learned conditional mean reconstructions caused by this ambiguity. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FISR++ consistently outperforms existing ASR methods in reconstruction quality and flux accuracy, while generalizing to unseen filters, highlighting its effectiveness for photometrically faithful ASR.
