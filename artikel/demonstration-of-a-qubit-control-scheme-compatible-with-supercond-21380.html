Demonstration of a Qubit Control Scheme Compatible with Superconducting Digital Signals

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (218 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22166v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Superconducting quantum computing faces a critical scalability bottleneck arising from the escalating complexity of traditional analog control infrastructure. Single-Flux-Quantum(SFQ) systems operate at cryogenic temperatures and offer a potential route to addressing these challenges. Using microwave signals at qubit-dependent frequencies to trigger SFQ pulses requires many independent analog microwave sources, limiting the potential to reduce control wiring and increase integration density. A scalable SFQ control system therefore needs a scheme that can address qubits with different transition frequencies using a common clock. Here, we propose and experimentally demonstrate a control scheme for implementing both single- and two-qubit gates. The scheme uses guiding waveforms to rapidly generate bipolar high-speed pulse sequences on a common clock, providing a basis for future implementation in SFQ control systems. Numerically, this scheme achieves a simulated fidelity of 99.99% for 40ns $R(\varphi, \frac{\pi}{2})$ gates with arbitrary initial phases and 99.9% for 100ns controlled-Z(CZ) gates. Experimentally, by utilizing an arbitrary waveform generator(AWG) operated at a fixed sampling rate of 25GSa/s to deliver the bipolar high-speed pulse sequences, we successfully demonstrate 40ns single-qubit gates with an average gate fidelity of 99.94% estimated using cross-entropy benchmarking(XEB) and 120ns CZ gates with 99.63% fidelity. These results demonstrate the feasibility of common-clock encoded pulse control for both single- and two-qubit gates.
