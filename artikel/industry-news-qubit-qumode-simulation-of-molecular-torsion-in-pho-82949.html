Industry News: Qubit-qumode simulation of molecular torsion in photoisomerization

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (243 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.23408v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Photochemical reactions driven by large nuclear motion, such as the photoisomerization of retinal, depend strongly on both the global anharmonic forces of the potential and the periodic topology of molecular torsion. In this work, we develop a hybrid discrete (qubit) and continuous variable (qumode) quantum simulation framework for photochemical reactions that maps electronic states to qubits, vibrations to qumodes, and the periodic torsion to a compact qumode encoding. Applying this approach to the two-mode Hahn-Stock and three-mode PSB3 models of retinal photoisomerization, we compare i) local vibronic approximations, ii) the full trigonometric potential on the noncompact line, and iii) the same potential on the compact circle, which matches the molecular torsion topology. We numerically show that the topological description can significantly alter relevant observables. We realize the compact torsion via both a wrapped Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) encoding and a two-qumode circle encoding and introduce two methods that reduce the hardware resources of both encodings. Spreading an encoded coordinate over several qumodes lowers the average occupation number of each mode, and adding counterterms to the Hamiltonian reduces the required squeezing amount. We expect that these methods make percent-level simulations of the considered molecules promising targets for hybrid qubit-qumode hardware. For more complex molecules, each additional nuclear coordinate is represented directly as a single qumode or an encoded register using the same native operations, so the framework extends naturally to larger systems.
