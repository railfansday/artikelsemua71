Breaking: Hierarchical Control of Antiphase Synchronization in Biomorphic Spiking Neural Architectures for Locomotor Control

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (192 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.22639v2 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Central pattern generators (CPGs) provide a biologically inspired foundation for adaptive locomotor control, yet systematically characterizing their dynamic regimes in spiking neural networks remains challenging. This study introduces a framework for identifying and stabilizing antiphase synchronization in a biomorphic spiking CPG model embedded in a closed neuromuscular loop. A composite synchronization metric is developed, integrating phase coherence, amplitude balance, and temporal stability. Its performance is evaluated across systematically varied stimulation waveforms and synaptic configurations. Statistical analysis reveals that stimulation type dominates regime determination, with tonic drive yielding the most robust antiphase synchronization. Periodic inputs induce nonlinear saturation and context-dependent network routing. Although synaptic weights alone show limited discriminative power, they significantly modulate robustness within a given stimulation context, with reciprocal Ia interneuron connections emerging as key stabilizers. The proposed composite metric achieves robust antiphase detection, outperforming traditional phase-only measures while preserving biophysical interpretability. These findings establish a statistically validated, hierarchical modeling approach in which external drive defines the operating regime and targeted synaptic configurations fine-tune dynamic stability, providing actionable guidelines for closed-loop neuromechanical controllers and adaptive neuroprosthetic interfaces.
