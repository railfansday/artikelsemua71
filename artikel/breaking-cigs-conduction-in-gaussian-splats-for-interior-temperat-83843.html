Breaking: CiGS: Conduction in Gaussian Splats for Interior Temperature Field from Thermal Images

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (201 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.20283v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We present a method for inferring the interior temperature distribution of a solid object from registered multi-view visible-spectrum and thermal images, even when the thermal views cover only part of its surface. We formulate steady-state heat conduction as a Laplace boundary value problem and solve it with a direct boundary integral equation discretized over a point cloud: a Gaussian reconstruction of the visible-spectrum images supplies the boundary geometry, and the thermal images supply the boundary data. We do not build surface or volume meshes at any stage in our method. Instead, we sample collocation points from rendered depth without the pixels at depth discontinuities, and use a density subtraction that keeps the interior evaluation accurate up to the surface. The whole chain is differentiable, and we use it to recover the pose and depth of heating pipes, insulation defects, and the shape of cracks from thermal images. We validate our method through experiments on boundary value problems with Dirichlet-only and mixed Dirichlet and Robin boundaries, using synthetic and real captured data on scenes with challenging topology. These experiments show that our method matches analytical solutions and has better accuracy than FEM solvers.
