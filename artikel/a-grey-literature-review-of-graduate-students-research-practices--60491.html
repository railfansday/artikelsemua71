A Grey Literature Review of Graduate Students' Research Practices and Skills in the LLM Era

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (249 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2607.29519v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Context: Graduate students in computing now use Large Language Models (LLMs) throughout their research, from literature reviews and coding to data analysis and writing. Yet there is little shared understanding of which skills competent use of these tools requires, so graduate programs struggle to teach, mentor, or assess it, and unverified outputs can propagate into the scientific record. Objective: We investigate which LLM-based practices graduate students employ in their research and which skills they need to benefit from these practices. Method: We conducted a grey literature review of Reddit discussions. We retrieved 1,383 posts from five research-oriented subreddits, screened them against explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria, and analyzed the 54 included posts with thematic analysis, in which two authors independently coded each excerpt. Results: We identified seven categories of practices and nine categories of skills. Commenters locate the problem in accepting generated output without inspection rather than in using LLMs, and warn that delegating the effortful parts of a task, such as debugging, reading, or validating sources, delivers the output without building the skill the task was meant to develop. Traditional skills, such as programming and critical thinking, are what allow students to verify what an LLM produces. Conclusions: Our categories give graduate programs a concrete basis to teach students to work with LLMs without eroding the skills that research training should build, and they add research-specific skills, such as data sharing and scientific reproducibility, to existing AI competency frameworks.
