Spectral World Renderer: Unified 3D Hyperspectral Unmixing

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (191 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.19520v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Hyperspectral images (HSI) preserve material evidence lost in RGB, yet 3D material identification from multi-view HSI faces two obstacles. Spectral Gaussian splatting reconstructs spectra accurately but leaves unmixing to per-pixel post-processing, while multi-view hyperspectral capture is costly and real datasets lack pixel-level material labels and sub-pixel proportions. We propose Spectral World Renderer (SWR), a material-aware explicit representation that unifies 3D spectral reconstruction and unmixing: it fits a Gaussian scaffold from RGB and decodes ray-Gaussian intersections into abundances over a shared endmember dictionary. Additionally, appearance guidance prevents spectrally similar materials from merging when intensity absorbs their brightness differences. Moreover, SWR requires no material-label supervision during training. To address the data gap, we further construct the SWR Benchmark (SWR-Bench): a coding-agent pipeline generates eight indoor and outdoor worlds from measured reflectance, aligning HSI with both annotations. SWR achieves the highest mIoU in all eight worlds and the highest mean PSNR on each real dataset. On an 11-class urban world, it trains at least 3.67x faster than unmixing baselines; rendering averages 34.9 joint spectral and label views/s over four worlds on a single RTX 4090.
