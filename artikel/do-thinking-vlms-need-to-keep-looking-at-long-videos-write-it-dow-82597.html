Do Thinking VLMs Need to Keep Looking at Long Videos? Write It Down, Then Look Away

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (212 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24259v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Vision-language models (VLMs) reason about long videos over hundreds to thousands of tokens. Every decoding step re-reads the key-value (KV) cache of the entire video. Must the model keep looking while it reasons? In LookAway, the model first writes timestamped notes on what the question needs and then puts the video away. Once the notes close, the inference engine hides the video's KV cache for the rest of the response. Across both hybrid and pure-attention VLMs, dependence on the video is concentrated early in the response. On Qwen3.6-27B, writing notes preserves accuracy better than free reasoning at the same withdrawal step. Replacing them with another video's notes harms accuracy, whereas keeping the video visible alongside the model's own notes adds no significant benefit. Without additional training, LookAway nearly matches full-video thinking accuracy at 0.12-0.13 times its modeled decode cost. To shorten this initial look, we fine-tune the model on its own correct LookAway responses. Reinforcement learning then rewards correct answers while penalizing KV reads. The trained model puts the video away after a median of 66-68 tokens. Across three held-out benchmarks, it writes more tokens than an identically trained full-video model yet reads only 27-35% as many KV positions at matched accuracy.
