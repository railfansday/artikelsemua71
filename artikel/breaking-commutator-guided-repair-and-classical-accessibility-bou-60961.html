Breaking: Commutator-guided repair and classical-accessibility boundaries for shallow quantum circuits

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (151 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.22087v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Trainability does not guarantee low-energy arrival: a shallow quantum circuit can be optimizable yet fail, under fixed depth and evaluation budgets, to reach Hamiltonian-selected states. We introduce a commutator-guided repair for this operational failure mode. Low-order host--Hamiltonian commutators and fixed same-support companion axes define a restricted pool of local Pauli rotations, from which a compact completion layer is selected before full reoptimization. Across 189 paired six-qubit trials, unrepaired circuits achieve 0/189 strict successes, a non-deployable per-trial control envelope achieves 109/189, and two predeclared selectors achieve 133/189 and 127/189. The repair loses to matched controls for transverse-field Ising, is weak for pure-ZZ MaxCut, and remains size-limited for weighted MIS. Under fixed-depth geometric locality, repaired losses and gradients are classically computable, while corresponding ground-state targets admit low-bond-dimension tensor-network approximations through the tested sizes. The method reveals a trainability--arrival--classical-accessibility tradeoff rather than quantum advantage.
